# Reproduction in flowering plants

## Simple idea
A flower is a plant's way of making seeds. Pollen from the anther lands on a stigma, which is pollination. The pollen grows a tube down to the ovary and joins an ovule, which is fertilisation. The ovule becomes a seed, and the ovary becomes the fruit around it.

## Key formulas
- Pollination → pollen tube grows → fertilisation → seed
- Ovule → seed; ovary → fruit
- Male parts: stamen (anther + filament); female part: carpel (stigma + style + ovary)

## Picture it
Draw `flower`. Label `flower.anther` "pollen made here" and `flower.stigma` "pollen lands here". Arrow from `flower.anther` to `flower.stigma` labelled "pollination", and label `flower.ovary` "becomes the fruit".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up pollination and fertilisation: pollination moves pollen, fertilisation joins the cells.
- Thinking the stamen is female: stamens are male, the carpel is female.
- Thinking the seed becomes the fruit: the ovary becomes the fruit, the ovule the seed.

## Check questions
1. Which part of a flower makes pollen?
   Answer: The anther.
2. Name two ways pollen can be carried.
   Answer: Wind and insects.
3. Why do many flowers have bright petals and nectar?
   Answer: To attract insects that carry pollen.

## Flashcards
Q: What is pollination?
A: Pollen moving from an anther to a stigma.
Q: What does the ovule become after fertilisation?
A: A seed.
Q: What does the ovary become?
A: The fruit.
Q: What is cross-pollination?
A: Pollen moving to a flower on another plant.
Q: Why is a fruit useful to a plant?
A: It helps spread the seeds.
