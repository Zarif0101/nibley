# Radians and arc length

## Simple idea
A radian measures an angle by how far you walk round a circle's edge. Walk a distance equal to the radius and you have turned 1 radian. Think of cutting a pizza crust piece as long as the pizza's radius. A full turn is 2π radians, which is 360°.

## Key formulas
- θ = s / r. θ: angle (radians, no unit needed), s: arc length (same length unit as r), r: radius (cm, m, ...)
- s = r·θ. Arc length, solved for s
- 2π rad = 360°, so π rad = 180°. π ≈ 3.14
- radians = degrees × π / 180
- A = ½·r²·θ. A: area of the slice (sector) (cm², m²), r: radius, θ: angle (radians)

## Picture it
Draw `circle` and put the angle at `circle.center`. Label `circle.radius-mid` "radius r". Mark an arc on the edge near `circle.edge-right`, labelled "arc s = r·θ".
Draw `angle` beside it, with "θ in radians" at `angle.arc-mid`.

## Common mistakes
- Using degrees in s = r·θ: change to radians first.
- Thinking π is 180: π radians equals 180°, the number itself is 3.14.
- Leaving a calculator in degree mode for radian questions: check the mode.

## Check questions
1. How many radians are in a half turn?
   Answer: π radians.
2. A circle has radius 5 cm. What is the arc length for an angle of 2 radians?
   Answer: s = 5 × 2 = 10 cm.
3. Why does the radian have no unit?
   Answer: It is arc length divided by radius, so the lengths cancel.
