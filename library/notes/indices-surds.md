# Indices and surds

## Simple idea
An index is the little raised number that says how many times to multiply, like 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. A surd is a root that does not come out neat, like √2. We leave it as √2 because that is exact. A decimal would be rounded.

## Key formulas
- aᵐ · aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ and aᵐ / aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ. a: the base, m and n: the indices
- (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ. a⁰ = 1. a⁻ⁿ = 1 / aⁿ
- a^(1/n) = the n-th root of a. So a^(1/2) = √a
- √(ab) = √a · √b. √2 ≈ 1.414

Draw `right-triangle` with both short sides 1. Label `right-triangle.base-mid`: "1" and `right-triangle.side-mid`: "1".
Label `right-triangle.hypotenuse-mid`: "√2 (exact)".

## Common mistakes
- Multiplying the bases: 2³ · 2⁴ is 2⁷, not 4⁷. Add the indices.
- Thinking a negative index makes a negative number: 2⁻³ = 1/8.
- Adding unlike surds: √2 + √3 is not √5.

## Check questions
1. Simplify 2³ · 2⁴.
   Answer: 2⁷, which is 128.
2. What is 8^(2/3)?
   Answer: 4 (the cube root of 8 is 2, then square it).
3. Why is a⁰ equal to 1?
   Answer: aⁿ / aⁿ = 1, and by the rule it also equals a⁰.

## Flashcards
Q: What is aᵐ · aⁿ?
A: aᵐ⁺ⁿ.
Q: What is a⁻¹?
A: 1 / a.
Q: What is a^(1/2)?
A: √a, the square root of a.
Q: Write √50 in simplest form.
A: 5√2.
Q: What is a surd?
A: A root that is not a whole number or fraction, like √2.

## Root check
- powers-roots: What is 2⁵? Answer: 32.
- fractions-decimals: What is 1/2 + 1/4? Answer: 3/4.
