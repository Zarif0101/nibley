# Electric current

## Simple idea
Think of a river. Current is how much water passes a spot each second. In a wire it is how much charge passes a point each second. Batteries push the charge round a loop. We draw current going from the positive end to the negative end (conventional current), though electrons really move the other way.

## Key formulas
- I = Q / t. I: current (amperes, A), Q: charge passing (coulombs, C), t: time (seconds, s)
- N = Q / e. N: number of electrons, Q: charge (C), e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

Draw `simple-circuit`. Arrow along `simple-circuit.left-side`, labelled "current I", going from the battery's + end round to its − end.
Label `simple-circuit.battery`: "pushes charge". Label `simple-circuit.resistor`: "same current here".

## Common mistakes
- Saying current gets used up: the same current flows all round a single loop.
- Mixing up electron direction and current direction: arrows show + to −, electrons go opposite.
- Forgetting to use seconds: change minutes to seconds first.

## Check questions
1. 6 C of charge passes a point in 2 s. What is the current?
   Answer: I = 6 / 2 = 3 A.
2. A 2 A current flows for 5 s. How much charge moves?
   Answer: Q = 2 × 5 = 10 C.
3. Why does current go the opposite way to the electrons?
   Answer: Electrons are negative, and the arrow shows the way positive charge would flow.

## Flashcards
Q: What is electric current?
A: The charge passing a point each second.
Q: What is the unit of current?
A: The ampere (A), equal to one coulomb per second.
Q: Which way does conventional current flow?
A: From the positive terminal to the negative terminal.
Q: How many electrons make 1 C?
A: About 6.25 × 10¹⁸, since 1 / 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹.
Q: Is current the same all round a single loop?
A: Yes.

## Root check
- coulombs-law: Do like charges attract or repel? Answer: Repel.
