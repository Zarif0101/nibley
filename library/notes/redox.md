# Redox and oxidation numbers

## Simple idea
Iron rusting is electrons moving. One thing hands electrons over and another takes them. Giving electrons away is oxidation. Taking them is reduction. They always happen together, so one cannot happen without the other. Oxidation numbers help you track the electrons.

## Key formulas
- OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)
- Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻. Oxidation, e⁻: an electron
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. Reduction
- Oxidation numbers: uncombined element 0; O is usually −2; H is usually +1; the total equals the overall charge

## Picture it
Draw `atom`. Arrow from `atom.electron` leaving the atom, labelled "oxidation: loses e⁻". A second arrow towards `charge-negative.center`, labelled "e⁻ moves to the other atom".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up the two words: use OIL RIG.
- Thinking oxidation number goes down when electrons are lost: losing electrons makes it go up.
- Forgetting the total: the numbers in a compound add up to its charge (zero if neutral).

## Check questions
1. In Mg + Cu²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Cu, which species is oxidised?
   Answer: Magnesium, it loses 2 electrons.
2. What is the oxidation number of Mn in MnO₄⁻?
   Answer: +7, because 4 × (−2) = −8 and the total is −1.
3. Why can oxidation never happen alone?
   Answer: The electrons lost must go somewhere, to something being reduced.

## Flashcards
Q: What does OIL RIG stand for?
A: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain of electrons.
Q: What is the oxidation number of an uncombined element?
A: Zero.
Q: What is the usual oxidation number of oxygen?
A: −2.
Q: When an atom loses electrons, does its oxidation number rise or fall?
A: It rises.
Q: What is a reducing agent?
A: A substance that gives electrons away, so it gets oxidised.
