# Equilibrium constant Kc

## Simple idea
Kc is a score that says where an equilibrium ends up. Put the product amounts on top and the reactant amounts on the bottom. A big score means mostly products. A tiny score means mostly reactants. Only temperature changes it.

## Key formulas
- For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD: Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ). [ ]: concentration at equilibrium (mol/dm³); a, b, c, d: balancing numbers
- N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ gives Kc = [NO₂]² / [N₂O₄], in mol/dm³

## Picture it
Draw `equilibrium-arrows`. Write "products on top" above `equilibrium-arrows.forward` and "reactants on bottom" below `equilibrium-arrows.backward`. Write "Kc = products / reactants" at `equilibrium-arrows.center`.

## Common mistakes
- Putting reactants on top: products always go on top.
- Forgetting powers: the balancing number becomes the power.
- Thinking concentration or a catalyst changes Kc: only temperature does.

## Check questions
1. For N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, [NO₂] = 0.2 and [N₂O₄] = 0.1 mol/dm³. What is Kc?
   Answer: 0.04 ÷ 0.1 = 0.4 mol/dm³.
2. Kc is 1000. Mostly reactants or mostly products?
   Answer: Mostly products.
3. Why is [NO₂] squared in the Kc expression?
   Answer: The balanced equation has 2NO₂, and the 2 becomes the power.

## Flashcards
Q: Which concentrations go on top in Kc?
A: The products.
Q: What does a very large Kc mean?
A: The equilibrium lies far towards products.
Q: What does a very small Kc mean?
A: The equilibrium lies far towards reactants.
Q: What is the only thing that changes Kc?
A: Temperature.
Q: What do square brackets mean in Kc?
A: Concentration in mol/dm³ at equilibrium.

## Root check
- chemical-equilibrium: In a reversible reaction at equilibrium, how do the forward and back rates compare? Answer: They are equal.
