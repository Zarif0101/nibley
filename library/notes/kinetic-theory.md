# Kinetic theory of gases

## Simple idea
A gas is a crowd of tiny particles flying about and bouncing off walls. Each bounce gives the wall a tiny push, and millions of pushes make pressure. Heat the gas and the particles move faster, so they hit harder and more often. Temperature tracks their average kinetic energy.

## Key formulas
- Average KE of one particle = (3/2)·k·T. k: Boltzmann constant, 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K, T: temperature (kelvin, K), KE: kinetic energy (joules, J)
- Average KE is proportional to T in kelvin

## Common mistakes
- Thinking all particles move at the same speed: speeds vary, we use the average.
- Thinking pressure comes from particles pushing each other: it comes from hitting walls.
- Using °C for the KE link: use kelvin.

## Check questions
1. What causes gas pressure?
   Answer: Particles hitting the walls.
2. Temperature of a gas doubles in kelvin. What happens to average KE?
   Answer: It doubles.
3. Why does heating a sealed gas raise its pressure?
   Answer: Particles move faster, so they hit walls harder and more often.

## Flashcards
Q: What does temperature measure in kinetic theory?
A: Average kinetic energy of the particles.
Q: Why does a gas fill its whole container?
A: Its particles move freely in all directions.
Q: Formula for average KE of a gas particle?
A: (3/2)kT.
Q: Value of k?
A: 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K.
Q: Smaller container, same gas. Why more pressure?
A: Particles hit the walls more often.

## Root check
- ideal-gas-law: A gas is squeezed to half its volume at constant temperature. What happens to pressure? Answer: It doubles.
- kinetic-energy: What is the kinetic energy of a 2 kg object moving at 3 m/s? Answer: ½ × 2 × 3² = 9 J.
