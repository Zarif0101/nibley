# Continuity

## Simple idea
A function is continuous if you can draw its graph without lifting the pen. A road with no gaps is continuous. A road with a missing bridge piece, or a hole, is not. Math checks three things at a point: the function has a value there, the graph heads to one value from both sides, and those two match.

## Key formulas
- Continuous at x = a when lim(x→a) f(x) = f(a). f(a): the function's value at a, lim: the value the graph approaches (no unit)
- Piecewise or split functions: the left limit and the right limit must both equal f(a)
- Polynomials are continuous everywhere

## Common mistakes
- Checking only that a limit exists: f(a) must exist and equal it too.
- Looking at one side only: check both sides of the point.
- Thinking 1/x is broken everywhere: it breaks only at x = 0, where it has no value.

## Check questions
1. What are the three conditions for continuity at a point?
   Answer: f(a) exists, the limit exists, and they are equal.
2. f(x) = x + 1 for x ≠ 2, but f(2) = 7. Is f continuous at 2?
   Answer: No, the limit is 3 but f(2) = 7.
3. Why is (x² − 1)/(x − 1) not continuous at x = 1?
   Answer: It is undefined there (0/0), so there is a hole.

## Flashcards
Q: Describe a continuous function in simple words.
A: You can draw its graph without lifting your pen.
Q: What is the condition for continuity at x = a?
A: The limit as x → a equals f(a).
Q: What is a removable discontinuity?
A: A single missing point, a hole in the graph.
Q: Are polynomials continuous?
A: Yes, everywhere.
Q: Where is 1/x not continuous?
A: At x = 0.

## Root check
- limits: What does lim(x→2) of (x + 3) equal? Answer: 5.
