# Power

## Simple idea
Power tells you how fast energy is being used or moved. Two people carry the same box up the same stairs. The one who does it in half the time has twice the power. A 60 watt bulb uses 60 joules every second.

## Key formulas
- P = W / t. P: power (watts, W), W: work or energy (joules, J), t: time (seconds, s)
- P = F·v. F: force (N), v: speed along the force (m/s)

## Picture it
Draw `pulley` with a rope on the right lifting `block`. Arrow from `pulley.rope-right-end` pointing down, labelled "pull F". Arrow above `block.top` pointing up, labelled "speed v".
Label at `pulley.axle`: "power = work ÷ time".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up energy and power: joules measure energy, watts measure how fast.
- Using minutes: change time to seconds first.
- Thinking more power means more total energy: it can just mean faster.

## Check questions
1. What is 1 watt in joules and seconds?
   Answer: 1 joule per second.
2. A motor does 500 J of work in 10 s. What is its power?
   Answer: 500 ÷ 10 = 50 W.
3. Why does a 2000 W kettle boil water faster than a 1000 W one?
   Answer: It transfers twice as many joules each second.

## Flashcards
Q: What is power?
A: The rate of doing work or transferring energy.
Q: What is the formula for power?
A: P = W / t.
Q: What is the unit of power?
A: The watt (W), equal to 1 J/s.
Q: How much work does a 200 W motor do in 30 s?
A: 200 × 30 = 6000 J.
Q: What is P in terms of force and speed?
A: P = F·v.

## Root check
- work-done: A 10 N force moves a box 3 m in its own direction. How much work? Answer: 30 J.
