# Pendulums and springs

## Simple idea
A playground swing takes the same time per swing whether you swing high or low, as long as the swing is small. A longer swing takes longer. A bouncing spring is different: a heavier weight bounces slower, a stiffer spring bounces faster. Mass of the pendulum bob does not matter.

## Key formulas
- T = 2π√(L/g) for a pendulum. T: period (seconds, s), L: string length (m), g: 9.8 m/s²
- T = 2π√(m/k) for a mass on a spring. m: mass (kg), k: spring stiffness (N/m)

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a heavier bob swings slower: a pendulum's T does not depend on mass.
- Using big angles: the formula works for small swings only.
- Forgetting the square root: double L makes T about 1.4 times longer.

## Check questions
1. What is the period of a 1.0 m pendulum? (g = 9.8)
   Answer: 2π√(1/9.8) ≈ 2.0 s.
2. L is made 4 times longer. What happens to T?
   Answer: T doubles.
3. Why does a stiffer spring bounce faster?
   Answer: It pulls back harder, so each bounce is quicker and T is smaller.

## Flashcards
Q: Pendulum period formula?
A: T = 2π√(L/g).
Q: Does bob mass change a pendulum's period?
A: No.
Q: Spring period formula?
A: T = 2π√(m/k).
Q: Heavier mass on a spring: period?
A: Longer.
Q: What is a small swing?
A: A small angle, around 10° or less, where the formulas work.
