# Cell structure

## Simple idea
A cell is like a tiny factory with little parts, called organelles, each with a job. Animal and plant cells both have a nucleus, but plant cells also have a wall, a big vacuole and chloroplasts. Bacteria have no nucleus: their DNA floats loose.

## Picture it
Draw `cell-animal` and `cell-plant` side by side. On the plant, label `cell-plant.cell-wall`: "wall, only in plants", `cell-plant.chloroplast`: "makes food from light", `cell-plant.vacuole`: "stores water". Draw `bacterium`; label `bacterium.dna`: "loose DNA, no nucleus".

## Common mistakes
- Saying plant cells have no mitochondria: they have them too, for energy.
- Mixing up the cell wall and the membrane: the wall is a tough outer layer, the membrane is the thin skin inside it.
- Thinking bacteria have a nucleus: their DNA is loose in the cell.

## Check questions
1. Which organelle holds the cell's DNA in an animal cell?
   Answer: The nucleus.
2. Name two parts a plant cell has that an animal cell does not.
   Answer: Any two of: cell wall, chloroplasts, large vacuole.
3. Why do muscle cells have lots of mitochondria?
   Answer: They need lots of energy, and mitochondria release it from food.

## Flashcards
Q: What do mitochondria do?
A: They release energy from food, in respiration.
Q: What do chloroplasts do?
A: They make food by photosynthesis, using light.
Q: What do ribosomes do?
A: They build proteins.
Q: What is the plant cell wall made of?
A: Cellulose, a tough fibre that supports the cell.
Q: What is a plasmid?
A: A small ring of extra DNA in bacteria.
