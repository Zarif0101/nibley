# Mass-energy, fission and fusion

## Simple idea
Mass is a very packed form of energy. When a nucleus reaction ends with slightly less mass than it started, the missing mass leaves as energy. Fission splits a heavy nucleus, like uranium, in a power station. Fusion joins light nuclei, like hydrogen in the Sun.

## Key formulas
- E = Δm·c². E: energy released (joules, J), Δm: mass lost (kilograms, kg), c: 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s

## Picture it
Draw `nucleus` on the left labelled "heavy nucleus"; a neutron arrow arrives at `nucleus.edge`. On the right draw two smaller `nucleus` pictures, labelled "two lighter nuclei" and "energy released".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking mass is lost for good: it turns into energy, total stays the same.
- Forgetting to square c: use c² = 9.0 × 10¹⁶ m²/s².
- Mixing up the two: fission splits big nuclei, fusion joins small ones.

## Check questions
1. A reaction loses 1.0 × 10⁻³⁰ kg of mass. How much energy is released?
   Answer: 9.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ J.
2. Which one joins nuclei, and which splits them?
   Answer: Fusion joins; fission splits.
3. Why does a tiny mass loss give so much energy?
   Answer: c² is huge, so a little mass equals a lot of energy.

## Flashcards
Q: What is Einstein's mass-energy equation?
A: E = m·c².
Q: What is nuclear fission?
A: A heavy nucleus splitting into smaller nuclei, releasing energy.
Q: What is nuclear fusion?
A: Light nuclei joining into a heavier one, releasing energy.
Q: What powers the Sun?
A: Fusion of hydrogen into helium.
Q: What is a mass defect?
A: The mass a nucleus is missing compared with its separate parts.
