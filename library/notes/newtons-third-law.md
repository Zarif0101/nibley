# Newton's third law

## Simple idea
Push a wall while standing on skates and you roll backwards. You pushed the wall, so the wall pushed you back just as hard. Forces always come in pairs. The two forces are equal in size, opposite in direction, and act on different objects.

## Key formulas
- F(A on B) = −F(B on A). F: force (newtons, N). Same size, opposite direction

## Picture it
Draw `block` on `ground`. Arrow from `block.bottom` pointing down labelled "block pushes ground". Arrow from `ground.mid` pointing up labelled "ground pushes block".
Label above `block.top`: "equal and opposite pair".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the pair cancels: the two forces act on different objects, so they cannot cancel.
- Thinking the bigger object pushes harder: both forces are equal in size.
- Mixing up with balanced forces: a third-law pair is on two objects, balanced forces are on one.

## Check questions
1. You push a wall with 30 N. How hard does the wall push you?
   Answer: 30 N, in the opposite direction.
2. A truck hits a small car. Which feels the bigger force?
   Answer: Neither, both feel the same size of force.
3. Why does a rocket move forward when it pushes gas backwards?
   Answer: The gas pushes the rocket forward with an equal force.

## Flashcards
Q: State Newton's third law.
A: Every force has an equal and opposite force on the other object.
Q: Do third-law pairs act on the same object?
A: No, on two different objects.
Q: Are the pair forces equal in size?
A: Yes, always.
Q: Why does swimming work?
A: You push water back, and the water pushes you forward.
Q: Why don't pair forces cancel?
A: They act on different objects.

## Root check
- newtons-first-law: A book rests on a table. Is the net force on it zero or not? Answer: Zero, the forces balance.
