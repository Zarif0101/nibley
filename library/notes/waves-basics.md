# Wave basics

## Simple idea
Flick one end of a rope and a bump runs along it, but the rope itself stays put. A wave carries energy from place to place without carrying material. Every part of the rope just moves up and down like a small simple harmonic motion. The material sets the speed; v = f·λ links speed, frequency and wavelength.

## Key formulas
- v = f·λ. v: wave speed (m/s), f: frequency (hertz, Hz), λ: wavelength (m)
- T = 1 / f. T: period, time for one wave (seconds, s)

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the material travels: only energy travels.
- Mixing amplitude and wavelength: amplitude is height, wavelength is length of one wave.
- Forgetting units: wavelength in metres, frequency in Hz.

## Check questions
1. What does a wave carry from place to place?
   Answer: Energy.
2. A wave has f = 50 Hz and λ = 0.2 m. What is its speed?
   Answer: 50 × 0.2 = 10 m/s.
3. Why does a floating cork only bob up and down as waves pass?
   Answer: The wave moves energy forward, but the water just moves up and down.

## Flashcards
Q: What is wavelength?
A: The distance between two crests in a row.
Q: What is frequency?
A: The number of waves passing each second.
Q: Wave speed formula?
A: v = f·λ.
Q: What is amplitude?
A: The biggest height of the wave from the rest line.
Q: Unit of frequency?
A: The hertz (Hz).
