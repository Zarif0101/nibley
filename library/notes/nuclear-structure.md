# The nucleus

## Simple idea
An atom is mostly empty space. In the very centre sits a tiny nucleus of protons (positive) and neutrons (no charge). It holds almost all the atom's mass. Protons repel each other, so something stronger, the strong nuclear force, holds the nucleus together at close range.

## Key formulas
- A = Z + N. A: mass number, protons plus neutrons; Z: atomic number, number of protons; N: number of neutrons (all counts, no unit)

## Picture it

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the nucleus has electrons in it: electrons orbit outside.
- Mixing up A and Z: Z counts protons only, A counts protons plus neutrons.
- Thinking isotopes are different elements: they have the same Z but different N.

## Check questions
1. Carbon-12 has Z = 6. How many neutrons does it have?
   Answer: 6.
2. What are isotopes?
   Answer: Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
3. Why do protons not fly apart, even though they repel?
   Answer: The strong nuclear force pulls nucleons together, and is stronger than the repulsion up close.

## Flashcards
Q: What is in a nucleus?
A: Protons and neutrons, together called nucleons.
Q: What does Z tell you?
A: The number of protons.
Q: What is the charge of a neutron?
A: Zero.
Q: Where is nearly all of an atom's mass?
A: In the nucleus.
Q: What force works between nucleons at close range?
A: The strong nuclear force.
