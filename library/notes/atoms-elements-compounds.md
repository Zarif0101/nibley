# Atoms, elements and compounds

## Simple idea
Everything is built from tiny atoms. An element has only one kind of atom, like oxygen gas. A compound has different atoms joined together in a fixed recipe, like water. Think of LEGO: one colour of brick is an element, a fixed model built from two colours is a compound.

## Key formulas
- H₂O: the small 2 means 2 hydrogen (H) atoms joined to 1 oxygen (O) atom
- O₂: 2 oxygen atoms joined, one element
- CO₂: 1 carbon (C) atom and 2 oxygen atoms, one compound
- NaCl: 1 sodium (Na) atom for each 1 chlorine (Cl) atom

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a compound is just a mixture: its atoms are joined in a fixed recipe.
- Thinking O₂ is a compound: it has one kind of atom, so it is an element.
- Reading the small number as belonging to the whole formula: it only counts the atom before it.

## Check questions
1. Is oxygen gas, O₂, an element or a compound?
   Answer: An element, because it has only one kind of atom.
2. How many atoms of each kind are in one CO₂ molecule?
   Answer: 1 carbon atom and 2 oxygen atoms.
3. Why is water a compound and not a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen?
   Answer: Its atoms are joined together in a fixed recipe, 2 hydrogen to 1 oxygen.

## Flashcards
Q: What is an atom?
A: The tiny building block that everything is made of.
Q: What is an element?
A: A substance made of only one kind of atom.
Q: What is a compound?
A: A substance with different atoms joined in a fixed recipe.
Q: What does the 2 in H₂O tell you?
A: There are 2 hydrogen atoms in each molecule.
Q: What is the symbol for sodium?
A: Na.
