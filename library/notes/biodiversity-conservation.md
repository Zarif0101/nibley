# Biodiversity and conservation

## Simple idea
Biodiversity is the variety of life in a place: many species, plus the variety of genes inside each one. A rainforest has far more than a farm field. Losing species weakens an ecosystem, like pulling bricks from a wall. We protect biodiversity by saving habitats, setting up reserves and breeding endangered species.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking biodiversity means only the number of animals: it counts species and genetic variety.
- Thinking extinction is only natural: human activity speeds it up.
- Thinking protecting one animal is enough: its habitat and food must be kept too.

## Check questions
1. What is biodiversity?
   Answer: The variety of living things in an area.
2. Name one threat to biodiversity.
   Answer: Habitat loss, pollution, over-hunting, invasive species or climate change.
3. Why is saving a habitat better than saving a single animal?
   Answer: The habitat supports all the species that depend on each other.

## Flashcards
Q: What does extinct mean?
A: No living members of the species remain.
Q: What is a habitat?
A: The place where an organism lives.
Q: What is an invasive species?
A: A non-native species that harms the local ecosystem.
Q: Name one way to conserve species.
A: Nature reserves, seed banks or captive breeding.
Q: Which is usually the biggest threat to wildlife?
A: Habitat loss.
