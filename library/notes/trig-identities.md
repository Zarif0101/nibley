# Trigonometric identities

## Simple idea
An identity is an equation that is true for every angle, like "double a number then halve it gives the number back". You use identities to swap a messy trig expression for a simpler one. The main one comes from Pythagoras: the dot on the unit circle has x² + y² = 1.

## Key formulas
- sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. θ: any angle (degrees or radians)
- tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
- sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ and cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ. The main identity, rearranged
- sin(90° − θ) = cos θ

## Common mistakes
- Writing sin²θ as sin(θ²): sin²θ means (sin θ) squared.
- Thinking sin²θ + cos²θ = 2 or 0: it is always 1.
- Forgetting the ± when taking a square root: check the quadrant for the sign.

## Check questions
1. What is sin²30° + cos²30°?
   Answer: 1.
2. sin θ = 0.6 and θ is acute. What is cos θ?
   Answer: cos²θ = 1 − 0.36 = 0.64, so cos θ = 0.8.
3. Why is sin²θ + cos²θ = 1?
   Answer: Pythagoras on the unit circle triangle: legs cos θ and sin θ, hypotenuse 1.

## Flashcards
Q: State the main trig identity.
A: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.
Q: What is an identity?
A: An equation true for every value of the angle.
Q: Write tan θ using sin and cos.
A: tan θ = sin θ / cos θ.
Q: Rearrange the main identity to get sin²θ.
A: sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ.
Q: What does sin²θ mean?
A: (sin θ) multiplied by itself.

## Root check
- trig-functions: What are sin 90° and cos 90°? Answer: sin 90° = 1, cos 90° = 0.
