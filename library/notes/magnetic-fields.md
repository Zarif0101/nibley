# Magnetic fields

## Simple idea
A fridge magnet pulls on metal without touching it. The magnet has a field around it, an invisible area where it pushes or pulls. Moving charge makes a field too, so a wire carrying current is a magnet while the current flows. Outside a magnet, field lines run from the north end to the south end.

## Key formulas
- B = μ₀·I / (2π·r). B: field strength near a long straight wire (tesla, T), I: current (amperes, A), r: distance from wire (metres, m), μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A
- Direction (right-hand grip rule): thumb along the current, curled fingers show the field

## Picture it
Draw `magnet`. Arrows along `magnet.field-top` pointing from `magnet.north-end` toward `magnet.south-end`, labelled "field lines".
Draw `wire-field` with `wire-field.current` labelled "current I", and label `wire-field.ring`: "circles round the wire".

## Common mistakes
- Saying field lines start and stop: they form closed loops (inside the magnet they go south to north).
- Thinking a wire's field points along the wire: it circles around it.
- Forgetting that distance matters: field gets weaker further away.

## Check questions
1. What happens when two north poles are brought together?
   Answer: They repel.
2. A wire carries 5 A. What is the field 0.1 m away?
   Answer: B = 4π × 10⁻⁷ × 5 / (2π × 0.1) = 1 × 10⁻⁵ T.
3. Why does a wire with current affect a compass?
   Answer: Moving charge makes a magnetic field.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the unit of magnetic field strength?
A: The tesla (T).
Q: Which poles attract?
A: Opposite poles: north and south.
Q: What shape is the field round a straight wire?
A: Circles around the wire.
Q: Outside a magnet, which way do field lines run?
A: From the north end to the south end.
Q: What makes a magnetic field besides magnets?
A: Moving charges, such as current in a wire.
