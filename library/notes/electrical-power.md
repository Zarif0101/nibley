# Electrical power and energy

## Simple idea
Power is how fast energy is used. A 100 W bulb turns 100 joules into light and heat every second, so it uses more than a 10 W bulb. More voltage and more current both mean more power. Your electricity bill counts energy, which is power multiplied by time.

## Key formulas
- P = V·I. P: power (watts, W), V: voltage (volts, V), I: current (amperes, A)
- P = I²·R and P = V² / R. R: resistance (ohms, Ω)
- E = P·t. E: energy (joules, J), t: time (seconds, s)
- 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J. kWh: kilowatt-hour, the unit on the bill

## Common mistakes
- Using power as energy: energy is power times time.
- Mixing up kW and W: 1 kW = 1000 W.
- Using minutes or hours in joules: change time to seconds.

## Check questions
1. A 12 V supply drives 2 A. What is the power?
   Answer: P = 12 × 2 = 24 W.
2. A 100 W bulb runs for 10 s. How much energy does it use?
   Answer: E = 100 × 10 = 1000 J.
3. Why does doubling the current through a fixed resistor give four times the power?
   Answer: P = I²R, and 2² = 4.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the unit of power?
A: The watt (W), equal to one joule per second.
Q: How do you find electrical power from V and I?
A: P = V·I.
Q: Power formula using current and resistance?
A: P = I²·R.
Q: How many joules in a kilowatt-hour?
A: 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
Q: How do you find energy from power?
A: Multiply by time: E = P·t.

- resistance-ohms-law: 12 V is across a 6 Ω resistor. What is the current? Answer: 2 A.
