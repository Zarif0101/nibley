# Carbon and nitrogen cycles

## Simple idea
Unlike energy, matter is used again and again. Plants take carbon dioxide from the air to build sugars. Animals eat the plants, then breathe carbon dioxide out. Decomposers rot dead things and return carbon to the air and nitrogen to the soil. Plants need nitrogen from the soil to make proteins.

## Key formulas
- Carbon: CO₂ in air → plants (photosynthesis) → animals (eating) → CO₂ (respiration, decay, burning)
- Nitrogen: N₂ in air → nitrogen-fixing bacteria → nitrates in soil → plants → animals → decomposers → soil
- Air is about 78% nitrogen, but plants cannot use N₂ directly

Draw `plant`. Arrow into `plant.leaf` labelled "CO₂ from air". Arrow from `plant.root` labelled "nitrates from soil". Draw `bacterium` below `plant.ground`, labelled "bacteria fix nitrogen".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking plants use nitrogen gas from the air: they need nitrates from the soil.
- Forgetting decomposers: without them, carbon and nitrogen stay locked in dead matter.
- Thinking only animals add CO₂: plants respire too, and burning fuels adds it.

## Check questions
1. Name two ways carbon dioxide returns to the air.
   Answer: Respiration, decay, or burning.
2. What do nitrogen-fixing bacteria do?
   Answer: They turn nitrogen gas into forms plants can use.
3. Why do plants need nitrogen?
   Answer: To build proteins.

## Flashcards
Q: What process removes CO₂ from the air?
A: Photosynthesis.
Q: What do decomposers do?
A: Break down dead matter and return nutrients to the soil.
Q: Which gas makes up most of the air?
A: Nitrogen.
Q: What do plants take up from the soil for nitrogen?
A: Nitrates.
Q: Where do root nodules hold bacteria?
A: In legume plants, such as peas and beans.
