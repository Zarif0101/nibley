# Composite and inverse functions

## Simple idea
Chain two machines and you get a composite function: the output of the first goes into the second. Think of putting on socks then shoes. An inverse function runs the machine backwards, like taking shoes off then socks, and gets you back to the start.

## Key formulas
- (f∘g)(x) = f(g(x)). Do g first, then f
- f⁻¹(f(x)) = x. f⁻¹: the inverse of f, it undoes f
- To find f⁻¹: write y = f(x), swap x and y, solve for y
- The graph of f⁻¹ is the graph of f mirrored in the line y = x

## Common mistakes
- Doing the functions in the wrong order: in f(g(x)), g acts first.
- Reading f⁻¹ as 1/f: it means the inverse, not a reciprocal.
- Forgetting to swap x and y before solving for the inverse.

## Check questions
1. f(x) = x + 1 and g(x) = 2x. What is f(g(3))?
   Answer: 7 (g(3) = 6, then f(6) = 7).
2. Find the inverse of f(x) = 2x + 3.
   Answer: f⁻¹(x) = (x − 3) / 2.
3. Why is the graph of an inverse a mirror image across y = x?
   Answer: The inverse swaps inputs and outputs, so every point (a, b) becomes (b, a).

## Flashcards
Q: What does f(g(x)) mean?
A: Put x into g first, then put that result into f.
Q: What does an inverse function do?
A: It undoes the original function.
Q: What is f⁻¹(f(x))?
A: x.
Q: Which line is the mirror between a function and its inverse?
A: The line y = x.
Q: Is f⁻¹(x) the same as 1/f(x)?
A: No, it means the inverse, not the reciprocal.

## Root check
- functions: If f(x) = x² − 1, what is f(3)? Answer: 8.
- rearranging-formulas: Make x the subject of y = 3x − 5. Answer: x = (y + 5) / 3.
