# Protein synthesis

## Simple idea
A gene is a recipe for one protein. The recipe book (DNA) stays safe in the nucleus, so the cell copies one recipe onto a note called mRNA. The note travels to a ribosome. The ribosome reads it three letters at a time and joins amino acids in order to build the protein.

## Key formulas
- DNA → mRNA → protein (transcription, then translation)
- Base pairs in RNA: A pairs with U, C pairs with G
- Codon: 3 mRNA bases = 1 amino acid

## Picture it
Draw `dna-helix`. Arrow from `dna-helix.base-pair` curving away, labelled "copy to mRNA". At `dna-helix.bottom` write "ribosome reads 3 bases at a time".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up the two steps: transcription copies DNA to mRNA, translation builds the protein.
- Using T in RNA: RNA has U instead of T.
- Thinking one base codes for one amino acid: it takes three bases (a codon).

## Check questions
1. What is the mRNA base opposite A on a DNA template strand?
   Answer: U.
2. A DNA template reads TAC. What is the mRNA?
   Answer: AUG.
3. Why does the cell make an mRNA copy?
   Answer: DNA stays safe in the nucleus while the copy goes to the ribosome.

## Flashcards
Q: What is transcription?
A: Copying a gene from DNA into mRNA.
Q: What is translation?
A: Building a protein by reading mRNA.
Q: Where does translation happen?
A: At a ribosome.
Q: What is a codon?
A: Three mRNA bases that code for one amino acid.
Q: What does tRNA carry?
A: An amino acid to the ribosome.
