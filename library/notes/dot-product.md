# Dot product

## Simple idea
The dot product multiplies two vectors and gives one plain number. The number tells you how much they point the same way. Two people pulling a sled in the same direction work together, so it is big. Pulling at right angles does nothing together, so it is zero.

## Key formulas
- a·b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃. a, b: vectors, a₁ to b₃: their parts (the result is a plain number)
- a·b = |a|·|b|·cos θ. |a|, |b|: lengths, θ: angle between them (degrees or radians)
- a·b = 0 when the vectors are at right angles

## Common mistakes
- Expecting a vector back: the dot product is just a number.
- Using sin instead of cos in the angle form: dot uses cos.
- Thinking zero means a zero vector: it can mean the vectors are at right angles.

## Check questions
1. Find (1, 2, 3)·(4, −5, 6).
   Answer: 4 − 10 + 18 = 12.
2. If |a| = 2, |b| = 3 and θ = 60°, what is a·b?
   Answer: 3.
3. Why is a·b = 0 at right angles?
   Answer: cos 90° = 0.

## Flashcards
Q: Is the dot product a vector or a number?
A: A number.
Q: Write the dot product with an angle.
A: a·b = |a||b| cos θ.
Q: What does a·b = 0 tell you?
A: The vectors are at right angles (or one is zero).
Q: Find (2, 1)·(3, 4).
A: 10.
Q: How do you find the angle between two vectors?
A: cos θ = a·b / (|a||b|).
