# Equilibrium and Le Chatelier's principle

## Simple idea
Picture a busy escalator pair, one going up and one going down, with equal numbers of people each way. The crowd on each floor stays the same even though everyone is moving. A reversible reaction in a closed container does this. If you disturb it, it shifts to cancel the change.

## Key formulas
- N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH = −92 kJ/mol. ⇌ means the reaction goes both ways
- At equilibrium: rate forward = rate backward (concentrations stay constant)

## Picture it
Draw `equilibrium-arrows`. Label `equilibrium-arrows.forward` "forward: reactants to products" and `equilibrium-arrows.backward` "back: products to reactants". Label `equilibrium-arrows.center` "rates equal".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the reaction stops: both reactions keep going at equal rates.
- Thinking concentrations are equal: they are constant, not the same.
- Thinking a catalyst shifts the position: it only gets there faster.

## Check questions
1. N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ is exothermic. Does raising the temperature shift it left or right?
   Answer: Left, towards the endothermic direction.
2. For the same reaction, which way does more pressure shift it?
   Answer: Right, to the side with fewer gas molecules (2 instead of 4).
3. Why does adding more reactant make more product?
   Answer: The system shifts to use up the extra reactant.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a dynamic equilibrium?
A: Forward and back rates are equal, so concentrations stay constant.
Q: What does Le Chatelier's principle say?
A: A system shifts to oppose any change made to it.
Q: Which symbol shows a reversible reaction?
A: ⇌
Q: Does a catalyst change the equilibrium position?
A: No, it only speeds up reaching it.
Q: Raise the temperature. Which direction does equilibrium shift?
A: The endothermic direction.
