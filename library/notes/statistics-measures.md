# Mean, spread and standard deviation

## Simple idea
The mean is the fair share: add everything up and divide by how many there are. Two classes can both average 60 on a test, but in one everyone scored near 60 and in the other scores were all over the place. Standard deviation says how far a typical value sits from the mean.

## Key formulas
- x̄ = Σx / n. x̄: the mean, Σx: the sum of all values, n: how many values
- σ = √( Σ(x − x̄)² / n ). σ: standard deviation (same unit as the data), x: each value
- I divide by n (the population version, as most school courses do here). Some courses divide by n − 1 for a sample.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the square root at the end: variance is σ², take √ to get σ.
- Adding raw differences from the mean: they cancel to 0, so square them first.
- Dividing by the wrong number: divide by n, the count of values.

## Check questions
1. What is the mean of 2, 4 and 9?
   Answer: 15 ÷ 3 = 5.
2. Find the standard deviation of 4 and 8.
   Answer: Mean 6, differences ±2, squares 4 and 4, average 4, so σ = 2.
3. Why do we square the differences from the mean?
   Answer: Plain differences add to zero; squares are all positive, so spread shows up.

## Flashcards
Q: How do you find the mean?
A: Add all the values, then divide by how many there are.
Q: What does a large standard deviation mean?
A: The data is spread out far from the mean.
Q: What is the standard deviation of 5, 5, 5, 5?
A: 0, because nothing is spread out.
Q: What is variance?
A: The standard deviation squared, σ².
Q: Name the steps for standard deviation.
A: Find the mean, square the differences, average them, take the square root.
