# Alkanes and alkenes

## Simple idea
Hydrocarbons are made of only hydrogen and carbon, like petrol and gas for a stove. Alkanes have only single bonds, so they are full up and not very reactive. Alkenes have one C=C double bond, which can open up, so they react easily.

## Key formulas
- Alkane: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. n: number of carbon atoms
- Alkene: CₙH₂ₙ
- Complete burning: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

## Common mistakes
- Using CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ for alkenes: alkenes are CₙH₂ₙ.
- Saying alkenes are saturated: they are unsaturated, with fewer hydrogens.
- Forgetting the test: alkenes turn orange bromine water colourless; alkanes do not.

## Check questions
1. What is the formula of the alkane with 3 carbons?
   Answer: C₃H₈.
2. What is the formula of the alkene with 4 carbons?
   Answer: C₄H₈.
3. Why is an alkene more reactive than an alkane?
   Answer: Its C=C double bond can open up and add other atoms.

## Flashcards
Q: What two elements are in a hydrocarbon?
A: Carbon and hydrogen only.
Q: What is the general formula of alkanes?
A: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.
Q: What is the general formula of alkenes?
A: CₙH₂ₙ.
Q: What happens to bromine water with an alkene?
A: It turns from orange to colourless.
Q: What are the products of complete combustion of a hydrocarbon?
A: Carbon dioxide and water.
