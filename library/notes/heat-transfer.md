# Conduction, convection and radiation

## Simple idea
Heat travels in three ways. In a metal spoon in hot soup, particles bump their neighbours along (conduction). In a pan of water, warm water rises and cool water sinks (convection). The Sun warms you across empty space with invisible infrared rays (radiation).

## Common mistakes
- Saying heat rises: warm fluid rises; heat itself goes hot to cold.
- Thinking convection works in solids: it needs a liquid or gas that can flow.
- Thinking radiation needs air: it crosses a vacuum.

## Check questions
1. Which way does heat reach Earth from the Sun?
   Answer: Radiation.
2. Name one good conductor and one good insulator.
   Answer: Metal conducts well; wool or air insulates.
3. Why is a heater best placed near the floor of a room?
   Answer: Warm air rises and cool air sinks, so the room circulates and warms evenly.

## Flashcards
Q: What is conduction?
A: Heat passing along by particles bumping into each other.
Q: What is convection?
A: Heat carried by a warm liquid or gas moving.
Q: What is radiation?
A: Heat carried by infrared waves, needing no matter.
Q: Which transfer works in a vacuum?
A: Radiation.
Q: Which surface absorbs radiation best: dull black or shiny white?
A: Dull black.

## Root check
- temperature-heat: Which way does heat flow between a hot and a cold object? Answer: From hot to cold.
