# Carboxylic acids and esters

## Simple idea
Vinegar tastes sour because of ethanoic acid, a carboxylic acid. It is a weak acid, so only a small share of its molecules let go of H⁺. Warm it with an alcohol and a drop of strong acid and you get an ester, which smells fruity, like pear drops.

## Key formulas
- R–COOH ⇌ R–COO⁻ + H⁺. Weak acid, partly ionised
- 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂
- CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O. Acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst)
- Ester hydrolysis with NaOH gives the carboxylate salt + alcohol

## Common mistakes
- Calling carboxylic acids strong: they only partly ionise.
- Naming the ester backwards: the alcohol part comes first, so ethanol + ethanoic acid gives ethyl ethanoate.
- Forgetting water as the second product of making an ester.

## Check questions
1. What gas bubbles off when ethanoic acid meets sodium carbonate?
   Answer: Carbon dioxide.
2. Name the ester made from methanol and ethanoic acid.
   Answer: Methyl ethanoate.
3. Why is ethanoic acid weak?
   Answer: Only a small fraction of its molecules lose H⁺ in water.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the functional group of a carboxylic acid?
A: –COOH.
Q: What is the functional group of an ester?
A: –COO– between two carbon chains.
Q: What catalyst makes esters from acid and alcohol?
A: Concentrated sulfuric acid.
Q: Is making an ester a reversible reaction?
A: Yes, it is an equilibrium.
Q: What do esters smell like?
A: Sweet and fruity.

## Root check
- carbonyl-compounds: Which has C=O at the end of the chain, an aldehyde or a ketone? Answer: An aldehyde.
- acids-bases: What ion do acids release in water? Answer: H⁺ (hydrogen ions).
