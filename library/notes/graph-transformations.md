# Graph transformations

## Simple idea
Change a function's formula a little and its graph moves or stretches, like sliding a drawing on a table or pulling it taller. Add to the whole function and it moves up. Change the number inside the brackets and it moves sideways, but the opposite way to what you might expect.

## Key formulas
- y = f(x) + a: slide up by a (down if a is negative)
- y = f(x + a): slide left by a. So f(x − a) slides right by a
- y = a·f(x): stretch up and down by factor a. y = −f(x): flip upside down
- y = f(−x): flip left to right

## Common mistakes
- Moving the wrong way for f(x + a): plus inside the brackets moves left.
- Mixing shifts and stretches: adding slides, multiplying stretches.
- Forgetting that changes inside the brackets only affect x.

## Check questions
1. How does y = (x − 3)² move compared with y = x²?
   Answer: Right by 3.
2. What does y = f(x) + 2 do to the graph?
   Answer: Slides it up by 2.
3. Why does f(x + 2) move the graph left?
   Answer: The old input is now reached when x is 2 less.

## Flashcards
Q: What does y = −f(x) do?
A: Flips the graph upside down, across the x-axis.
Q: What does y = f(x) − 4 do?
A: Slides the graph down by 4.
Q: What does y = f(x − 1) do?
A: Slides the graph right by 1.
Q: What does y = 2f(x) do?
A: Stretches the graph up and down by factor 2.
Q: What does y = f(−x) do?
A: Flips the graph left to right, across the y-axis.

## Root check
- functions: What is the domain of f(x) = 1/x? Answer: All x except 0.
- graphs-and-slope: What is the slope of y = 2x + 5? Answer: 2.
