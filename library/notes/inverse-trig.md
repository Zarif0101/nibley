# Inverse trig functions

## Simple idea
Normal sine takes an angle and gives a ratio. Inverse sine goes backwards: you know the ratio, and it gives back the angle. Like knowing a ramp rises 1 m for every 2 m of ramp length, and wanting to know its tilt. The calculator buttons are sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ and tan⁻¹.

## Key formulas
- θ = sin⁻¹(opposite / hypotenuse). θ: angle (degrees or radians)
- θ = cos⁻¹(adjacent / hypotenuse)
- θ = tan⁻¹(opposite / adjacent)
- sin⁻¹ gives −90° to 90°, cos⁻¹ gives 0° to 180°, tan⁻¹ gives −90° to 90°. Inputs to sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹ must be between −1 and 1.

## Picture it

Draw `unit-circle`. Label `unit-circle.point` "(cos θ, sin θ)" and `unit-circle.angle` "θ".

## Common mistakes
- Reading sin⁻¹ x as 1 / sin x: the −1 means "undo", not "one over".
- Calculator in the wrong mode: check degrees or radians first.
- Trying sin⁻¹ of a number above 1: sine never goes higher than 1.

## Check questions
1. What does sin⁻¹(0.5) give, in degrees?
   Answer: 30°.
2. A ramp is 6 m long and rises 3 m. What is its angle?
   Answer: sin⁻¹(3/6) = sin⁻¹(0.5) = 30°.
3. Why does sin⁻¹(2) have no answer?
   Answer: Sine only gives values from −1 to 1, so nothing makes 2.

## Flashcards
Q: What does sin⁻¹ do?
A: It takes a sine ratio and gives back the angle.
Q: What is tan⁻¹(1)?
A: 45°.
Q: What is cos⁻¹(0)?
A: 90°.
Q: What is the output range of sin⁻¹?
A: −90° to 90°.
Q: What is the output range of cos⁻¹?
A: 0° to 180°.

## Root check
- trig-functions: What is sin 30°? Answer: 1/2.
- composite-inverse-functions: If f(x) = x + 3, what is the inverse f⁻¹(x)? Answer: x − 3.
