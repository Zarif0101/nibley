# Power rule and basic differentiation

## Simple idea
The derivative tells you how steep a curve is at each point. For powers of x there is a quick trick: bring the power down in front, then make the power one smaller. Think of a hill: instead of measuring the slope with a ruler each time, the rule hands you a slope formula.

## Key formulas
- d/dx (xⁿ) = n·xⁿ⁻¹. x: the variable, n: the power (a number, no unit)
- d/dx (k·xⁿ) = k·n·xⁿ⁻¹. k: a constant number multiplying the term
- d/dx (c) = 0. c: a constant, a flat line has slope 0
- Differentiate a sum one term at a time. The answer is often written f′(x) or dy/dx

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to lower the power: 3x⁴ becomes 12x³, not 12x⁴.
- Differentiating a plain number as if it had an x: the derivative of 7 is 0.
- Not rewriting 1/x² or √x as powers first: write x⁻² and x^(1/2), then use the rule.

## Check questions
1. What is the derivative of x⁵?
   Answer: 5x⁴.
2. Find the slope of y = x² at x = 3.
   Answer: dy/dx = 2x, so the slope is 6.
3. Why is the derivative of a constant zero?
   Answer: A constant is a flat horizontal line, and a flat line has slope 0.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the power rule?
A: Bring the power down in front, then subtract 1 from the power.
Q: What is d/dx of 3x⁴?
A: 12x³.
Q: What is d/dx of 9?
A: 0.
Q: What is d/dx of x?
A: 1.
Q: How do you write √x so you can use the power rule?
A: x^(1/2).
