# Covalent bonding

## Simple idea
Two non-metal atoms each want a full outer shell. Instead of giving electrons away, they share a pair, like two friends sharing one umbrella. The shared pair sits between the atoms and holds them together. That joined group is a molecule.

## Key formulas
- One shared pair is a single bond: H–H
- Two shared pairs are a double bond: O=O
- Three shared pairs are a triple bond: N≡N
- Each shared pair is 2 electrons
- Water: H–O–H, with 2 single bonds

## Picture it
Draw `molecule-diatomic` and label `molecule-diatomic.bond` "shared pair: 2 electrons". Label `molecule-diatomic.atom-left` "H" and `molecule-diatomic.atom-right` "H".
Draw `water-molecule`, label `water-molecule.oxygen` "O" and each hydrogen "H".

## Common mistakes
- Saying electrons move across: in covalent bonds they are shared, not transferred.
- Counting a bond as one electron: each bond is a pair, so 2 electrons.
- Thinking covalent bonds are only between metals: they join non-metals.

## Check questions
1. How many electrons are in a double bond?
   Answer: 4, two shared pairs.
2. How many shared pairs are in one water molecule?
   Answer: 2, one in each O–H bond.
3. Why do atoms share electrons?
   Answer: So each atom gets a full outer shell.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a covalent bond?
A: A shared pair of electrons between two atoms.
Q: Which kinds of atoms form covalent bonds?
A: Non-metal atoms.
Q: What bond joins the atoms in N₂?
A: A triple bond.
Q: What is a molecule?
A: A group of atoms joined by covalent bonds.
Q: How many electrons are in one single bond?
A: Two.

## Root check
- electron-configuration: How many electrons can the 2p subshell hold? Answer: 6.
