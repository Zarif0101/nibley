# Magnetic force on charges and wires

## Simple idea
A moving charge in a magnetic field gets pushed sideways, at right angles to both its motion and the field. It never gets pushed forward or back. So it curves instead of speeding up. A current is many moving charges, so a whole wire gets pushed.

## Key formulas
- F = q·v·B·sinθ. F: force (newtons, N), q: charge (coulombs, C), v: speed (m/s), B: field (tesla, T), θ: angle between v and B
- F = B·I·L·sinθ. I: current (amperes, A), L: length of wire in the field (m), θ: angle between wire and B
- Direction (Fleming's left-hand rule): First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current, thuMb = force

## Common mistakes
- Using the wrong angle: θ is between the motion (or wire) and the field.
- Thinking the force is along the field: it is at right angles to the field and the motion.
- Expecting force when moving along the field: sin 0° = 0, so no force.

## Check questions
1. A wire of 0.5 m with 5 A sits at right angles to a 0.4 T field. What is the force?
   Answer: F = 0.4 × 5 × 0.5 × 1 = 1.0 N.
2. An electron moves at 1 × 10⁶ m/s at right angles to 0.5 T. What is the force?
   Answer: F = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 10⁶ × 0.5 = 8 × 10⁻¹⁴ N.
3. Why does the magnetic force not change a charge's speed?
   Answer: It is at right angles to the motion, so it only turns it.

## Flashcards
Q: When is the magnetic force on a moving charge biggest?
A: When it moves at right angles to the field.
Q: When is the magnetic force zero?
A: When the charge moves along the field (θ = 0°).
Q: What is the formula for force on a current-carrying wire?
A: F = B·I·L·sinθ.
Q: What path does a charge take at right angles to a field?
A: A circle.
Q: Which direction is the force relative to B and v?
A: At right angles to both.
