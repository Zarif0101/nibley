# Cells: the units of life

## Simple idea
Every living thing is made of cells, tiny building blocks, like a wall is made of bricks. You are made of trillions. Each cell can take in food, release energy, grow and make copies of itself. Some living things, like bacteria, are just one cell.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking cells are only in animals: plants, fungi and bacteria are made of cells too.
- Thinking only big things are alive: one tiny cell can be a whole living thing.
- Calling cells "tiny organs": organs are made of cells, so a cell is smaller.

## Check questions
1. What are all living things made of?
   Answer: Cells.
2. A bacterium is made of how many cells?
   Answer: One. It is a single-celled living thing.
3. Why do we say a cell is a "unit of life"?
   Answer: It is the smallest part that can do all the jobs of life, like growing and using energy.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the basic unit of life?
A: The cell.
Q: What does the nucleus do?
A: It controls the cell and holds its instructions (DNA).
Q: What does the cell membrane do?
A: It is the thin outer skin that controls what goes in and out.
Q: What is cytoplasm?
A: The jelly-like liquid inside a cell where many jobs happen.
Q: Name three things all living things do.
A: Grow, use energy, and reproduce (make more of themselves).
