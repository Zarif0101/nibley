# Polymers

## Simple idea
A polymer is like a long paper-clip chain made from thousands of identical clips. The clips are small molecules called monomers. Plastic bags are one kind of chain. In one method the clips just snap together. In the other, each join pushes out a small molecule such as water.

## Key formulas
- n CH₂=CH₂ → (–CH₂–CH₂–)ₙ. Addition: n: number of monomers, no atoms lost
- Condensation: monomers with two functional groups join and lose a small molecule (often H₂O)
- Polyester: diol + dicarboxylic acid. Polyamide: diamine + dicarboxylic acid
- Polymer mass = n × M(monomer). M: molar mass (g/mol)

## Common mistakes
- Keeping the double bond in the repeat unit: it opens up and becomes single.
- Thinking addition polymers lose a molecule: only condensation ones do.
- Using one functional group per monomer for condensation: it needs two, one at each end.

## Check questions
1. What kind of monomer makes an addition polymer?
   Answer: One with a C=C double bond, such as an alkene.
2. A chain has 500 ethene units (M = 28 g/mol each). What is its molar mass?
   Answer: 500 × 28 = 14 000 g/mol.
3. Why does a polyester need two functional groups on each monomer?
   Answer: Each monomer must join at both ends to keep the chain growing.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a monomer?
A: A small molecule that joins others to make a polymer.
Q: What is lost in a condensation polymer link?
A: A small molecule, often water.
Q: Name a polymer made by addition.
A: Poly(ethene).
Q: Which two monomer types make nylon?
A: A diamine and a dicarboxylic acid.
Q: What does the n in a polymer formula mean?
A: The number of repeat units.

## Root check
- hydrocarbons: What is the formula of ethene? Answer: C₂H₄.
- carboxylic-acids-esters: Which two substances react to make an ester? Answer: A carboxylic acid and an alcohol.
