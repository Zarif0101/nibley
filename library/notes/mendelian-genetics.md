# Mendelian genetics

## Simple idea
You get two copies of each gene, one from each parent. Different versions of a gene are called alleles. A dominant allele shows up even if you have just one copy. A recessive allele only shows when you have two copies. A Punnett square is a small grid that shows the possible children.

## Key formulas
- Cross Tt × Tt gives genotypes TT : Tt : tt = 1:2:1
- Same cross gives phenotypes dominant : recessive = 3:1
- Cross Tt × tt gives dominant : recessive = 1:1
- Dihybrid cross (two genes, both parents heterozygous) gives 9:3:3:1

## Picture it
Draw `punnett-square`. Write "T" at `punnett-square.top-1` and "t" at `punnett-square.top-2`; the same, "T" and "t", at `punnett-square.side-1` and `punnett-square.side-2`. Fill each cell, for example "TT" in `punnett-square.cell-11`.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking dominant means more common: it only means it shows when present.
- Mixing up genotype and phenotype: genotype is the letters, phenotype is the look.
- Forgetting heterozygous (Tt) looks dominant: only tt shows the recessive trait.

## Check questions
1. Which genotype shows a recessive trait?
   Answer: Two recessive alleles, like tt.
2. Cross Tt × Tt. What fraction of children are tt?
   Answer: 1 in 4.
3. Why can two tall parents have a short child?
   Answer: Both can carry a hidden recessive allele (Tt) and pass it on.

## Flashcards
Q: What is an allele?
A: A version of a gene.
Q: What does homozygous mean?
A: Two identical alleles for a gene.
Q: What does heterozygous mean?
A: Two different alleles for a gene.
Q: What is a genotype?
A: The alleles an organism has.
Q: What ratio comes from Tt × Tt?
A: 3 dominant to 1 recessive.

## Root check
- meiosis: How many chromosome sets does a gamete have compared with a body cell? Answer: Half as many.
- probability-basics: What is the chance of heads on one coin toss? Answer: 1 in 2, or 50%.
