# Temperature and heat

## Simple idea
Everything is made of tiny particles that jiggle all the time. Temperature tells you how hard they jiggle on average, so a hot cup has faster jiggling than a cold one. Heat is the energy that flows from the hotter thing to the colder thing, like warmth moving from your tea into your hands.

## Key formulas
- T(K) = T(°C) + 273. T(K): temperature in kelvin (K), T(°C): temperature in degrees Celsius (°C)
- Heat is energy, so it is measured in joules (J). Temperature is not energy.

## Picture it
Draw `thermometer`. Label `thermometer.bulb` "bulb touches the hot thing" and put an arrow at `thermometer.level` labelled "liquid rises when hotter".
Label `thermometer.top` "higher = hotter".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up heat and temperature: temperature is how hot, heat is energy moving.
- Saying "cold flows into" things: heat always flows from hot to cold.
- Using °C in gas formulas: add 273 to get kelvin first.

## Check questions
1. Which way does heat flow between a hot drink and cold air?
   Answer: From the hot drink to the cold air.
2. Convert 27 °C to kelvin.
   Answer: 300 K.
3. Why does a thermometer's liquid rise when it touches something hot?
   Answer: The liquid's particles get more energy and take up more space, so it expands up the tube.

## Flashcards
Q: What does temperature measure?
A: How hot something is, the average jiggling energy of its particles.
Q: What is heat?
A: Energy flowing from a hotter thing to a colder thing.
Q: Unit of heat?
A: The joule (J).
Q: How do you change °C to K?
A: Add 273.
Q: When does heat stop flowing between two things?
A: When they reach the same temperature.
