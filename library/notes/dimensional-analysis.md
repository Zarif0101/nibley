# Dimensional analysis

## Simple idea
You cannot add apples to metres. Both sides of an equation must be the same kind of thing: length, mass or time. Check the units on each side. If they do not match, the equation is wrong. It is a quick test for a formula you half remember.

## Key formulas
- [L] length, [M] mass, [T] time. These are the basic "kinds" (dimensions)
- speed = distance ÷ time has dimensions [L]/[T]. Unit: m/s
- acceleration has dimensions [L]/[T]². Unit: m/s²
- force has dimensions [M][L]/[T]². Unit: newton, N = kg·m/s²

## Picture it
On note paper, write the formula in the middle. Under the left side write its units, under the right side write its units. Draw an equals sign between the two unit lines and tick it if they match.

## Common mistakes
- Adding terms with different units: only add things with the same unit.
- Thinking a matching check proves the formula: it only catches wrong units, not wrong numbers like ½.
- Forgetting squares: a in m/s² has T squared, not T.

## Check questions
1. What are the dimensions of speed?
   Answer: Length over time, [L]/[T].
2. Do the units match in v = u + a·t? Check them.
   Answer: Yes. a·t is (m/s²)·s = m/s, the same as v and u.
3. Why can't you add 5 m to 3 s?
   Answer: Length and time are different kinds of quantity.

## Flashcards
Q: What are the three basic dimensions in mechanics?
A: Length, mass and time.
Q: What does dimensional analysis check?
A: That both sides of an equation have the same units.
Q: Dimensions of acceleration?
A: [L]/[T]², metres per second squared.
Q: Can dimensional analysis find a number like ½?
A: No, it only checks units.
Q: What unit is 1 newton in base units?
A: 1 kg·m/s².
