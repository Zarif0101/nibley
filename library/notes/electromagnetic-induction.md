# Electromagnetic induction

## Simple idea
Push a magnet into a coil of wire and a voltage appears, as long as the magnet is moving. Hold it still and nothing happens. The coil only cares about change: when the magnetic field through it grows, shrinks or turns, a voltage is made. Faster change gives bigger voltage, and more turns give more too.

## Key formulas
- Φ = B·A·cosθ. Φ: magnetic flux (webers, Wb), B: field (tesla, T), A: coil area (m²), θ: angle between B and the coil's face-on direction
- ε = −N·ΔΦ / Δt. ε: induced voltage (volts, V), N: number of turns, ΔΦ: change in flux (Wb), Δt: time taken (s)
- ε = B·L·v. A rod of length L (m) moving at speed v (m/s) at right angles to B

## Picture it
Draw `solenoid`. Arrow from `magnet.north-end` pointing at `solenoid.inside`, labelled "magnet moving in".
Label `solenoid.left-lead`: "voltage appears". Label `solenoid.center`: "N turns".

## Common mistakes
- Expecting voltage from a still magnet: only a changing flux makes voltage.
- Forgetting N: more turns multiply the voltage.
- Using time in minutes: Δt must be in seconds.

## Check questions
1. A 100-turn coil's flux changes by 0.02 Wb in 0.5 s. What is the voltage?
   Answer: ε = 100 × 0.02 / 0.5 = 4 V.
2. A coil of area 0.05 m² faces a 0.2 T field head-on. What is the flux?
   Answer: Φ = 0.2 × 0.05 = 0.01 Wb.
3. Why does pulling the magnet out faster give more voltage?
   Answer: The flux changes in less time, so ΔΦ / Δt is bigger.

## Flashcards
Q: What is magnetic flux?
A: The amount of field passing through a coil's area.
Q: What is the unit of magnetic flux?
A: The weber (Wb), equal to T·m².
Q: What does Faraday's law say?
A: Induced voltage equals the rate of change of flux, times turns.
Q: When is no voltage induced?
A: When the flux through the coil is not changing.
Q: Name three ways to change flux.
A: Change B, change the area, or turn the coil.
