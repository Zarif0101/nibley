# Alcohols

## Simple idea
An alcohol is a hydrocarbon with an –OH group on it. Ethanol in hand sanitiser is one. The –OH group is the active spot. Alcohols burn well, can be oxidised, and join with acids to make sweet-smelling esters.

## Key formulas
- C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O. Burning ethanol
- Primary alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid (gentle then strong oxidation)
- Secondary alcohol → ketone. Tertiary alcohol: not oxidised
- C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O. Dehydration with hot concentrated H₂SO₄

## Picture it
Draw `carbon-chain` and label `carbon-chain.group` "–OH group".
Label `carbon-chain.carbon-2` "carbon holding the –OH", and write beside it "primary, secondary or tertiary?"

## Common mistakes
- Thinking all alcohols oxidise: tertiary ones have no H on the C–OH carbon, so they do not.
- Mixing up the products: primary gives aldehyde or acid, secondary gives a ketone.
- Forgetting the oxidising agent: acidified potassium dichromate turns orange to green.

## Check questions
1. What group do all alcohols contain?
   Answer: The –OH group.
2. What forms when propan-2-ol is oxidised?
   Answer: Propanone, a ketone.
3. Why can a tertiary alcohol not be oxidised easily?
   Answer: Its C–OH carbon has no hydrogen atom to lose.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the functional group of an alcohol?
A: –OH.
Q: What colour change shows an alcohol being oxidised by dichromate?
A: Orange to green.
Q: Which alcohols give ketones when oxidised?
A: Secondary alcohols.
Q: What do an alcohol and a carboxylic acid make?
A: An ester and water.
Q: What is made when ethanol is dehydrated?
A: Ethene and water.
