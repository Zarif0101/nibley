# Displacement and velocity

## Simple idea
Walk 100 m round a track and end where you started. You travelled 100 m, but your displacement is zero, because displacement only counts how far you ended from the start, in a straight line, with direction. Velocity is displacement divided by time, so it has a direction too.

## Key formulas
- Δx = x₂ − x₁. Δx: displacement (metres, m), x₁: start position (m), x₂: end position (m)
- v = Δx ÷ t. v: average velocity (m/s), t: time taken (seconds, s)
- average speed = total distance ÷ t. Distance in m, t in s, speed in m/s

## Picture it
Draw `car` facing right. Arrow from `car.back` to `car.front` labelled "displacement 40 m east". Draw `vector-arrow` above it, with the head at `vector-arrow.head` labelled "velocity v".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up distance and displacement: displacement is start-to-end, with direction.
- Forgetting the sign: going left is negative if right is positive.
- Using minutes with m/s: change time to seconds first.

## Check questions
1. A runner goes 6 m in 2 s in a straight line. What is the velocity?
   Answer: 3 m/s in that direction.
2. You walk 50 m east, then 50 m west. What is your displacement?
   Answer: Zero.
3. Why can average speed be non-zero while average velocity is zero?
   Answer: Speed counts the whole path, velocity only start to end, so returning home gives zero.

## Flashcards
Q: What is displacement?
A: Change in position, in a straight line, with direction.
Q: Is displacement a scalar or vector?
A: A vector.
Q: Formula for average velocity?
A: Displacement divided by time taken.
Q: Unit of velocity?
A: Metres per second, m/s.
Q: What does a negative velocity mean?
A: Moving in the opposite direction to the positive one.
