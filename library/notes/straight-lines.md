# Straight lines

## Simple idea
A straight line on a grid follows one rule linking x and y. The slope tells how steep it is: how far it goes up for each step across, like a ramp. The y-intercept tells where it crosses the y-axis. Together they give y = mx + c.

## Key formulas
- y = mx + c. m: slope (gradient), c: y-intercept (where x = 0)
- m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁). Slope through points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂)
- y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). Line with slope m through (x₁, y₁)
- Parallel lines: same m. Perpendicular lines: m₁ × m₂ = −1
- Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)

## Common mistakes
- Putting run over rise: slope is rise over run.
- Subtracting the points in different orders on top and bottom: keep the same order.
- Reading c as the x-intercept: c is where the line crosses the y-axis.

## Check questions
1. Find the slope through (1, 2) and (3, 6).
   Answer: (6 − 2) / (3 − 1) = 2.
2. What is the y-intercept of y = 3x − 2?
   Answer: −2.
3. Why is the perpendicular slope to m = 2 equal to −1/2?
   Answer: The slopes must multiply to −1, and 2 × (−1/2) = −1.

## Flashcards
Q: What does m mean in y = mx + c?
A: The slope: rise over run.
Q: What does c mean in y = mx + c?
A: The y-intercept, where the line crosses the y-axis.
Q: What do parallel lines share?
A: The same slope.
Q: Slope of a line perpendicular to slope 3?
A: −1/3.
Q: Slope of a horizontal line?
A: 0.
