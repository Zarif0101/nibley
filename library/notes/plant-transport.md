# Transport in plants

## Simple idea
A plant has two sets of pipes. Xylem carries water and minerals up from the roots, like a straw. Phloem carries sugar made in leaves to wherever it is needed. Water evaporates from the leaves, and this pulls more water up the plant behind it.

## Key formulas
- transpiration: water evaporates from leaves through stomata, pulling water up the xylem
- translocation: sugars move in the phloem, in any direction, from leaves to where needed

## Picture it
Draw `plant`. Arrow from `plant.root` up through `plant.stem`, labelled "xylem: water up". Arrow from `plant.leaf` down the stem, labelled "phloem: sugar moves". Arrow out of `plant.leaf`, labelled "water vapour out".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up xylem and phloem: xylem is water, phloem is sugar.
- Thinking water only goes up and sugar only goes down: sugar goes wherever it is needed.
- Thinking roots push water all the way up: evaporation pulls it.

## Check questions
1. Which tissue carries water up a plant?
   Answer: Xylem.
2. Name the tiny holes in a leaf where water vapour leaves.
   Answer: Stomata.
3. Why does a plant wilt on a hot, dry day?
   Answer: It loses water faster than the roots can supply it.

## Flashcards
Q: What does phloem carry?
A: Sugars made in the leaves.
Q: What is transpiration?
A: Water evaporating from the leaves through the stomata.
Q: What do root hairs do?
A: Take in water and minerals by giving a big surface area.
Q: How do root hairs take in water?
A: By osmosis.
Q: What speeds up transpiration?
A: Heat, wind, dry air and bright light.
