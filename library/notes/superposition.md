# Superposition of waves

## Simple idea
When two ripples cross in a pond, they pass through each other. While they overlap, you just add their heights at each point. Two crests make a bigger crest. A crest and an equal trough cancel to flat water. Afterwards each ripple carries on as before.

## Key formulas
- y = y₁ + y₂. y: combined displacement (m), y₁ and y₂: displacements of the two waves at that point (m); crests are positive, troughs negative

## Picture it
Draw `wave-transverse` twice, one above the other. Label `wave-transverse.crest` "crest + crest = big crest".
Label `wave-transverse.trough` "crest + trough = flat".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking waves bounce off each other: they pass through.
- Forgetting signs: troughs are negative, so add with the minus sign.
- Thinking cancelling destroys energy: the energy shows up elsewhere.

## Check questions
1. A 3 cm crest meets a 2 cm crest. What is the combined height?
   Answer: 5 cm.
2. A 3 cm crest meets a 3 cm trough. What is the result?
   Answer: 0 cm, flat.
3. Why can two waves cancel out?
   Answer: Their displacements add, and a crest plus an equal trough is zero.

## Flashcards
Q: State the principle of superposition.
A: Overlapping waves add their displacements.
Q: What is constructive interference?
A: Crests meet crests, making a bigger wave.
Q: What is destructive interference?
A: A crest meets a trough, making a smaller wave or none.
Q: Do waves change after crossing?
A: No, they carry on unchanged.
Q: Two waves of height 2 cm, both crests. Total?
A: 4 cm.
