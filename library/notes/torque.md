# Torque and moments

## Simple idea
Torque is a force's turning effect. A door is easy to open when you push far from the hinge, and hard near the hinge. A longer spanner turns a stubborn nut the same way. Turning effect depends on the force and on how far it is from the pivot.

## Key formulas
- τ = F·d. τ: torque or moment (N·m), F: force (N), d: perpendicular distance from the pivot (m)
- clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments, when balanced

## Picture it
Draw `seesaw`. Arrow down at `seesaw.left-end`, labelled "force F". Dashed line from `seesaw.pivot` to `seesaw.left-end`, labelled "distance d".
Label at `seesaw.pivot`: "pivot".

## Common mistakes
- Measuring distance to the wrong place: measure from the pivot, at right angles to the force.
- Adding up forces instead of moments: multiply each force by its distance.
- Writing N instead of N·m for torque.

## Check questions
1. What is the unit of torque?
   Answer: The newton metre (N·m).
2. A 20 N push acts 0.5 m from a pivot at right angles. What is the torque?
   Answer: 20 × 0.5 = 10 N·m.
3. Why is a door handle placed far from the hinges?
   Answer: A bigger distance gives more torque for the same push.

## Flashcards
Q: What is torque?
A: The turning effect of a force about a pivot.
Q: What is the torque formula?
A: τ = F·d.
Q: What does d mean in the torque formula?
A: Perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force's line.
Q: Doubling the distance from the pivot does what to torque?
A: It doubles it.
Q: What is the torque of a force acting through the pivot?
A: Zero.
