# Digestion

## Simple idea
Food is too big to pass into your blood, so your gut breaks it into small pieces. Chewing and churning cut it up, then enzymes break the molecules apart, like scissors on a long rope. The tiny pieces pass through the gut wall into the blood.

## Key formulas
- starch → sugars (enzyme: amylase)
- protein → amino acids (enzyme: protease)
- fat → fatty acids + glycerol (enzyme: lipase)

## Picture it
Draw `enzyme-substrate`. Label `enzyme-substrate.enzyme`: "digestive enzyme". Label `enzyme-substrate.substrate`: "big food molecule". Arrow from `enzyme-substrate.active-site`, labelled "broken into small pieces".

## Common mistakes
- Saying digestion happens only in the stomach: most of it and the absorbing happen in the small intestine.
- Thinking bile is an enzyme: bile breaks fat into droplets, but it does not break chemical bonds.
- Mixing up digestion and absorption: digestion breaks food down, absorption moves it into the blood.

## Check questions
1. What does protease break protein into?
   Answer: Amino acids.
2. Where is most food absorbed?
   Answer: The small intestine.
3. Why must starch be digested before it can enter the blood?
   Answer: It is too big to pass through the gut wall.

## Flashcards
Q: What does amylase digest?
A: Starch, into sugars.
Q: What does lipase digest?
A: Fats, into fatty acids and glycerol.
Q: Where is bile made?
A: In the liver, and it is stored in the gall bladder.
Q: What do villi do?
A: They give the small intestine a big surface to absorb food.
Q: What does the large intestine mainly absorb?
A: Water.
