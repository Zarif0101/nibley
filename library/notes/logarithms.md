# Logarithms

## Simple idea
A logarithm asks: what power do I need? log₂ 8 asks "2 to what power gives 8?" The answer is 3. So a logarithm undoes an exponential. It turns multiplying into adding, which is why it makes huge numbers easy to handle.

## Key formulas
- log_b x = y means bʸ = x. b: base, x: the number, y: the power
- log a + log b = log(ab). log a − log b = log(a/b)
- log(aⁿ) = n·log a
- log_b b = 1. log_b 1 = 0

## Common mistakes
- Writing log(a + b) = log a + log b: only multiplying splits into adding.
- Taking the log of zero or a negative: not possible, x must be above 0.
- Mixing up which number is the base and which is the power.

## Check questions
1. What is log₂ 8?
   Answer: 3, since 2³ = 8.
2. What is log₁₀ 1000?
   Answer: 3.
3. Why can you not take the log of a negative number?
   Answer: A positive base to any power never gives a negative result.

## Flashcards
Q: What does log_b x = y mean?
A: bʸ = x.
Q: What is log_b 1?
A: 0, because b⁰ = 1.
Q: What is log₅ 5?
A: 1.
Q: What is log a + log b?
A: log(ab).
Q: What is log₂ 16?
A: 4.
