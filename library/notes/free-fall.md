# Free fall

## Simple idea
Drop a heavy book and a sheet of paper scrunched into a ball. They land together. When only gravity pulls on something, it speeds up by the same amount every second, whatever its mass. Near Earth that is about 9.8 m/s² downward. Air pushing back is ignored.

## Key formulas
- g = 9.8 m/s². g: acceleration due to gravity, pointing down
- v = g·t. v: speed after dropping from rest (m/s), t: time (s)
- h = ½·g·t². h: distance fallen from rest (m)
- v² = 2·g·h. v: speed (m/s), h: height fallen (m)

## Picture it
Draw `ground` along the bottom and `ball` high above it. Arrow from `ball.bottom` pointing down labelled "g = 9.8 m/s²". Label `ground.mid`: "lands here".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking heavier things fall faster: without air, all fall at the same rate.
- Thinking gravity stops at the top of a throw: speed is zero for an instant, but g still acts.
- Mixing directions: pick up or down as positive and keep to it.

## Check questions
1. A ball is dropped from rest. What is its speed after 2 s?
   Answer: 19.6 m/s.
2. How far does it fall in those 2 s?
   Answer: ½ × 9.8 × 2² = 19.6 m.
3. Why do a feather and a hammer land together on the Moon?
   Answer: There is no air, so only gravity acts and g is the same for both.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the value of g near Earth?
A: About 9.8 m/s², downward.
Q: What is free fall?
A: Falling with only gravity acting.
Q: Does mass change how fast things fall in free fall?
A: No, all fall with the same acceleration.
Q: How much does speed grow each second in free fall?
A: By about 9.8 m/s.
Q: What is the acceleration at the top of a thrown ball's path?
A: Still 9.8 m/s² down.
