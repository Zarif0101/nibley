# Forces and weight

## Simple idea
A force is a push or a pull. Gravity pulls everything down toward the Earth, and that pull on an object is its weight. Mass is how much stuff is in the object. A bag of rice has the same mass anywhere, but its weight changes if gravity changes.

## Key formulas
- W = m·g. W: weight (newtons, N), m: mass (kilograms, kg), g: gravity strength (9.8 N/kg)
- 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². This is how the newton is built from basic units

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up mass and weight: mass is in kg, weight is a force in N.
- Giving weight in kilograms: multiply by 9.8 to get newtons.
- Forgetting direction: weight always pulls down.

## Check questions
1. What is the weight of a 5 kg bag?
   Answer: 49 N (5 × 9.8).
2. What is the SI unit of force?
   Answer: The newton (N).
3. Why does an astronaut weigh less on the Moon but have the same mass?
   Answer: Gravity is weaker there, but the amount of stuff in the astronaut is unchanged.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a force?
A: A push or a pull.
Q: What is weight?
A: The pull of gravity on a mass.
Q: What is g on Earth?
A: 9.8 N/kg (the same as 9.8 m/s²).
Q: Which direction does weight act?
A: Straight down, toward the centre of the Earth.
Q: What is the weight of 1 kg?
A: 9.8 N.
