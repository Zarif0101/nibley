# Ecosystems and energy flow

## Simple idea
Energy in an ecosystem starts with the Sun. Plants, the producers, capture it. A rabbit eats the plant and a fox eats the rabbit. At each step only about 10% passes on, since most energy is used for living or lost as heat. That is why food chains are short and there are fewer animals at the top.

## Key formulas
- Food chain: producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer
- At each level only about 10% of energy passes on
- Example: 1000 units in plants → about 100 in herbivores → about 10 in carnivores

## Common mistakes
- Saying energy is recycled: energy flows one way, and ends as heat; matter is recycled.
- Thinking the energy vanishes: it is used for movement, warmth and life, or lost as heat.
- Thinking arrows show who eats whom: arrows show the direction energy moves.

## Check questions
1. What is a producer?
   Answer: An organism, usually a plant, that makes its own food.
2. Plants hold 5000 units of energy. About how much reaches herbivores?
   Answer: About 500 units.
3. Why are there so few top predators?
   Answer: Little energy reaches the top, so it supports only a few animals.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the original source of energy for most ecosystems?
A: The Sun.
Q: What is a herbivore?
A: An animal that eats plants.
Q: What is a decomposer?
A: An organism that breaks down dead matter.
Q: How much energy passes to the next level?
A: Only about 10%.
Q: In what form is most energy lost?
A: Heat.

## Root check
- photosynthesis: What does a plant make in photosynthesis? Answer: Glucose and oxygen.
- cell-respiration: Why do cells respire? Answer: To release energy from food.
