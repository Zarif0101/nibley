# Free-body diagrams

## Simple idea
A free-body diagram is a quick sketch of one object with every push and pull on it drawn as an arrow. Think of a book on a table. Draw just the book as a box, then arrows: weight down, table push up. Longer arrow means bigger force. Then you can see the net force.

## Key formulas
- W = m·g. W: weight (newtons, N), m: mass (kg), g: 9.8 m/s²
- net force = sum of all forces. Add with directions, in N
- balanced forces: net force = 0 N

## Picture it
Draw `block` alone. Arrow from `block.bottom` pointing down labelled "weight W". Arrow from `block.top` pointing up labelled "normal force N". Arrow from `block.right` pointing right labelled "push", and from `block.left` pointing left labelled "friction".
Draw one `vector-arrow` beside it labelled "arrow = one force".

## Common mistakes
- Drawing forces the object pushes on others: only draw forces on the object.
- Drawing the object's motion as a force: velocity is not a force.
- Arrows of wrong length: balanced forces need equal lengths.

## Check questions
1. A 2 kg book rests on a table. What is its weight?
   Answer: 2 × 9.8 = 19.6 N.
2. What is the table's push on that book?
   Answer: 19.6 N upward, since the forces balance.
3. Why draw only one object in the diagram?
   Answer: Only forces on that object decide how it moves.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a free-body diagram?
A: A sketch of one object with all forces on it as arrows.
Q: What does arrow length show?
A: The size of the force.
Q: What does arrow direction show?
A: The direction the force pushes or pulls.
Q: Name the two forces on a book at rest on a table.
A: Weight down and normal force up.
Q: What is the net force if forces are balanced?
A: Zero.

## Root check
- newtons-first-law: A box moves at steady speed in a straight line. What is the net force on it? Answer: Zero.
- scalars-vectors: Is force a scalar or a vector? Answer: A vector, it has direction.
