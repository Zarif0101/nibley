# Quadratic equations and graphs

## Simple idea
A quadratic has an x² in it, like x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Its graph is a U-shaped curve called a parabola. The answers to the equation are the roots: the places where the curve crosses the x-axis. The curve can cross twice, touch once, or miss completely.

## Key formulas
- ax² + bx + c = 0. a, b, c: numbers, with a not zero
- x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a). The quadratic formula
- Δ = b² − 4ac. Δ: discriminant. Δ > 0: two roots, Δ = 0: one root, Δ < 0: none
- x = −b / (2a): the x-position of the lowest or highest point

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the ± so you only find one root: there are usually two.
- Sign slips with −b when b is negative: write brackets, −(−5) = 5.
- Solving before the equation equals zero: move everything to one side first.

## Check questions
1. Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
   Answer: x = 2 or x = 3.
2. Find Δ for x² + 2x + 5. How many roots?
   Answer: Δ = 4 − 20 = −16, so no real roots.
3. Why does x² = 9 give two answers?
   Answer: Both 3 and −3 squared make 9.

## Flashcards
Q: What shape is a quadratic graph?
A: A parabola, a U-shaped curve.
Q: What are the roots of a quadratic?
A: The x-values where the graph crosses the x-axis.
Q: What is the discriminant?
A: b² − 4ac.
Q: What does a negative discriminant tell you?
A: The graph never crosses the x-axis, so no real roots.
Q: What is the quadratic formula?
A: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a).

## Root check
- factorising: Factorise x² + 5x + 6. Answer: (x + 2)(x + 3).
- graphs-and-slope: In y = mx + c, what does c tell you? Answer: Where the line crosses the y-axis.
