# Rearranging formulas

## Simple idea
A formula like d = v·t gives distance when you know speed and time. What if you know distance and speed and want time? Rearrange it. Use the balance rule: do the same to both sides until the letter you want stands alone. That letter is the "subject".

## Key formulas
- d = v·t. d: distance (m), v: speed (m/s), t: time (s)
- t = d ÷ v. The same formula with t as the subject
- y = mx + c gives x = (y − c) ÷ m. m and c are fixed numbers

## Picture it
Draw `scale-balance`. Label `scale-balance.left-pan` "d" and `scale-balance.right-pan` "v × t".
Label `scale-balance.pivot` "÷ v on both sides".

## Common mistakes
- Moving a term without flipping it: y = x + c gives x = y − c.
- Dividing only part: x = (y − c) ÷ m, not y − c ÷ m. Divide the whole side.
- Turning it upside down: t = d ÷ v, not v ÷ d.

## Check questions
1. In d = v·t, which letter is the subject?
   Answer: d.
2. Distance is 20 m, speed is 4 m/s. What is the time?
   Answer: 5 s.
3. Why do you do the same thing to both sides?
   Answer: The formula stays true, like a balance staying level.

## Flashcards
Q: Make x the subject of y = x + 5.
A: x = y − 5.
Q: Make a the subject of F = m·a.
A: a = F ÷ m.
Q: Make d the subject of v = d ÷ t.
A: d = v·t.
Q: Make x the subject of y = x − 3.
A: x = y + 3.
Q: First step to make x the subject of y = mx + c?
A: Subtract c from both sides.
