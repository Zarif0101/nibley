# Breathing and gas exchange

## Simple idea
Your cells need oxygen and make carbon dioxide. Your lungs swap them. When you breathe in, air reaches tiny air sacs called alveoli. Oxygen slips into the blood and carbon dioxide slips out, both by diffusion. It works well because the walls are very thin and next to blood vessels.

## Key formulas
- oxygen: air in alveolus → blood (diffusion)
- carbon dioxide: blood → air in alveolus (diffusion)

## Picture it
Draw `alveolus`. Arrow along `alveolus.oxygen-arrow` into the blood, labelled "oxygen in". Arrow along `alveolus.co2-arrow` out to the sac, labelled "carbon dioxide out". Label `alveolus.capillary`: "blood vessel, thin wall".

## Common mistakes
- Saying we breathe out no oxygen: breathed-out air still has some oxygen.
- Mixing up inhaling: the diaphragm moves down and the ribs go up and out.
- Thinking gas moves by being pumped: it moves by diffusion.

## Check questions
1. Which gas moves from the alveolus into the blood?
   Answer: Oxygen.
2. Name two features that make alveoli good for gas exchange.
   Answer: Any two of: thin walls, big surface area, many capillaries, moist.
3. Why does breathing get faster during exercise?
   Answer: Muscles need more oxygen and make more carbon dioxide to remove.

## Flashcards
Q: What are alveoli?
A: Tiny air sacs in the lungs where gases are swapped.
Q: How do gases cross the alveolus wall?
A: By diffusion.
Q: What happens to the diaphragm when you breathe in?
A: It contracts and moves down.
Q: Which tube carries air from the mouth to the lungs?
A: The trachea (windpipe).
Q: Why is a big surface area helpful?
A: More gas can cross at once.
