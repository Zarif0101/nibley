# Kinetic energy

## Simple idea
Kinetic energy is the energy something has because it is moving. A rolling tennis ball is easy to stop; a speeding car is not. Heavier and faster means more energy. Speed matters most: go twice as fast and the energy is four times bigger.

## Key formulas
- KE = ½·m·v². KE: kinetic energy (joules, J), m: mass (kg), v: speed (m/s)
- W = ΔKE. W: net work done (J), ΔKE: change in kinetic energy (J)

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to square the speed: v² means v times v.
- Doubling the speed and doubling the energy: it becomes four times bigger.
- Using km/h: change to m/s first (divide by 3.6).

## Check questions
1. What is the kinetic energy formula?
   Answer: KE = ½·m·v².
2. What is the kinetic energy of a 2 kg ball moving at 3 m/s?
   Answer: ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.
3. Why does a car at twice the speed need about four times the braking distance?
   Answer: It has four times the energy, and the brakes remove it at the same rate per metre.

## Flashcards
Q: What is kinetic energy?
A: Energy an object has because it is moving.
Q: What is the formula for kinetic energy?
A: KE = ½·m·v².
Q: Speed triples. What happens to kinetic energy?
A: It becomes nine times bigger.
Q: Can kinetic energy be negative?
A: No. Mass and v² are never negative.
Q: What is the unit of kinetic energy?
A: The joule (J).

## Root check
- work-done: A 50 N force moves a box 2 m in its own direction. How much work? Answer: 100 J.
- powers-roots: What is 5² and what is √36? Answer: 25 and 6.
