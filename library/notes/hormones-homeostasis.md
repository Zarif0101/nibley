# Hormones and homeostasis

## Simple idea
Your body keeps things steady, like a thermostat in a house. Hormones are chemical messages sent through the blood. They reach certain organs and change what they do. After a big meal, blood sugar rises, so the body releases insulin to bring it back down. This steady balance is called homeostasis.

## Key formulas
- Negative feedback loop: change from normal → body responds → change is reversed
- Blood sugar high → insulin released → sugar stored → blood sugar falls
- Blood sugar low → glucagon released → sugar released → blood sugar rises

## Picture it
Draw `thermometer` and put "normal level" at `thermometer.level`. Arrow up from `thermometer.bulb` labelled "too hot", then an arrow from `thermometer.top` labelled "body sweats, cools down".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up insulin and glucagon: insulin lowers blood sugar, glucagon raises it.
- Thinking hormones act as fast as nerves: hormones travel in blood, so they are slower but last longer.
- Thinking feedback pushes the change further: negative feedback reverses the change.

## Check questions
1. Which hormone lowers blood sugar?
   Answer: Insulin.
2. After exercise, body temperature rises. Name one way the body cools down.
   Answer: Sweating, or widening skin blood vessels.
3. Why is it called negative feedback?
   Answer: The response reverses the change, bringing the body back to normal.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a hormone?
A: A chemical messenger carried in the blood.
Q: What is homeostasis?
A: Keeping the body's inside conditions steady.
Q: Which gland makes insulin?
A: The pancreas.
Q: What does glucagon do?
A: It raises blood sugar.
Q: Which type of feedback keeps conditions steady?
A: Negative feedback.
