# Trig functions and the unit circle

## Simple idea
Picture a dot going round a circle of radius 1, like a rider on a ferris wheel. Its height is sine of the angle, and how far it is across is cosine. After one full turn the dot is back at the start, so the values repeat. That repeating is why trig describes waves and spinning things.

## Key formulas
- cos θ = x, sin θ = y. (x, y): the dot's position on a circle of radius 1, θ: angle from the positive x-axis (radians or degrees)
- tan θ = sin θ / cos θ. Undefined when cos θ = 0
- −1 ≤ sin θ ≤ 1 and −1 ≤ cos θ ≤ 1
- Period: sin and cos repeat every 2π radians (360°)

Draw `unit-circle`. Put a dot at `unit-circle.point`, label the angle at `unit-circle.angle` "θ". Label the dashed drop at `unit-circle.foot` "cos θ across", and the vertical height "sin θ up".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up which is which: cosine is across (x), sine is up (y).
- Forgetting signs: in the left half cos is negative, in the bottom half sin is negative.
- Saying sin can be 2: on a radius-1 circle it never goes past 1.

## Check questions
1. What are sin 90° and cos 90°?
   Answer: sin 90° = 1 and cos 90° = 0.
2. Is cos 180° positive or negative, and what is its value?
   Answer: Negative, −1.
3. Why can sine never be bigger than 1?
   Answer: It is the height on a circle of radius 1, so it cannot go higher than 1.

## Flashcards
Q: On the unit circle, what does cos θ equal?
A: The x-coordinate of the point.
Q: On the unit circle, what does sin θ equal?
A: The y-coordinate of the point.
Q: What is the period of sine, in radians?
A: 2π.
Q: What is the range of sine?
A: From −1 to 1.
Q: Write tan θ using sin and cos.
A: tan θ = sin θ / cos θ.

## Root check
- trig-ratios: A right triangle has opposite side 3 and hypotenuse 5. What is sin of the angle? Answer: 3/5.
- radians: How many radians is 180°? Answer: π radians.
- functions: If f(x) = 2x + 1, what is f(3)? Answer: 7.
