# DNA structure and replication

## Simple idea
DNA is like a twisted ladder. The sides are made of sugar and phosphate, and the rungs are pairs of bases. Before a cell divides, the ladder unzips down the middle. Each side then collects new matching bases, so you end up with two identical copies. Each copy keeps one old strand.

## Key formulas
- Base pairs in DNA: A pairs with T, C pairs with G
- Replication: 1 DNA double helix → 2 identical double helices
- Semi-conservative: each new DNA = 1 old strand + 1 new strand
- Base ratio: amount of A = amount of T, amount of C = amount of G

## Picture it
Draw `dna-helix`. Label `dna-helix.strand-1` "sugar-phosphate backbone" and `dna-helix.base-pair` "A–T or C–G". Arrow from `dna-helix.top` pointing down, labelled "unzips here".

## Common mistakes
- Pairing A with G: A pairs with T, and C pairs with G.
- Thinking a copy is two brand-new strands: each copy keeps one old strand.
- Thinking uracil is in DNA: DNA has thymine, uracil is in RNA.

## Check questions
1. Which base pairs with C in DNA?
   Answer: G.
2. One strand reads A-T-G-C. What does the matching strand read?
   Answer: T-A-C-G.
3. Why is replication called semi-conservative?
   Answer: Each new DNA keeps one old strand and gets one new strand.

## Flashcards
Q: What shape is DNA?
A: A double helix, like a twisted ladder.
Q: Which enzyme joins new DNA bases together?
A: DNA polymerase.
Q: What does the enzyme helicase do?
A: It unzips the two DNA strands.
Q: What are the four DNA bases?
A: Adenine, thymine, cytosine and guanine.
Q: What holds the two strands together?
A: Hydrogen bonds between the paired bases.

## Root check
- biomolecules: Name the four main types of biomolecule. Answer: Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids.
