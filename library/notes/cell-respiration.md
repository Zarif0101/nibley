# Cellular respiration

## Simple idea
Respiration is not breathing. It is how every cell releases energy from food. Think of a car burning petrol, but slow and gentle. Cells break down glucose and store the energy in a tiny fuel called ATP. With oxygen, you get a lot of ATP. Without oxygen, only a little.

## Key formulas
- aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (+ a lot of ATP)
- C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
- anaerobic in muscle: glucose → lactic acid (+ a little ATP)
- anaerobic in yeast: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide

## Common mistakes
- Calling respiration breathing: breathing moves air, respiration releases energy in cells.
- Thinking plants do not respire: all living cells do, day and night.
- Thinking energy is made: it is released from glucose and moved to ATP.

## Check questions
1. Where in the cell does aerobic respiration mostly happen?
   Answer: In the mitochondria.
2. Name the two products of aerobic respiration.
   Answer: Carbon dioxide and water.
3. Why do you pant after sprinting?
   Answer: Muscles used anaerobic respiration, made lactic acid, and need extra oxygen to clear it.

## Flashcards
Q: What is ATP?
A: The small molecule cells use to carry and supply energy.
Q: Write the word equation for aerobic respiration.
A: Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.
Q: Does anaerobic respiration make a lot or a little ATP?
A: A little.
Q: What do yeast make in anaerobic respiration?
A: Ethanol and carbon dioxide.
Q: Why are mitochondria folded inside?
A: To give more surface for the reactions.
