# Wave-particle duality

## Simple idea
Light makes stripes like a wave, but it also hits a metal in packets like a particle. Electrons act the same way: they leave dots like particles, yet pile into stripes like waves. Everything small has both sides. Which one you see depends on the experiment.

## Key formulas
- λ = h / p. λ: wavelength (metres, m), h: 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, p: momentum (kg·m/s)
- p = m·v. p: momentum, m: mass (kg), v: speed (m/s)
- E = h·f. E: photon energy (joules, J), f: frequency (hertz, Hz)

## Common mistakes
- Thinking it is half wave and half particle at once: it shows one side per experiment.
- Thinking big things have noticeable wavelengths: a ball's λ is far too tiny to see.
- Using mass in grams: p needs kilograms.

## Check questions
1. Which experiment shows light as a wave, and which as a particle?
   Answer: Double slit shows wave; photoelectric effect shows particle.
2. An electron has momentum 6.63 × 10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s. What is its wavelength?
   Answer: 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.
3. Why do we never notice a thrown ball's wavelength?
   Answer: Its momentum is huge, so λ = h / p is far too tiny.

## Flashcards
Q: Who proposed that matter has a wavelength?
A: Louis de Broglie.
Q: What is the de Broglie wavelength?
A: λ = h / p.
Q: What showed electrons act as waves?
A: Electrons make diffraction patterns, like waves do.
Q: What showed light acts as particles?
A: The photoelectric effect.
Q: What happens to wavelength if momentum rises?
A: It gets smaller.

## Root check
- photoelectric-effect: Does the photoelectric effect show light as a wave or as particles? Answer: As particles (photons).
- diffraction: What do waves do when they pass through a narrow gap? Answer: They spread out.
- momentum-impulse: What is the momentum of a 2 kg trolley moving at 3 m/s? Answer: 6 kg·m/s.
