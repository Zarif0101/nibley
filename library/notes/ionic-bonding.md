# Ionic bonding

## Simple idea
A metal atom gives electrons away and a non-metal atom takes them. Each becomes an ion, an atom with a charge. Opposite charges attract strongly, like magnets, and hold the ions in a neat repeating grid called a lattice. Table salt (NaCl) is the classic example.

## Key formulas
- Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (sodium loses 1 electron, e⁻)
- Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (chlorine gains 1 electron)
- Magnesium chloride: Mg²⁺ with 2Cl⁻ gives MgCl₂
- Total positive charge = total negative charge in the formula

## Common mistakes
- Saying electrons are shared: in ionic bonding they are transferred.
- Forgetting the charges must balance: Mg²⁺ needs two Cl⁻, so the formula is MgCl₂.
- Thinking solid salt conducts: ions only move when melted or dissolved.

## Check questions
1. What ion does sodium (2,8,1 electrons) form?
   Answer: Na⁺, by losing 1 electron.
2. What is the formula of magnesium oxide, from Mg²⁺ and O²⁻?
   Answer: MgO.
3. Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?
   Answer: Strong attraction between oppositely charged ions needs a lot of energy to break.

## Flashcards
Q: What happens to electrons in ionic bonding?
A: They move from a metal atom to a non-metal atom.
Q: What is an ion?
A: An atom with an electric charge.
Q: What is a lattice?
A: A repeating grid of ions.
Q: When does an ionic compound conduct electricity?
A: When molten or dissolved in water.
Q: What charge does a chloride ion have?
A: 1−.
