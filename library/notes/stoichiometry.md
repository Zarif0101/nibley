# Reacting masses and limiting reagents

## Simple idea
A balanced equation is a recipe in moles. If a cake needs 2 eggs for each cup of flour, then the ingredient that runs out first stops the baking. In reactions, that ingredient is the limiting reagent. Convert masses to moles, use the equation's ratio, then convert back.

## Key formulas
- n = m / M. n: moles (mol), m: mass (g), M: molar mass (g/mol)
- 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. Ratio Mg : MgO is 2 : 2, which is 1 : 1
- Steps: mass → moles → ratio from equation → moles of wanted → mass
- Limiting reagent: the reactant that runs out first

## Common mistakes
- Using the masses in the ratio: use the moles, because the equation's numbers count moles.
- Ignoring the big numbers in front of formulas: they give the mole ratio.
- Picking the smaller mass as limiting: compare moles scaled by the equation.

## Check questions
1. In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, how many mol of water form from 3 mol H₂ with plenty of O₂?
   Answer: 3 mol.
2. What mass of MgO forms from 2.4 g Mg in 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (Mg 24, MgO 40 g/mol)?
   Answer: 4.0 g, from 0.10 mol Mg making 0.10 mol MgO.
3. Why does the limiting reagent decide how much product forms?
   Answer: When it is used up, the reaction stops.

## Flashcards
Q: Where do you get the mole ratio?
A: From the big numbers in the balanced equation.
Q: What is a limiting reagent?
A: The reactant that is used up first.
Q: What is an excess reagent?
A: A reactant left over when the reaction stops.
Q: What is the first step in a reacting-mass question?
A: Convert the given mass to moles.
Q: What is the formula for moles from mass?
A: n = m / M.
