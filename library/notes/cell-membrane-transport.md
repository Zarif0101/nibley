# Membranes and transport

## Simple idea
The cell membrane is a thin skin that lets some things through. Smell from a cooking pot spreads across a room: particles move from where there are lots to where there are few. That is diffusion, and it needs no energy. Active transport pushes the other way and does need energy.

## Key formulas
- diffusion: high concentration → low concentration, no energy needed
- osmosis: water moves from dilute solution → concentrated solution, through a partially permeable membrane
- active transport: low concentration → high concentration, uses energy from respiration

## Common mistakes
- Saying osmosis moves any particle: osmosis is only water.
- Thinking diffusion needs energy: it runs downhill by itself.
- Mixing up the direction: active transport goes against the gradient, so it costs energy.

## Check questions
1. Which way do particles move in diffusion?
   Answer: From high concentration to low concentration.
2. A potato slice sits in very salty water. Does it gain or lose water?
   Answer: It loses water, since the water moves toward the saltier side.
3. Why does active transport need energy?
   Answer: It moves particles uphill, from low to high concentration.

## Flashcards
Q: What is diffusion?
A: Net movement of particles from high to low concentration.
Q: What is osmosis?
A: Movement of water across a partially permeable membrane, dilute to concentrated.
Q: What is active transport?
A: Moving particles against the gradient, using energy.
Q: What is the membrane made of?
A: Two layers of phospholipids with proteins in them.
Q: Name a factor that speeds up diffusion.
A: A steeper concentration gradient, or a higher temperature.
