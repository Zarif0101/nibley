# Newton's law of gravitation

## Simple idea
Every object pulls on every other object. You pull on the Earth, and the Earth pulls on you, with equal force. We only feel it with huge masses like planets. The closer two things are, the stronger the pull, and it drops fast: twice as far gives a quarter of the force.

## Key formulas
- F = G·m₁·m₂ / r². F: gravity force (N), G: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg², m₁, m₂: masses (kg), r: distance between centres (m)
- g = G·M / r². g: field strength (N/kg), M: mass of the planet (kg)

## Common mistakes
- Using r instead of r²: the distance must be squared.
- Measuring r from the surface: use the distance between centres.
- Thinking the bigger mass pulls harder: both feel equal forces.

## Check questions
1. If the distance between two masses doubles, what happens to the force?
   Answer: It becomes one quarter.
2. Two 1000 kg masses are 1 m apart. What is the force?
   Answer: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 10⁶ ÷ 1 = 6.67 × 10⁻⁵ N.
3. Why do we not feel the pull from a friend standing nearby?
   Answer: G is tiny and the masses are small, so the force is very small.

## Flashcards
Q: State Newton's law of gravitation in words.
A: Force is proportional to both masses, and falls with distance squared.
Q: What is the value of G?
A: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg².
Q: Distance triples. What happens to gravity force?
A: It becomes one ninth.
Q: What is the force the Earth puts on you called?
A: Your weight.
Q: Is gravity attractive or repulsive?
A: Attractive. It always pulls masses together.
