# Heat engines and the second law

## Simple idea
A hot drink cools in a cold room by itself, but never heats up by itself. A heat engine takes heat from something hot, turns part into useful work, and must throw the rest away to something cooler. A car engine gets hot and wastes heat out of the exhaust, so no engine is 100% efficient.

## Key formulas
- W = Q_hot − Q_cold. W: useful work (joules, J), Q_hot: heat taken from the hot source (J), Q_cold: heat given out to the cold side (J)
- efficiency = W / Q_hot. Efficiency has no unit; times 100 for a percentage

## Picture it
Draw `burner` under `piston`. Arrow from `burner.flame-tip` up into `piston.cylinder-bottom` labelled "Q_hot in".
Arrow up from `piston.rod-end` labelled "useful work W"; arrow out from `piston.gas` labelled "Q_cold wasted".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking waste heat breaks energy conservation: energy is conserved, it just spreads out.
- Efficiency over 100%: it is always less than 1.
- Mixing Q_hot and Q_cold: efficiency uses the heat taken in.

## Check questions
1. An engine takes 500 J and does 100 J of work. What is its efficiency?
   Answer: 100 / 500 = 0.2, or 20%.
2. How much heat does that engine give out to the cold side?
   Answer: 500 − 100 = 400 J.
3. Why can no engine turn all its heat into work?
   Answer: Some heat must always flow out to a colder place.

## Flashcards
Q: Which way does heat flow by itself?
A: From hot to cold.
Q: What does a heat engine do?
A: Turns some heat from a hot source into work.
Q: Formula for efficiency?
A: Work out divided by heat in.
Q: Can efficiency reach 100%?
A: No, some heat is always wasted.
Q: Where does the wasted heat go?
A: To a colder place, the surroundings.

## Root check
- first-law-thermodynamics: A gas gains 80 J of heat and does 30 J of work. Find ΔU. Answer: 50 J.
