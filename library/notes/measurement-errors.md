# Measurement and uncertainty

## Simple idea
No measurement is exact. Measure your desk with a ruler twice and you may get 120.3 cm and 120.5 cm. Uncertainty is the "give or take" you attach to a result, like 120.4 ± 0.2 cm. A smaller give-or-take means a more precise measurement.

## Key formulas
- result = value ± uncertainty. Both have the same unit, like cm
- percentage uncertainty = (uncertainty ÷ value) × 100. Gives a percent (%)
- average = (sum of readings) ÷ (number of readings). Same unit as the readings

## Common mistakes
- Writing a result with no uncertainty: always add ± and the unit.
- Thinking more digits means more accurate: only write digits the ruler can show.
- Mixing up precise and accurate: precise means readings agree, accurate means close to the true value.

## Check questions
1. What does 5.0 ± 0.1 cm mean?
   Answer: The true length is probably between 4.9 cm and 5.1 cm.
2. A length is 20 cm ± 1 cm. What is the percentage uncertainty?
   Answer: 5%.
3. Why do we repeat a measurement and take the average?
   Answer: Random errors push readings both ways, so averaging cancels some of them.

## Flashcards
Q: What is uncertainty?
A: The give-or-take range around a measured value.
Q: What does "precise" mean?
A: Repeated readings are close to each other.
Q: What does "accurate" mean?
A: A reading is close to the true value.
Q: How do you find percentage uncertainty?
A: Uncertainty divided by value, times 100.
Q: What does repeating readings help reduce?
A: Random errors.
