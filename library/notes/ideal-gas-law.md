# Gas laws

## Simple idea
Squeeze a bike pump and the air pushes back harder. Leave a balloon in a hot car and it swells. A gas's pressure, volume and temperature are tied together. Change one and the others adjust. The ideal gas law puts all three into one equation.

## Key formulas
- pV = nRT. p: pressure (pascals, Pa), V: volume (m³), n: amount of gas (moles, mol), R: 8.31 J/(mol·K), T: temperature in kelvin (K)
- p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ when T and n stay fixed (Boyle)
- V/T stays constant when p and n stay fixed (Charles). T(K) = °C + 273

## Common mistakes
- Using °C: always convert to kelvin before using pV = nRT.
- Forgetting units: use m³ and Pa (1 L = 0.001 m³).
- Mixing up Boyle: smaller volume means bigger pressure, not smaller.

## Check questions
1. A gas is squeezed to half its volume at constant temperature. What happens to pressure?
   Answer: It doubles.
2. Roughly what pressure has 1 mol at 300 K in 0.025 m³?
   Answer: p = 8.31 × 300 / 0.025 ≈ 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa.
3. Why must T be in kelvin?
   Answer: Kelvin starts at true zero, so V and p stay proportional to T.

## Flashcards
Q: Write the ideal gas law.
A: pV = nRT.
Q: Value of R?
A: 8.31 J/(mol·K).
Q: Boyle's law in words?
A: At fixed temperature, pressure times volume is constant.
Q: 27 °C in kelvin?
A: 300 K.
Q: Volume constant, temperature doubles in kelvin. Pressure?
A: It doubles.
