# Electric potential and voltage

## Simple idea
Lifting a ball up a shelf gives it energy; the higher the shelf, the more energy. Charge works the same way. Potential is the energy each coulomb has at a point. Voltage is the difference between two points, like the height between two shelves. A 9 V battery gives each coulomb 9 joules.

## Key formulas
- V = W / q. V: voltage (volts, V), W: energy or work done (joules, J), q: charge (coulombs, C)
- V = k·Q / r. Potential of a point charge. Q: source charge (C), r: distance (m), k ≈ 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
- ΔE = q·ΔV. ΔE: energy change (J), q: charge (C), ΔV: voltage difference (V)

## Common mistakes
- Thinking voltage is energy: voltage is energy per coulomb.
- Using potential at one point as if it were voltage: voltage is a difference between two points.
- Forgetting the charge: energy gained = q·ΔV, not just ΔV.

## Check questions
1. 12 J of work moves 3 C across a battery. What is the voltage?
   Answer: V = 12 / 3 = 4 V.
2. An electron crosses 100 V. How much energy does it gain?
   Answer: 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 100 = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ J.
3. Why does a positive charge speed up when it moves toward lower potential?
   Answer: It loses electric energy, which becomes kinetic energy.

## Flashcards
Q: What is one volt?
A: One joule of energy per coulomb.
Q: What does potential difference mean?
A: The change in energy per coulomb between two points.
Q: What is the formula for energy gained by charge q across ΔV?
A: ΔE = q·ΔV.
Q: How does potential of a point charge change with distance?
A: It falls as 1/r, so it drops further away.
Q: Which way does a positive charge move on its own?
A: From high potential to low potential.
