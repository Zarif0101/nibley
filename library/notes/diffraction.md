# Diffraction

## Simple idea
You can hear someone talking around a corner but cannot see them. Sound waves spread round the corner; light spreads far less. Waves spread out when they pass through a gap or round an edge. The spreading is biggest when the gap is about the same size as the wavelength.

## Key formulas
- a·sinθ = λ. First dark spot beside the centre of a single slit. a: slit width (metres, m), θ: angle to first dark spot (degrees), λ: wavelength (m)

## Picture it
Draw `single-slit`. Straight lines at `single-slit.waves-in` labelled "straight waves", curved arcs at `single-slit.waves-out` labelled "waves spread out". Label `single-slit.gap` "gap about one wavelength".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking only light diffracts: all waves do, including sound and water.
- Thinking a wider gap spreads more: a narrower gap spreads waves more.
- Mixing it up with refraction: diffraction is spreading, refraction is bending from a speed change.

## Check questions
1. Does a narrower or a wider gap spread waves more?
   Answer: A narrower gap.
2. λ = 500 nm and slit width a = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m. What is the angle to the first dark spot?
   Answer: sin θ = 0.5, so θ = 30°.
3. Why do we hear round corners but not see round them?
   Answer: Sound wavelengths are metres long, much bigger than light's, so sound spreads far more.

## Flashcards
Q: What is diffraction?
A: Waves spreading out through a gap or round an edge.
Q: When is diffraction strongest?
A: When the gap is about the same size as the wavelength.
Q: What pattern does a single slit make?
A: A wide bright centre with dimmer stripes either side.
Q: What happens to the spread if wavelength increases?
A: The waves spread out more.
Q: Which diffracts more, red or blue light?
A: Red, it has the longer wavelength.

## Root check
- interference: Crest meets trough. What happens to the wave? Answer: They cancel, and you get a small or zero wave.
