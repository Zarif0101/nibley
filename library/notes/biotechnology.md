# Biotechnology and genetic engineering

## Simple idea
Scientists can copy, sort and move DNA, like photocopying and cutting pages. PCR makes millions of copies of a small piece of DNA. Gel electrophoresis sorts DNA pieces by size, as smaller pieces move further. Genetic engineering moves a gene into another organism, like putting the insulin gene into bacteria.

## Key formulas
- PCR: each cycle doubles the DNA (1 → 2 → 4 → 8 ...)
- Number of copies after n cycles = 2ⁿ (n: number of cycles)
- Gel electrophoresis: DNA is negative, so it moves toward the positive end; smaller pieces move further

## Common mistakes
- Thinking PCR makes protein: it only copies DNA.
- Saying big DNA pieces travel furthest in a gel: small pieces travel further.
- Thinking genes cannot cross species: enzymes cut and paste a gene, and all life shares the DNA code.

## Check questions
1. What does PCR do?
   Answer: It makes many copies of a piece of DNA.
2. After 3 PCR cycles, how many copies come from one DNA piece?
   Answer: 8.
3. Why does small DNA travel further in a gel?
   Answer: It squeezes through the gel's tiny gaps more easily.

## Flashcards
Q: What does PCR stand for?
A: Polymerase chain reaction.
Q: What do restriction enzymes do?
A: They cut DNA at specific sequences.
Q: What is a plasmid?
A: A small ring of DNA in bacteria.
Q: Which charge does DNA carry in a gel?
A: Negative.
Q: Which protein is made by engineered bacteria for diabetics?
A: Insulin.
