# Rates of reaction

## Simple idea
Think of a crowded dance floor. More people and faster moving means more bumps. Reactions need particles to bump into each other hard enough. More collisions, or harder collisions, make a faster reaction. Heat, concentration and surface area all speed things up.

## Key formulas
- rate = change in concentration ÷ time. Concentration: mol/dm³; time: s; rate: mol/dm³/s
- Speeding up: higher temperature, higher concentration, more surface area, a catalyst

## Picture it
Draw `gas-box` and add an arrow at `gas-box.particle` labelled "collision". Draw `conical-flask` with a label at `conical-flask.liquid-surface` "bubbles of gas: rate".

## Common mistakes
- Saying particles collide more only with heat: heat also gives more energy per collision.
- Thinking every collision causes a reaction: only collisions with enough energy do.
- Mixing up powder with whole lumps: powder has more surface area, so it is faster.

## Check questions
1. Concentration rises by 0.30 mol/dm³ in 60 s. What is the average rate?
   Answer: 0.005 mol/dm³/s.
2. Why does powdered marble react faster than a lump?
   Answer: More surface is exposed, so more collisions per second.
3. Why does a warmer solution react faster?
   Answer: Particles move faster, collide more often and with more energy.

## Flashcards
Q: What do particles need to do to react?
A: Collide with enough energy.
Q: Name three ways to speed up a reaction.
A: Heat it, raise concentration, or crush the solid.
Q: What is the unit of reaction rate?
A: mol/dm³/s.
Q: Why does higher concentration speed things up?
A: More particles per volume, so more collisions.
Q: Does a catalyst get used up?
A: No, it is not used up.

## Root check
- particles-matter: Are gas particles close together or far apart? Answer: Far apart, and moving fast.
- concentration-solutions: 0.5 mol is dissolved in 2 dm³. What is the concentration? Answer: 0.25 mol/dm³.
