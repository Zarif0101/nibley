# Electrolysis

## Simple idea
A battery can push a reaction that would never happen by itself. Melt some table salt and pass a current through it. The battery pulls electrons out of one electrode and pushes them onto the other, which splits the salt into sodium metal and chlorine gas.

## Key formulas
- Q = I·t. Q: charge (coulombs, C), I: current (amperes, A), t: time (seconds, s)
- n(e⁻) = Q / F. n: moles of electrons (mol), F: Faraday constant, 96 500 C/mol
- Cathode (reduction): Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na
- Anode (oxidation): 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up which electrode is which: oxidation is always at the anode, reduction at the cathode.
- Forgetting ions must be free to move: a solid salt will not conduct, a melt or solution will.
- Putting time in minutes: change to seconds before Q = I·t.

## Check questions
1. In the electrolysis of molten sodium chloride, what forms at the cathode?
   Answer: Sodium metal.
2. How many moles of electrons carry 9650 C?
   Answer: 9650 / 96 500 = 0.10 mol.
3. Why will solid sodium chloride not electrolyse?
   Answer: Its ions are locked in place and cannot move to the electrodes.

## Flashcards
Q: What happens at the anode?
A: Oxidation: ions lose electrons.
Q: What happens at the cathode?
A: Reduction: ions gain electrons.
Q: What is an electrolyte?
A: A melted or dissolved compound that conducts by moving ions.
Q: What does 96 500 C/mol stand for?
A: The Faraday constant: charge of one mole of electrons.
Q: Which ions move towards the cathode?
A: Positive ions (cations).

## Root check
- redox: Which species is reduced in Cu²⁺ + Zn → Cu + Zn²⁺? Answer: Cu²⁺ (it gains electrons).
- electric-current: How much charge flows when 3 A runs for 10 s? Answer: 30 C.
