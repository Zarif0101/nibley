# Polynomials and the factor theorem

## Simple idea
A polynomial is an expression built from powers of x added together, like x³ − 2x² + 5. The highest power is its degree. The factor theorem is a shortcut: put a number into the polynomial, and if you get zero, then you have found a factor.

## Key formulas
- p(x): a polynomial in x. Its degree is the highest power of x
- Factor theorem: if p(a) = 0, then (x − a) is a factor of p(x)
- Remainder theorem: dividing p(x) by (x − a) leaves remainder p(a)
- A polynomial of degree n has at most n roots

## Common mistakes
- Getting the sign wrong: factor (x + 3) means the root is −3.
- Testing p(a) with the wrong sign: for (x − 2), test x = 2.
- Expecting more roots than the degree: a cubic has at most 3.

## Check questions
1. Is (x − 2) a factor of x² − 5x + 6?
   Answer: Yes, p(2) = 4 − 10 + 6 = 0.
2. What is the remainder when x³ + 2x + 1 is divided by (x − 1)?
   Answer: 4, since p(1) = 1 + 2 + 1.
3. Why does p(a) = 0 mean (x − a) is a factor?
   Answer: The remainder is zero, so (x − a) divides in exactly.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the degree of a polynomial?
A: Its highest power of x.
Q: State the factor theorem.
A: If p(a) = 0, then (x − a) is a factor.
Q: What is the remainder when p(x) is divided by (x − a)?
A: p(a).
Q: What is the most roots a cubic can have?
A: Three.
Q: If (x + 4) is a factor, what is a root?
A: x = −4.
