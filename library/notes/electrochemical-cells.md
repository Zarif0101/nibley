# Electrochemical cells

## Simple idea
A battery is a controlled chemical reaction. One metal wants to give away electrons and another ion wants to take them. If we keep them in separate beakers and join them with a wire, the electrons must travel through the wire, and that moving charge is the current that lights a bulb.

## Key formulas
- E°cell = E°(reduction electrode) − E°(oxidation electrode). E°: standard electrode potential (volts, V)
- Positive E°cell: the reaction works by itself
- Oxidation (losing electrons) happens at the anode, reduction (gaining electrons) at the cathode
- Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu

## Common mistakes
- Swapping the two electrodes in the subtraction: take reduction electrode minus oxidation electrode.
- Saying electrons flow through the salt bridge: electrons use the wire, ions use the salt bridge.
- Doubling E° when you double the equation: voltage does not scale with amount.

## Check questions
1. At which electrode does oxidation happen?
   Answer: The anode.
2. E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V and E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = −0.76 V. Find E°cell.
   Answer: 0.34 − (−0.76) = +1.10 V.
3. Why is a salt bridge needed?
   Answer: Ions flowing through it keep charge balanced so the circuit stays complete.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a half-cell?
A: One electrode in a solution of its own ions.
Q: Which way do electrons flow in the wire?
A: From the oxidation electrode to the reduction electrode.
Q: What does a positive E°cell mean?
A: The reaction can happen by itself.
Q: What does a salt bridge carry?
A: Ions, not electrons.
Q: Reduction happens at which electrode?
A: The cathode.

## Root check
- redox: In oxidation, are electrons lost or gained? Answer: Lost.
- electric-potential: What is the unit of potential difference? Answer: The volt (V).
