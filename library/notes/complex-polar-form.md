# Complex numbers in polar form

## Simple idea
A complex number a + bi can be drawn as a point: a across, b up. Instead of "across and up", you can say how far from the centre and which way to turn. Like giving a treasure location as "10 paces at 40 degrees". That distance and angle are the polar form.

## Key formulas
- z = r(cos θ + i sin θ). r: distance from the origin, θ: angle from the positive real axis (radians or degrees)
- r = |z| = √(a² + b²), where z = a + bi. a: real part, b: imaginary part
- tan θ = b/a. Check which quadrant z is in
- To multiply: multiply the r's and add the θ's

## Common mistakes
- Using tan θ = b/a without checking the quadrant: tan repeats every 180°.
- Forgetting the square root in r: r = √(a² + b²).
- Mixing degrees and radians: stay in one.

## Check questions
1. Find r and θ for 1 + i.
   Answer: r = √2, θ = π/4 (45°).
2. Write −2 in polar form.
   Answer: 2(cos π + i sin π).
3. Why check the quadrant after using tan θ?
   Answer: Two opposite directions give the same tan, so the calculator can pick the wrong one.

## Flashcards
Q: What is r called for a complex number?
A: The modulus, its distance from the origin.
Q: What is θ called?
A: The argument, the angle from the positive real axis.
Q: Find |3 + 4i|.
A: 5.
Q: What happens to the angles when you multiply?
A: They add.
Q: What is i in polar form?
A: cos 90° + i sin 90°, so r = 1 and θ = 90°.

## Root check
- complex-numbers: What is i²? Answer: −1.
- trig-functions: What is sin 90°? Answer: 1.
