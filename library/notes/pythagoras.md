# Pythagoras' theorem

## Simple idea
A right triangle has one square corner. The longest side, across from that corner, is the hypotenuse. Square the two short sides and add them: you get the square of the long side. Check with 3, 4, 5: 9 + 16 = 25, and 5 × 5 = 25.

## Key formulas
- a² + b² = c². a, b: the short sides, c: the hypotenuse (all in the same unit)
- c = √(a² + b²) finds the long side
- a = √(c² − b²) finds a short side

## Picture it
Draw `right-triangle`. Label `right-triangle.base-mid` "a = 3", `right-triangle.side-mid` "b = 4" and `right-triangle.hypotenuse-mid` "c = ?".
Mark `right-triangle.right-angle` with a small square.

## Common mistakes
- Adding the sides instead of the squares: 3 + 4 is not 5 here, use 9 + 16.
- Stopping at 25: take the square root to get 5.
- Using it on a triangle with no right angle: it only works with one.

## Check questions
1. Which side is the hypotenuse?
   Answer: The longest side, opposite the right angle.
2. Short sides are 6 and 8. How long is the hypotenuse?
   Answer: 10.
3. Why is the hypotenuse always the longest side?
   Answer: c² is a² plus b², so it is bigger than either square.

## Flashcards
Q: State Pythagoras' theorem.
A: a² + b² = c², with c the hypotenuse.
Q: Which triangles does it work for?
A: Right-angled triangles only.
Q: What is the missing side when c = 13 and b = 5?
A: 12.
Q: Name a whole-number triple like 3, 4, 5.
A: 5, 12, 13.
Q: How do you find a short side?
A: Take c² minus b², then the square root.
