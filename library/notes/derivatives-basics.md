# The derivative as slope

## Simple idea
On a straight road the slope is the same everywhere. On a curved hill it changes. The derivative is the slope of the curve at one exact spot: how steeply it rises right there. Take two close points, find the slope between them, then slide them together. The limit of that slope is the derivative.

## Key formulas
- f′(x) = lim (h→0) [f(x + h) − f(x)] / h. f′(x): the slope at x, h: a tiny step across
- d/dx (xⁿ) = n·xⁿ⁻¹. n: any number
- d/dx (c) = 0 and d/dx (kx) = k. c, k: fixed numbers

## Common mistakes
- Using the slope between two far apart points: the derivative is at one point, so let h go to 0.
- Forgetting to lower the power: the derivative of x³ is 3x², not 3x³.
- Saying the derivative of a constant is the constant: it is 0.

## Check questions
1. What is the derivative of x³?
   Answer: 3x².
2. What is the slope of y = x² at x = 3?
   Answer: The derivative is 2x, so 6.
3. Why is the derivative of 5x equal to 5?
   Answer: It is a straight line with slope 5, the same everywhere.

## Flashcards
Q: What does the derivative measure?
A: The slope of a curve at one point.
Q: What is the derivative of xⁿ?
A: n·xⁿ⁻¹.
Q: What is the derivative of a constant?
A: 0.
Q: What line just touches a curve at one point?
A: The tangent.
Q: What is the derivative of x²?
A: 2x.

## Root check
- limits: What is the limit as x→3 of 2x + 1? Answer: 7.
- straight-lines: What is the slope of y = −2x + 5? Answer: −2.
