# Human reproduction

## Simple idea
A new human starts when a sperm cell joins an egg cell, called fertilisation. Each carries half the chromosomes, so the joined cell has a full set. The fertilised egg grows into an embryo in the uterus. Hormones control the monthly menstrual cycle that prepares the body for pregnancy.

## Key formulas
- Sperm (23 chromosomes) + egg (23 chromosomes) → zygote (46 chromosomes)
- Menstrual cycle is about 28 days, with ovulation near day 14
- Hormones: FSH grows the egg, oestrogen builds the uterus lining, LH triggers ovulation, progesterone keeps the lining

## Picture it
Draw `cell-animal` as the egg, with `cell-animal.nucleus` labelled "23 chromosomes". Arrow from outside to `cell-animal.membrane` labelled "sperm joins egg". Write "fertilisation" beside it.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking sperm and egg have 46 chromosomes: they have 23, made by meiosis.
- Mixing up fertilisation and ovulation: ovulation releases the egg, fertilisation joins sperm and egg.
- Thinking the cycle is exactly 28 days for everyone: it varies from person to person.

## Check questions
1. What is fertilisation?
   Answer: A sperm and an egg join.
2. How many chromosomes are in a human zygote?
   Answer: 46.
3. Why are gametes made by meiosis, not mitosis?
   Answer: Meiosis halves the chromosomes so fertilisation restores the full number.

## Flashcards
Q: Where are sperm made?
A: In the testes.
Q: Where are eggs released from?
A: The ovaries.
Q: What is ovulation?
A: The release of an egg from an ovary.
Q: Where does the embryo grow?
A: In the uterus.
Q: Which hormone keeps the uterus lining during pregnancy?
A: Progesterone.

## Root check
- meiosis: How many cells does meiosis make from one? Answer: Four, each with half the chromosomes.
- hormones-homeostasis: What carries hormones around the body? Answer: The blood.
