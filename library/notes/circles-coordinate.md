# Equation of a circle

## Simple idea
A circle is every point that sits the same distance from a centre, like the end of a string pinned down while you swing it round. Pythagoras measures that distance: across squared plus up squared equals the radius squared. That gives the circle's equation.

## Key formulas
- (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r². (a, b): centre, r: radius (same units as x and y)
- x² + y² = r². Circle centred at the origin (0, 0)
- A point (x, y) is on the circle if it makes the equation true

## Picture it
Draw `circle` on `axes`. Label `circle.center` "centre (a, b)", `circle.radius-mid` "radius r" and `circle.edge-right` "point (x, y)".
Label `axes.x-end` "x" and `axes.y-end` "y".

## Common mistakes
- Using the wrong sign for the centre: (x − 2) means centre x = +2, and (y + 3) means centre y = −3.
- Reading r² as the radius: take the square root, so 16 means r = 4.
- Forgetting to square r when writing the equation.

## Check questions
1. Write the circle with centre (0, 0) and radius 5.
   Answer: x² + y² = 25.
2. Is (3, 4) on x² + y² = 25?
   Answer: Yes, 9 + 16 = 25.
3. Why do the signs flip: (x − 2)² gives a centre at x = +2?
   Answer: The bracket is zero when x = 2, which is the centre's x-value.

## Flashcards
Q: Equation of a circle with centre (a, b), radius r?
A: (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r².
Q: Equation of a circle centred at the origin?
A: x² + y² = r².
Q: Centre of (x − 1)² + (y + 4)² = 9?
A: (1, −4).
Q: Radius of (x − 1)² + (y + 4)² = 9?
A: 3.
Q: Which theorem gives the circle equation?
A: Pythagoras.

## Root check
- straight-lines: Find the slope of the line through (0, 1) and (2, 5). Answer: 2.
- pythagoras: A right triangle has legs 3 and 4. How long is the hypotenuse? Answer: 5.
