# Lenz's law

## Simple idea
Nature dislikes sudden changes. When the magnetic field through a coil changes, the coil makes a current whose own field fights that change. Push a magnet toward a coil and the coil pushes back. Pull it away and the coil tries to hold it. You always have to do work, and that work becomes electrical energy.

## Key formulas
- ε = −N·ΔΦ / Δt. ε: induced voltage (volts, V), N: turns, ΔΦ: flux change (webers, Wb), Δt: time (seconds, s). The minus sign is Lenz's law: it opposes the change

## Picture it
Draw `solenoid` with `magnet` moving toward `solenoid.left-lead`. Arrow from `magnet.north-end` labelled "north pole in".
Label `solenoid.inside`: "coil makes north here, pushes back". Label `solenoid.right-lead`: "induced current".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the induced field opposes the magnet itself: it opposes the change in flux.
- Thinking it stops all flux: it only slows the change.
- Forgetting it is energy conservation: pushing against the coil is where the energy comes from.

## Check questions
1. You push a north pole toward a coil. Does the coil end nearest it act as north or south?
   Answer: North, so it repels the magnet.
2. You pull the magnet away. What does the near end of the coil do?
   Answer: It acts as south and attracts the magnet, resisting the pull.
3. Why can the induced current not help the change?
   Answer: It would create energy from nothing, which breaks conservation of energy.

## Flashcards
Q: State Lenz's law.
A: Induced current flows to oppose the change that caused it.
Q: What does the minus sign in Faraday's law mean?
A: The induced voltage opposes the flux change.
Q: Which law of physics does Lenz's law match?
A: Conservation of energy.
Q: Magnet falls through a copper tube. What happens to its fall?
A: It falls more slowly.
Q: Does Lenz's law set the size of the voltage?
A: No, it gives direction; Faraday's law gives size.

## Root check
- electromagnetic-induction: A 50-turn coil's flux changes by 0.04 Wb in 0.2 s. What is the voltage? Answer: 10 V.
- conservation-energy: Can a machine give out more energy than it takes in? Answer: No; energy only changes form.
