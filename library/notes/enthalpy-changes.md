# Enthalpy changes

## Simple idea
Reactions swap heat with their surroundings. A hand warmer gives out heat, so the surroundings get hotter: that is exothermic. A cold pack takes in heat, so it feels cold: that is endothermic. ΔH is the heat change measured per mole of reaction.

## Key formulas
- ΔH = H(products) − H(reactants). ΔH: enthalpy change (kJ/mol)
- ΔH negative: exothermic. ΔH positive: endothermic
- q = m·c·ΔT. q: heat (J), m: mass of water (g), c: 4.18 J/(g·°C), ΔT: temperature change (°C)
- Breaking bonds takes in energy. Making bonds gives out energy

## Common mistakes
- Getting the sign backwards: exothermic means ΔH is negative, as the system loses energy.
- Using J and kJ together: divide J by 1000 to get kJ.
- Using the mass of the solid in q = m·c·ΔT: use the mass of the water that was heated.

## Check questions
1. A reaction has ΔH = −90 kJ/mol. Exothermic or endothermic?
   Answer: Exothermic, because ΔH is negative.
2. 100 g of water warms by 5.0 °C. How much heat did it take in?
   Answer: 2090 J, since 100 × 4.18 × 5.0 = 2090.
3. Why does the temperature of the surroundings rise in an exothermic reaction?
   Answer: The reaction gives out heat to the surroundings.

## Flashcards
Q: What does exothermic mean?
A: The reaction gives out heat to the surroundings.
Q: What is the sign of ΔH for an endothermic reaction?
A: Positive.
Q: What is the unit of ΔH?
A: kJ/mol.
Q: What is the formula for heat from temperature change?
A: q = m·c·ΔT.
Q: Does breaking bonds take in or give out energy?
A: It takes in energy.
