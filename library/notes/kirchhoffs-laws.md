# Kirchhoff's laws

## Simple idea
Two simple rules for any circuit. First, charge is not lost: at a junction, what flows in is what flows out, like water at a pipe split. Second, energy is not lost: go once round any loop and the battery's gains cancel the drops across the resistors, so the total is zero.

## Key formulas
- ΣI(in) = ΣI(out). I: current (amperes, A) at a junction
- ΣV = 0 around a loop. V: voltage rises (battery) count +, drops (resistors, V = I·R) count −, all in volts (V)

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up signs in loops: count rises as + and drops as −, going one way.
- Guessing current directions are fixed: if your answer is negative, the real direction is opposite.
- Forgetting a branch at a junction: list every wire touching it.

## Check questions
1. 5 A flows into a junction. 2 A leaves one wire. What leaves the other wire?
   Answer: 3 A.
2. A 9 V battery drives a loop with two resistors. One drops 4 V. How much does the other drop?
   Answer: 5 V.
3. Why must the current into a junction equal the current out?
   Answer: Charge cannot pile up or vanish there.

## Flashcards
Q: State the junction rule.
A: Total current in equals total current out.
Q: State the loop rule.
A: Voltages around any closed loop add up to zero.
Q: The junction rule comes from conservation of what?
A: Charge.
Q: The loop rule comes from conservation of what?
A: Energy.
Q: What does a negative current in your answer mean?
A: The real current flows the opposite way to your arrow.

## Root check
- series-parallel: Two 10 Ω resistors are in parallel. What is the total resistance? Answer: 5 Ω.
