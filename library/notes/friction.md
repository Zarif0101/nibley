# Friction

## Simple idea
Slide a heavy box across a carpet and it fights back. That resisting force is friction. It is bigger on rough surfaces and when the surfaces press together harder. A box on ice slides easily because ice is slippery. Friction always points against the sliding.

## Key formulas
- f = μ·N. f: friction force (newtons, N), μ: friction coefficient (no unit), N: normal force (N)
- N = m·g on flat ground. m: mass (kg), g: 9.8 m/s²
- net force = push − f. Forces in N

## Picture it
Draw `block` on `ground`. Arrow from `block.right` pointing right labelled "push". Arrow from `block.left` pointing left labelled "friction f". Arrow from `block.top` pointing up labelled "normal force N".

## Common mistakes
- Pointing friction along the motion: it points against the motion.
- Thinking friction depends on contact area: it depends on the surfaces and N.
- Using mass instead of N: use f = μ·N, with N = m·g on flat ground.

## Check questions
1. A box has N = 50 N and μ = 0.4. What is the friction?
   Answer: 20 N.
2. A 3 kg box has a net force of 12 N. What is its acceleration?
   Answer: 4 m/s².
3. Why is it harder to push a heavier box?
   Answer: More weight means bigger N, so f = μ·N is bigger.

## Flashcards
Q: Which way does friction point?
A: Against the sliding motion.
Q: Formula for friction?
A: f = μ·N.
Q: What is μ?
A: The friction coefficient, how grippy the surfaces are.
Q: Does μ have a unit?
A: No, it is just a number.
Q: What does N stand for here?
A: The normal force, the push of the surface on the object.

## Root check
- newtons-second-law: A 3 kg trolley has a net force of 12 N. What is its acceleration? Answer: 4 m/s².
