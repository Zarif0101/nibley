# Atomic energy levels and spectra

## Simple idea
Think of a staircase: you can stand on a step but not between steps. An electron in an atom can only have certain energies, the steps. When it drops to a lower step, it gives out a photon. The step gap sets the colour. Each element has its own set of colours.

## Key formulas
- hf = E₂ − E₁. h: 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, f: photon frequency (hertz, Hz), E₂, E₁: upper and lower energies (joules, J or eV)
- Eₙ = −13.6 / n² eV. Hydrogen only. n: level number (1, 2, 3…), E: energy (eV)
- 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

## Common mistakes
- Thinking electrons can have any energy: only the set levels are allowed.
- Thinking a jump up gives out light: going up takes in energy, going down gives out a photon.
- Forgetting the negative sign: lower levels are more negative, ground state is −13.6 eV.

## Check questions
1. A hydrogen electron drops from −3.4 eV to −13.6 eV. What is the photon's energy?
   Answer: 10.2 eV.
2. Is the photon given out when an electron goes up or down a level?
   Answer: Down.
3. Why does each element give its own pattern of coloured lines?
   Answer: Each element has its own energy levels, so its jumps give its own photon energies.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the ground state?
A: The lowest energy level of an atom.
Q: What is an excited atom?
A: One with an electron in a higher level than the ground state.
Q: What is an emission spectrum?
A: Bright coloured lines from photons given out by atoms.
Q: What is an absorption spectrum?
A: Dark lines where atoms took photons out of white light.
Q: What sets the colour of a photon given out?
A: The energy gap between the two levels.

## Root check
- photoelectric-effect: What is the energy of a photon of frequency f? Answer: E = h·f.
