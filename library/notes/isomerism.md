# Isomerism

## Simple idea
Take the same set of Lego bricks and build two different shapes. Isomers are like that: the same atoms, joined in different ways. They have the same molecular formula but different structures, so they can have different properties, like boiling point.

## Key formulas
- C₄H₁₀ has two structural isomers: butane (straight chain) and methylpropane (branched). Same formula, different layout
- Structural isomers: same formula, atoms joined in a different order

## Picture it
Draw `carbon-chain` twice. First as a plain chain, labelled "butane". Second with the branch at `carbon-chain.group`, labelled "methylpropane". Draw `tetrahedral-molecule` and label `tetrahedral-molecule.bond-wedge` "bond towards you".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking isomers have different formulas: the formula is always the same.
- Counting a flipped or turned drawing as a new isomer: check how atoms are joined.
- Forgetting 3D: bonds around carbon point in a tetrahedral shape.

## Check questions
1. Do butane and methylpropane have the same molecular formula?
   Answer: Yes, both are C₄H₁₀.
2. How many structural isomers does C₄H₁₀ have?
   Answer: Two.
3. Why can isomers have different boiling points?
   Answer: Their shapes differ, so the forces between molecules differ.

## Flashcards
Q: What are isomers?
A: Molecules with the same molecular formula but different arrangements.
Q: What is a structural isomer?
A: Same formula, but atoms joined in a different order.
Q: How many carbons does pentane have?
A: Five.
Q: What is the bond angle around a carbon with four single bonds?
A: About 109.5°.
Q: Do isomers always have identical properties?
A: No, their properties can differ.
