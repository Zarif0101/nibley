# pH

## Simple idea
pH is a number that says how acidic a liquid is. Think of it like a volume dial for H⁺ ions, but backwards: the lower the pH, the more acidic. Water sits in the middle at 7. Each step down is ten times more H⁺.

## Key formulas
- pH = −log[H⁺]. [H⁺]: hydrogen ion concentration (mol/dm³)
- [H⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ. Same rule turned around
- Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C. [OH⁻]: hydroxide concentration (mol/dm³)
- pH + pOH = 14 at 25 °C

## Picture it
Draw `ph-scale`. Label `ph-scale.acid-end` "pH 0 acidic", `ph-scale.neutral` "pH 7 neutral" and `ph-scale.base-end` "pH 14 basic". Add an arrow below pointing left, labelled "more H⁺".

## Common mistakes
- Dropping the minus sign: pH = −log[H⁺], so 0.001 gives 3, not −3.
- Thinking pH 4 is a bit stronger than pH 5: it is ten times more H⁺.
- Using pH = −log[OH⁻] for bases: find pOH first, then do 14 − pOH.

## Check questions
1. What is the pH when [H⁺] = 0.001 mol/dm³?
   Answer: 3.
2. What is the pH of 0.01 mol/dm³ NaOH?
   Answer: [OH⁻] = 0.01, pOH = 2, so pH = 12.
3. Why does a pH 3 drink have ten times more H⁺ than a pH 4 drink?
   Answer: pH is a log scale, so each step is a factor of 10.

## Flashcards
Q: Write the formula for pH.
A: pH = −log[H⁺].
Q: What pH is neutral at 25 °C?
A: 7.
Q: What is the value of Kw at 25 °C?
A: 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴.
Q: Is pH 10 acidic or basic?
A: Basic.
Q: How much more H⁺ is in pH 2 than pH 3?
A: Ten times more.

## Root check
- acids-bases: What ion does an acid give away in water? Answer: The H⁺ ion.
- logarithms: What is log₁₀(1000)? Answer: 3.
