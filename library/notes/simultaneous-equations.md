# Simultaneous equations

## Simple idea
Two clues about two unknown numbers. Say x + y = 5 and x − y = 1. Add the equations: the y parts cancel and 2x = 6, so x = 3. Then y = 2. On a graph each equation is a straight line, and the answer is the point where the lines cross.

## Key formulas
- Elimination: add or subtract the equations to remove one letter
- Substitution: write y = … from one equation, put it into the other
- x and y: the two unknown numbers. Check by trying both values in both equations

## Picture it
Draw `axes`. Draw two straight lines across it, labelled "x + y = 5" and "x − y = 1". Put a dot where they cross, labelled "solution (3, 2)".
Beside it, label `graph-line.line-mid` "one equation = one line".

## Common mistakes
- Stopping after finding x: put it back to find y too.
- Sign slips when subtracting: (x + y) − (x − y) = 2y.
- Not checking: try both values in both equations.

## Check questions
1. What does the crossing point of the two lines give?
   Answer: The x and y that work in both equations.
2. If x + y = 5 and x − y = 1, what are x and y?
   Answer: x = 3, y = 2.
3. Why do two unknowns need two equations?
   Answer: One equation has many answer pairs; the second narrows it to one.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a solution of simultaneous equations?
A: Values that make both equations true at once.
Q: What does elimination mean?
A: Add or subtract equations so one letter disappears.
Q: x + y = 10 and x − y = 2. What is x?
A: 6.
Q: In that same pair, what is y?
A: 4.
Q: How do you check your answer?
A: Put both values into both equations.
