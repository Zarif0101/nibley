# Conic sections

## Simple idea
Slice a cone with a flat knife at different angles and you get different curves. Level cut: circle. Slightly tilted: ellipse, an oval. Parallel to the slope of the cone: parabola, like a thrown ball's path. Steeper still, through both halves: hyperbola, two separate curves.

## Key formulas
- Circle: x² + y² = r². r: radius
- Parabola: y = ax² + bx + c. a, b, c: numbers (a ≠ 0)
- Ellipse: x²/a² + y²/b² = 1. a, b: half-widths along x and y
- Hyperbola: xy = k, so y = k/x. k: a fixed number (k ≠ 0)
- Ellipse: distances from any point on it to its two foci add to a constant

## Common mistakes
- Calling every oval a circle: a circle has one centre and one radius, an ellipse has two foci.
- Thinking y = k/x touches the axes: they are asymptotes, lines it only gets closer to.
- Mixing the minus in hyperbola x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 with the plus in the ellipse.

## Check questions
1. Where does y = x² − 4 cross the x-axis?
   Answer: x = 2 and x = −2.
2. For x²/9 + y²/4 = 1, where does it cross the x-axis?
   Answer: x = 3 and x = −3.
3. Why does y = 1/x never touch the axes?
   Answer: x times y is 1, so neither can be 0.

## Flashcards
Q: Which curve comes from a level cut of a cone?
A: A circle.
Q: Name the curve with two foci whose distances add to a constant.
A: An ellipse.
Q: What shape is y = x²?
A: A parabola.
Q: Which conic has two separate branches?
A: The hyperbola.
Q: Equation of a hyperbola with k = 1?
A: y = 1/x.

## Root check
