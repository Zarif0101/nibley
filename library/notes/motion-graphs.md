# Motion graphs

## Simple idea
A graph is a picture of a journey. On a position-time graph, a steep line means moving fast and a flat line means standing still. Think of a bus route chart. On a velocity-time graph, a rising line means speeding up. The slopes and areas hold the numbers.

## Key formulas
- velocity = slope of position-time graph = rise ÷ run = Δx ÷ t. Δx: change in position (m), t: time (s)
- acceleration = slope of velocity-time graph = Δv ÷ t. Δv: change in velocity (m/s), t: time (s)
- displacement = area under velocity-time graph. Area in m, from m/s × s

## Picture it
Draw `axes` with "time (s)" at `axes.x-end` and "position (m)" at `axes.y-end`. Draw `graph-line` rising; label `graph-line.line-mid` "steady speed" and `graph-line.rise-mid` "slope = velocity".

## Common mistakes
- Reading a position-time slope as acceleration: that slope is velocity.
- Thinking a flat velocity-time line means stopped: it means steady velocity, not zero.
- Forgetting area: displacement is the area under the velocity-time line.

## Check questions
1. A position-time line rises 20 m in 4 s. What is the velocity?
   Answer: 5 m/s.
2. A velocity-time graph is flat at 3 m/s for 5 s. How far does it go?
   Answer: 15 m.
3. Why does a flat line on a position-time graph mean standing still?
   Answer: Position does not change, so velocity is zero.

## Flashcards
Q: What does the slope of a position-time graph give?
A: Velocity.
Q: What does the slope of a velocity-time graph give?
A: Acceleration.
Q: What does the area under a velocity-time graph give?
A: Displacement.
Q: What does a flat line on a velocity-time graph show?
A: Constant velocity, so zero acceleration.
Q: What does a curved position-time line show?
A: Changing velocity, so acceleration.

## Root check
- acceleration: A car goes from 4 m/s to 10 m/s in 3 s. What is its acceleration? Answer: 2 m/s².
- graphs-and-slope: A line rises 6 up over 3 across. What is its slope? Answer: 2.
