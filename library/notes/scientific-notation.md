# Scientific notation and significant figures

## Simple idea
Writing 3 000 000 000 means counting lots of zeros. Instead write 3 × 10⁹. The power of 10 counts how far the decimal point moves. Tiny numbers get a negative power: 0.002 = 2 × 10⁻³. Significant figures are the digits that matter, starting from the first non-zero digit.

## Key formulas
- N = a × 10ⁿ. a: a number from 1 up to (not including) 10, n: a whole number
- n is positive for big numbers, negative for tiny ones
- 10⁻ⁿ = 1 ÷ 10ⁿ, so 10⁻² = 0.01

## Common mistakes
- Writing 45 × 10³: a must be between 1 and 10, so it is 4.5 × 10⁴.
- Using a positive power for a tiny number: 0.002 is 2 × 10⁻³.
- Counting leading zeros as significant: 0.0052 has 2 significant figures.

## Check questions
1. What range must the number a be in?
   Answer: From 1 up to, but not including, 10.
2. Write 0.00072 in scientific notation.
   Answer: 7.2 × 10⁻⁴.
3. Why does a tiny number get a negative power?
   Answer: Each step smaller divides by 10, which is a negative power of 10.

## Flashcards

A: 5 × 10³.

A: 1 × 10⁻².
Q: What is 3 × 10⁴ in normal form?
A: 30 000.
Q: Round 3.678 to 2 significant figures.
A: 3.7.
Q: How many significant figures in 0.0052?
A: 2.
