# Forces on a slope

## Simple idea
A skateboard on a ramp does not fall straight down, it rolls along the slope. Gravity pulls straight down, but we split that pull into two parts: one along the slope, which makes it slide, and one into the slope, which the ramp pushes back on. A steeper ramp means a bigger slide part.

## Key formulas
- W = m·g. W: weight (N), m: mass (kg), g: 9.8 m/s²
- W along slope = m·g·sin θ. θ: ramp angle, the force is in N
- W into slope = m·g·cos θ. This equals the normal force N (N) when nothing else pushes into or off the slope
- a = g·sin θ. Slide acceleration (m/s²) with no friction

## Picture it
Draw `ramp` with `block` sitting on `ramp.slope-mid`. Label `ramp.angle` "θ". Arrow from `block.center` down the slope labelled "m·g·sin θ", arrow into the slope labelled "m·g·cos θ", arrow straight down labelled "weight m·g".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up sin and cos: along the slope is sin θ, into the slope is cos θ.
- Drawing the normal force straight up: it is at right angles to the slope.
- Using the angle to the vertical: θ is the ramp angle with the ground.

## Check questions
1. A 10 kg block sits on a 30° ramp. What is its weight?
   Answer: 10 × 9.8 = 98 N.
2. What part of that weight pulls it along the slope?
   Answer: 98 × sin 30° = 49 N.
3. Why does a steeper ramp make things slide faster?
   Answer: sin θ gets bigger, so the pull along the slope is bigger.

## Flashcards
Q: Which force part makes a block slide down a ramp?
A: m·g·sin θ, the part along the slope.
Q: Which part presses into the ramp?
A: m·g·cos θ.
Q: Which way does the normal force point on a slope?
A: At right angles to the slope surface.
Q: Slide acceleration on a frictionless ramp?
A: a = g·sin θ.
Q: On a flat surface, θ = 0. What is the slide part?
A: Zero.

## Root check
- newtons-second-law: A 2 kg trolley has a net force of 6 N. What is its acceleration? Answer: 3 m/s².
- trig-ratios: In a right triangle, opposite 3 and hypotenuse 5. What is sin θ? Answer: 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6.
