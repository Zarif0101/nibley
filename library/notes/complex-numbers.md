# Complex numbers

## Simple idea
No real number squared gives −1, so we invent one and call it i. A complex number has a real part and an i part, like 3 + 4i. Think of it as a point on a map: 3 steps across, 4 steps up. Adding and multiplying work like normal algebra, with i² swapped for −1.

## Key formulas
- i² = −1. i: the imaginary unit
- z = a + bi. a: real part, b: imaginary part (both ordinary numbers)
- |z| = √(a² + b²). Size (modulus) of z, the distance from the origin
- Conjugate of a + bi is a − bi
- (a + bi)(a − bi) = a² + b²

## Common mistakes
- Saying i² = 1: it is −1.
- Forgetting i² = −1 when multiplying: (1 + i)(1 − i) = 1 − i² = 2.
- Mixing real and imaginary parts when adding: add them separately.

## Check questions
1. What is (2 + 3i) + (1 − i)?
   Answer: 3 + 2i.
2. What is |3 + 4i|?
   Answer: √(9 + 16) = 5.
3. Why is (a + bi)(a − bi) always a real number?
   Answer: The i terms cancel and −b²i² becomes +b², giving a² + b².

## Flashcards
Q: What is i²?
A: −1.
Q: What are the two parts of a + bi?
A: Real part a and imaginary part b.
Q: What is the conjugate of 2 + 5i?
A: 2 − 5i.
Q: How do you find the size of a + bi?
A: √(a² + b²).
Q: Which axis shows the imaginary part?
A: The vertical (y) axis.

## Root check
- quadratics: Solve x² − 9 = 0. Answer: x = 3 or x = −3.
- indices-surds: Simplify √12. Answer: 2√3.
