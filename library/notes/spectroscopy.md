# Mass, IR and NMR spectroscopy

## Simple idea
Three instruments act like detectives for unknown molecules. Mass spectrometry weighs the molecule. Infrared shows which bonds are inside, like O–H or C=O. NMR shows how many different kinds of hydrogen there are and how many of each. Used together, they reveal the structure.

## Key formulas
- m/z = mass ÷ charge. For M⁺ (charge 1+), m/z equals the molecular mass
- Mass spec: the M⁺ peak, furthest right, gives the molecular mass
- IR: dips show bonds such as O–H and C=O
- NMR: number of peaks = number of hydrogen environments; peak area ∝ number of H

## Common mistakes
- Taking the tallest peak as the molecular mass: use the M⁺ peak, furthest right.
- Reading NMR peaks as atoms: a peak is a kind of hydrogen, and its area shows how many.
- Expecting IR to give the full structure: it only shows which bonds are present.

## Check questions
1. Which technique tells you the molecular mass?
   Answer: Mass spectrometry.
2. Ethanol, C₂H₅OH, has how many hydrogen environments, and what M⁺ mass?
   Answer: Three (CH₃, CH₂, OH); M⁺ at 46.
3. Why is IR useful for spotting an alcohol?
   Answer: It shows an O–H bond absorbing.

## Flashcards
Q: What does the M⁺ peak show?
A: The relative molecular mass of the molecule.
Q: What does infrared spectroscopy identify?
A: Bonds and functional groups, such as C=O.
Q: What does NMR tell you about hydrogen?
A: How many different environments, and how many H in each.
Q: Does a bigger NMR peak area mean more hydrogens?
A: Yes, area is proportional to the number of H.
Q: What does the m in m/z stand for?
A: Mass of the fragment or ion.

- organic-basics: What is a functional group? Answer: The atom or group that decides how a molecule reacts.
