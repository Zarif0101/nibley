# Nerves and nerve impulses

## Simple idea
Neurons are nerve cells that carry messages as fast electrical signals, like a text zipping down a wire. Dendrites receive the signal, the axon carries it, and the ends pass it on. Between two neurons is a tiny gap, a synapse, where a chemical carries the message across.

## Key formulas
- signal path: dendrite → cell body → axon → terminal
- at a synapse: electrical impulse → chemical (neurotransmitter) → next neuron

## Picture it
Draw `neuron`. Arrow along `neuron.dendrite` toward `neuron.cell-body`, labelled "signal in". Arrow along `neuron.axon` toward `neuron.terminal`, labelled "impulse travels". Label `neuron.terminal`: "releases chemicals".

## Common mistakes
- Saying the signal jumps the synapse as electricity: a chemical crosses the gap.
- Mixing up dendrites and axon: dendrites receive, the axon sends.
- Thinking nerves and neurons are the same: a nerve is a bundle of many neurons.

## Check questions
1. Which part of a neuron carries the impulse away from the cell body?
   Answer: The axon.
2. What crosses a synapse?
   Answer: A chemical called a neurotransmitter.
3. Why is a reflex faster than a thought-out action?
   Answer: The signal takes a short route through the spinal cord, skipping the brain.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a neuron?
A: A nerve cell that carries electrical impulses.
Q: What is a synapse?
A: The small gap between two neurons.
Q: What are the receptors for?
A: They detect changes, like light or touch, in the surroundings.
Q: What do effectors do?
A: They respond, like a muscle contracting or a gland releasing.
Q: What makes up the central nervous system?
A: The brain and spinal cord.
