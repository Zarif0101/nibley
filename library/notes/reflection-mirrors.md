# Reflection and mirrors

## Simple idea
A ball bounced off a wall leaves at the same angle it came in. Light does the same off a mirror. A flat mirror shows you an image as far behind it as you are in front. A curved mirror can focus light or spread it out.

## Key formulas
- θi = θr. θi: angle of incidence, θr: angle of reflection (degrees), both measured from the normal (the line at 90° to the mirror)
- 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. Cartesian convention. u: object distance, v: image distance, f: focal length (all in metres, m, from the mirror's pole)
- f = R / 2. R: radius of curvature (m)
- m = −v/u. m: magnification (no unit); size of image ÷ size of object

## Common mistakes
- Measuring angles from the mirror surface: measure from the normal.
- Saying f = R: the focus is halfway, f = R / 2.
- Thinking a plane mirror image is on the surface: it sits as far behind as you are in front.

## Check questions
1. A ray hits a mirror with angle of incidence 35°. What is the angle of reflection?
   Answer: 35°.
2. A concave mirror has radius of curvature 20 cm. What is its focal length?
   Answer: 10 cm.
3. Why can you see yourself in a smooth mirror but not in a rough wall?
   Answer: A smooth mirror reflects all rays in order; a rough wall scatters them in every direction.

## Flashcards
Q: State the law of reflection.
A: The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
Q: What is the normal?
A: A line at 90° to the surface at the point of hit.
Q: Is a plane mirror image real or virtual?
A: Virtual, and the same size as the object.
Q: What does a concave mirror do to parallel rays?
A: It brings them together at the focus.
Q: How is focal length linked to radius of curvature?
A: f = R / 2.
