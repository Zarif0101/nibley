# Geometric sequences and series

## Simple idea
A geometric sequence multiplies by the same number each step, like a rumour where each person tells 2 more people, or a ball that bounces back to half its last height. That number is the common ratio. If the ratio is between −1 and 1, the terms shrink and the endless sum settles on a fixed number.

## Key formulas
- aₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹. aₙ: the nth term, a: first term, r: common ratio, n: term number
- Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r). Sum of the first n terms (r ≠ 1)
- S∞ = a / (1 − r). Sum to infinity, only when |r| < 1

## Common mistakes
- Finding r by subtracting: divide a term by the one before it.
- Using rⁿ instead of rⁿ⁻¹ for the nth term.
- Using S∞ when |r| ≥ 1: the sum does not settle.

## Check questions
1. Find the 4th term of 2, 6, 18, ...
   Answer: r = 3, so 2 × 3³ = 54.
2. What is the sum of the first 3 terms of 2, 6, 18?
   Answer: 2 + 6 + 18 = 26.
3. Why does 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + ... settle at 2?
   Answer: S∞ = 1 / (1 − 1/2) = 2, because the terms keep halving and get tiny.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the common ratio?
A: The fixed number you multiply by each step.
Q: Formula for the nth term?
A: aₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹.
Q: Formula for the sum of n terms?
A: Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r).
Q: When does a sum to infinity exist?
A: When |r| < 1.
Q: What is r for 80, 40, 20, ...?
A: 1/2.

## Root check
- arithmetic-sequences: What is the next term of 5, 8, 11, ... and the common difference? Answer: Next term 14, difference 3.
- indices-surds: What is 2³ × 2²? Answer: 2⁵ = 32.
