# First law of thermodynamics

## Simple idea
Energy is never lost, only moved around. Heat a gas in a cylinder: some energy makes its particles move faster (it warms up), and some pushes the piston out (work). Heat in equals the warming plus the work done. Some books write the signs differently but mean the same.

## Key formulas
- ΔU = Q − W. ΔU: change in internal energy (joules, J), Q: heat added to the gas (J), W: work done by the gas (J)
- W = p·ΔV at constant pressure. p: pressure (Pa), ΔV: change in volume (m³)
- Other books (CBSE uses ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW; A-Level uses ΔU = q + w, w done on the gas) write it differently but mean the same.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing signs: with ΔU = Q − W, W is work done by the gas.
- Using °C or litres in W = p·ΔV: use Pa and m³.
- Thinking Q is stored heat: Q is heat that flowed in.

## Check questions
1. A gas gains 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work. Find ΔU.
   Answer: 500 − 200 = 300 J.
2. A gas expands by 0.002 m³ at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. How much work does it do?
   Answer: 1.0 × 10⁵ × 0.002 = 200 J.
3. Why does the gas not warm up if all the heat goes into work?
   Answer: Then ΔU = 0, so the internal energy and temperature stay the same.

## Flashcards
Q: State the first law with signs.
A: ΔU = Q − W, with W done by the gas.
Q: What is internal energy?
A: The total energy of the gas particles' motion.
Q: What does the first law really say?
A: Energy is conserved.
Q: Work done by a gas at constant pressure?
A: W = p·ΔV.
Q: Heat in, no work done. Where does it go?
A: All into internal energy.

## Root check
- ideal-gas-law: Gas volume is fixed and its kelvin temperature doubles. What happens to pressure? Answer: It doubles.
- work-done: How much work does a 10 N force do moving something 3 m along its direction? Answer: 30 J.
