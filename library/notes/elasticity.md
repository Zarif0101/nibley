# Hooke's law and elasticity

## Simple idea
Pull a spring and it stretches. Pull twice as hard and it stretches twice as far. That straight-line link is Hooke's law. A stiff spring needs a lot of force for a little stretch. Pull too far, past the elastic limit, and the spring stays stretched.

## Key formulas
- F = k·x. F: force (N), k: spring constant (N/m), x: extension from natural length (m)
- E = ½·k·x². E: energy stored (J)

## Picture it
Draw `mass-spring`. Arrow down from `mass-spring.mass-bottom`, labelled "weight F". Dashed bracket along `mass-spring.spring-mid`, labelled "extension x".
Or draw `spring` flat with an arrow from `spring.right` pointing right, labelled "pull F".

## Common mistakes
- Using the whole spring length: x is only the extra stretch.
- Using centimetres: change x to metres first.
- Using Hooke's law past the elastic limit: the line stops being straight.

## Check questions
1. What does the spring constant k tell you?
   Answer: How stiff the spring is: force per metre of stretch.
2. A spring has k = 200 N/m and is stretched 0.05 m. What is the force?
   Answer: 200 × 0.05 = 10 N.
3. Why does a 6 N pull that stretches a spring 3 cm make 12 N stretch it 6 cm?
   Answer: Extension is proportional to force, so doubling force doubles it.

## Flashcards
Q: State Hooke's law.
A: Extension is proportional to the force applied.
Q: What is the unit of the spring constant?
A: Newtons per metre (N/m).
Q: What is the elastic limit?
A: The point past which a material does not return to its shape.
Q: What does a force-extension graph look like within the limit?
A: A straight line through the origin.
Q: What is the slope of a force-extension graph?
A: The spring constant k.
