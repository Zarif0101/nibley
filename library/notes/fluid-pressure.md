# Pressure in fluids

## Simple idea
Pressure is how much force is squeezed onto each bit of area. A sharp knife cuts well because all your push sits on a tiny edge. In water, pressure grows as you dive deeper, because more water is stacked above you. That is why your ears feel it at the bottom of a pool.

## Key formulas
- p = F / A. p: pressure (pascals, Pa), F: force (N), A: area (m²)
- p = ρ·g·h. ρ: fluid density (kg/m³), g: 9.8 (m/s²), h: depth (m)

## Picture it
Draw `beaker`. Arrow pointing sideways at `beaker.liquid-mid`, labelled "pressure here". Longer arrow at `beaker.bottom`, labelled "more pressure deeper".
Label at `beaker.liquid-surface`: "depth h = 0, only air pressure".

## Common mistakes
- Using the wrong area: area must be in m², not cm².
- Thinking pressure depends on the container's shape: only depth and density matter.
- Thinking pressure only pushes down: in a fluid it pushes in all directions.

## Check questions
1. What is the unit of pressure?
   Answer: The pascal (Pa), equal to 1 N/m².
2. What is the pressure from water (1000 kg/m³) at 2 m deep?
   Answer: 1000 × 9.8 × 2 = 19 600 Pa.
3. Why is a high-heel shoe more damaging to a floor than a flat shoe?
   Answer: Same force on a smaller area gives much higher pressure.

## Flashcards
Q: What is pressure?
A: Force per unit area.
Q: What is the formula for pressure in a liquid?
A: p = ρ·g·h.
Q: Depth doubles. What happens to the liquid pressure?
A: It doubles.
Q: A 100 N force acts on 0.5 m². What is the pressure?
A: 100 ÷ 0.5 = 200 Pa.
Q: In which directions does a fluid push?
A: In all directions, at right angles to surfaces.
