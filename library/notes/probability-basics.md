# Probability basics

## Simple idea
Probability is a number that says how likely something is. Zero means it can never happen, one means it is certain. Roll a normal die: there are 6 equally likely faces, and only one of them is a 6. So the chance of a 6 is 1 out of 6.

## Key formulas
- P(A) = number of ways A can happen / total number of equally likely outcomes. P(A): probability of event A, a number from 0 to 1 (no unit)
- P(not A) = 1 − P(A)
- P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B), when A and B cannot both happen
- P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B), when A and B do not affect each other

## Picture it
Draw `dice`. Write "6 faces, equally likely" above `dice.top-face`.
Next to `dice.side-face` write "P(6) = 1/6", and next to `dice.front-face` write "P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2".

## Common mistakes
- Answers bigger than 1 or below 0: a probability must stay between 0 and 1.
- Counting outcomes that are not equally likely as if they were: list them fairly first.
- Adding when the events can both happen: the "or" rule only works if they cannot both happen.

## Check questions
1. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than 4 on a die?
   Answer: Two faces (5, 6), so 2/6 = 1/3.
2. The chance of rain is 0.3. What is the chance of no rain?
   Answer: 1 − 0.3 = 0.7.
3. Why can a probability never be more than 1?
   Answer: 1 means certain, and nothing can be more than certain.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the probability of an impossible event?
A: 0.
Q: What is the probability of a certain event?
A: 1.
Q: What is P(not A)?
A: 1 − P(A).
Q: What is the sample space?
A: The list of all possible outcomes.
Q: What is P(heads) for a fair coin?
A: 1/2.

## Root check
- fractions-decimals: Simplify 4/8. Answer: 1/2.
- sets: How many elements are in the set {2, 4, 6, 8}? Answer: 4.
