# Photosynthesis

## Simple idea
Plants make their own food using sunlight, like a tiny solar kitchen. Leaves take in carbon dioxide from air and water from the soil. In chloroplasts, light energy turns them into glucose, and oxygen is released. The glucose feeds the plant, and we breathe the oxygen.

## Key formulas
- carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (needs light and chlorophyll)
- 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
- It is the reverse of aerobic respiration, but it needs light energy in

## Common mistakes
- Thinking plants only photosynthesise and never respire: they respire all the time, day and night.
- Thinking plants get food from soil: they make glucose; soil gives water and minerals.
- Mixing up the gases: CO₂ goes in, oxygen comes out.

## Check questions
1. Which green substance traps light?
   Answer: Chlorophyll, found in chloroplasts.
2. Name the two raw materials of photosynthesis.
   Answer: Carbon dioxide and water.
3. Why does a plant in a dark cupboard make less glucose?
   Answer: There is no light energy to drive photosynthesis.

## Flashcards
Q: Write the word equation for photosynthesis.
A: Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, using light.
Q: Where does photosynthesis happen in the cell?
A: In the chloroplasts.
Q: What does chlorophyll do?
A: It absorbs light energy.
Q: How does carbon dioxide enter a leaf?
A: Through tiny holes called stomata.
Q: What does the plant use glucose for?
A: Energy, growth, and storing as starch.
