# Equations of motion

## Simple idea
When acceleration stays steady, like a car pulling away smoothly, four equations link distance, speeds, acceleration and time. Know three of the five quantities and you can find the rest. Some books write v₀ for u, and x for s. They mean the same things.

## Key formulas
- v = u + a·t. v: final velocity (m/s), u: starting velocity (m/s), a: acceleration (m/s²), t: time (s)
- s = u·t + ½·a·t². s: displacement (m)
- v² = u² + 2·a·s. v², u²: velocities squared (m²/s²)
- s = ½·(u + v)·t. Average velocity times time

## Common mistakes
- Using them when acceleration is not constant: they only work for steady a.
- Mixing signs: pick one positive direction and keep to it.
- Forgetting to square: in v² = u² + 2as, take the square root at the end.

## Check questions
1. A car starts at rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s. What is v?
   Answer: 10 m/s.
2. How far does it travel in those 5 s?
   Answer: s = ½ × 2 × 5² = 25 m.
3. Why does u = 0 make s = ½·a·t² simpler?
   Answer: The u·t part is zero, so only the acceleration part is left.

## Flashcards
Q: Which equation links v, u, a and t?
A: v = u + a·t.
Q: Which equation has no time in it?
A: v² = u² + 2·a·s.
Q: When can you use these equations?
A: Only when acceleration is constant.
Q: What does u stand for?
A: Starting (initial) velocity.
Q: What does "starts from rest" tell you?
A: u = 0.

## Root check
- acceleration: A scooter speeds from 2 m/s to 8 m/s in 3 s. What is a? Answer: 2 m/s².
- rearranging-formulas: Make t the subject of v = u + a·t. Answer: t = (v − u) ÷ a.
