# Lenses

## Simple idea
A magnifying glass bends sunlight to a bright spot. A convex lens, thick in the middle, pulls rays together to a focus. A concave lens, thin in the middle, spreads them apart. Where an object sits decides whether its image is big or small, upright or upside down.

## Key formulas
- 1/v − 1/u = 1/f. Cartesian convention. u: object distance, v: image distance, f: focal length (metres, m)
- m = v / u. m: magnification (no unit), image size ÷ object size; a negative m means inverted
- P = 1 / f. P: power of lens (dioptres, D), f in metres

## Picture it
Draw `lens-convex` with `lens-convex.focus-left` and `lens-convex.focus-right` both labelled "F". Parallel arrows from the left meet at `lens-convex.focus-right`, labelled "rays meet here".
Draw `lens-concave` with rays spreading out, labelled "rays spread apart".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing sign rules: pick one convention and keep it for the whole question.
- Calling every image real: a virtual image cannot be caught on a screen.
- Forgetting f in metres when finding power: 50 cm is 0.5 m.

## Check questions
1. A convex lens has f = 0.5 m. What is its power?
   Answer: 2 D.
2. An object sits at 2f from a convex lens. Where is the image and how big is it?
   Answer: At 2f on the other side, upside down, same size.
3. Why can a concave lens never make a real image?
   Answer: It always spreads the rays apart, so they never meet.

## Flashcards
Q: What does a convex lens do to parallel rays?
A: It brings them together at the focus.
Q: What kind of image does a concave lens always make?
A: Virtual, upright and smaller.
Q: What is a real image?
A: One formed where rays actually meet; it can show on a screen.
Q: What is the unit of lens power?
A: The dioptre (D), equal to 1/metre.
Q: What does a magnifying glass use?
A: A convex lens with the object closer than f.

## Root check
- refraction: Light goes from air into glass. Does it speed up or slow down? Answer: It slows down.
