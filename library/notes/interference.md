# Interference and the double slit

## Simple idea
Drop two stones in a pond and the ripples cross. Where two crests meet, the water rises high; where a crest meets a trough, it stays flat. Light from two narrow slits does the same on a screen, making bright and dark stripes. Stripes prove light is a wave.

## Key formulas
- Bright: path difference = n·λ. n: 0, 1, 2… (no unit), λ: wavelength (metres, m)
- Dark: path difference = (n + ½)·λ
- β = λ·D / d. β: fringe spacing (m), D: slits to screen distance (m), d: gap between slits (m)

## Common mistakes
- Using slit gap in millimetres or wavelength in nanometres unconverted: change both to metres.
- Thinking dark means no light arrived: two waves arrived and cancelled.
- Mixing the rules: whole wavelengths give bright, half wavelengths give dark.

## Check questions
1. Two waves meet crest to crest. Bright or dark?
   Answer: Bright, they add up.
2. λ = 6.0 × 10⁻⁷ m, D = 2.0 m, d = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m. What is the fringe spacing?
   Answer: 3.0 × 10⁻³ m, which is 3 mm.
3. Why does a path difference of half a wavelength give darkness?
   Answer: A crest meets a trough, so the two waves cancel.

## Flashcards
Q: What is interference?
A: Two waves overlapping and adding or cancelling.
Q: What is constructive interference?
A: Waves meet in step, making a bigger wave.
Q: What is destructive interference?
A: Waves meet out of step, making a smaller or zero wave.
Q: Who first showed light interferes?
A: Thomas Young, with his double-slit experiment.
Q: What happens to fringe spacing if slits move closer together?
A: The stripes spread wider apart.

## Root check
- superposition: Two waves meet crest to crest. What does the combined wave do? Answer: It gets bigger, as the two heights add.
- em-spectrum: How fast does light travel in a vacuum? Answer: 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.
