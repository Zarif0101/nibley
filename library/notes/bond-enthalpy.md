# Bond enthalpies

## Simple idea
Pulling two magnets apart takes effort, and letting them snap together gives energy back. Chemical bonds work the same way. Breaking bonds takes energy in. Making new bonds gives energy out. The difference between the two is the energy change of the reaction.

## Key formulas
- ΔH = (bonds broken) − (bonds made). ΔH: enthalpy change (kJ/mol); each side is the sum of bond enthalpies (kJ/mol)
- H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. Example reaction used below

## Common mistakes
- Doing bonds made − bonds broken: the formula is broken minus made.
- Forgetting to count every bond: 2HCl has two H–Cl bonds.
- Thinking breaking bonds gives out energy: breaking always takes energy in.

## Check questions
1. H–H is 436, Cl–Cl is 243 and H–Cl is 432 kJ/mol. Find ΔH for H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl.
   Answer: Broken 679, made 864, so ΔH = −185 kJ/mol.
2. Bonds broken total 1000 kJ/mol and bonds made total 900 kJ/mol. Exothermic or endothermic?
   Answer: Endothermic, ΔH = +100 kJ/mol.
3. Why is ΔH negative when more energy is released making bonds than taken in breaking them?
   Answer: The surroundings gain energy, so the reaction gives out heat.

## Flashcards
Q: Does breaking a bond take in or give out energy?
A: It takes energy in.
Q: Does making a bond take in or give out energy?
A: It gives energy out.
Q: Write the bond enthalpy formula for ΔH.
A: ΔH = bonds broken − bonds made.
Q: What unit do bond enthalpies use?
A: Kilojoules per mole, kJ/mol.
Q: Many strong new bonds form. Is the reaction likely exothermic?
A: Yes, ΔH comes out negative.
