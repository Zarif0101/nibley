# Mitosis and the cell cycle

## Simple idea
Mitosis is how one cell makes two identical copies of itself. Think of photocopying a page, then splitting the pile in two. First the cell copies its DNA, then the copies are pulled apart, then the cell divides. Your body uses it to grow and to heal a cut.

## Key formulas
- 1 cell with 46 chromosomes → 2 cells, each with 46 chromosomes
- stages in order: prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase

## Picture it
Draw `chromosome`. Label `chromosome.centromere`: "centromere joins the copies". Label `chromosome.arm-top-left`: "one chromatid". Then draw `cell-animal`, with label at `cell-animal.nucleus`: "DNA copied before dividing".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the daughter cells are different: mitosis makes identical copies.
- Mixing up mitosis and meiosis: mitosis is for growth and repair, meiosis makes sex cells.
- Forgetting DNA is copied first: it happens before mitosis starts.

## Check questions
1. How many cells does one mitosis make?
   Answer: Two.
2. A human cell has 46 chromosomes. How many does each new cell have after mitosis?
   Answer: 46.
3. Why do we need mitosis after a cut?
   Answer: To make new identical cells that replace the damaged ones.

## Flashcards
Q: What is mitosis?
A: Cell division making two genetically identical cells.
Q: Name two uses of mitosis.
A: Growth and repair.
Q: What happens in anaphase?
A: The sister chromatids are pulled apart to opposite ends.
Q: What is a chromatid?
A: One of the two identical copies of a copied chromosome.
Q: What happens to DNA before mitosis?
A: It is copied (replicated).
