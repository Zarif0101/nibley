# Rotational motion

## Simple idea
Spinning things resist changes in their spin, like moving things resist changes in speed. A spinning skater pulls her arms in and spins faster. Her spin amount (angular momentum) stays the same, so smaller spread means faster turning. Only an outside torque can change it.

## Key formulas
- L = I·ω. L: angular momentum (kg·m²/s), I: moment of inertia (kg·m²), ω: angular speed (rad/s)
- τ = I·α. τ: net torque (N·m), α: angular acceleration (rad/s²)
- I₁·ω₁ = I₂·ω₂. The same L before and after, with no outside torque

## Common mistakes
- Treating I as just mass: it also depends on how far the mass is from the axis.
- Forgetting ω must be in rad/s: not revolutions per minute.
- Using conservation of L when a torque acts: it needs zero net torque.

## Check questions
1. What does moment of inertia measure?
   Answer: How hard it is to change something's spin.
2. A disc has I = 2 kg·m² and spins at 5 rad/s. What is L?
   Answer: 2 × 5 = 10 kg·m²/s.
3. Why does a skater spin faster when she pulls her arms in?
   Answer: I gets smaller, L stays the same, so ω must go up.

## Flashcards
Q: What is angular momentum?
A: Spin amount: moment of inertia times angular speed.
Q: When is angular momentum conserved?
A: When no net outside torque acts.
Q: What is the unit of moment of inertia?
A: kg·m².
Q: I halves with no outside torque. What happens to ω?
A: It doubles.
Q: What does a net torque cause?
A: A change in spin, called angular acceleration.

## Root check
- torque: A 20 N force acts 0.5 m from a pivot at right angles. What is the torque? Answer: 10 N·m.
- circular-motion: An object spins at ω = 2 rad/s on a circle of radius 3 m. What is its speed? Answer: v = ω·r = 6 m/s.
