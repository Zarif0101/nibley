# Enzymes

## Simple idea
Enzymes are proteins that speed up reactions in your body without being used up. Each one fits only its own substrate, like a key fits one lock. Without them, digesting a meal would take far too long. Too much heat changes the enzyme's shape and it stops working.

## Key formulas
- substrate + enzyme → enzyme-substrate complex → products + enzyme
- starch → sugars, using the enzyme amylase

## Picture it
Draw `enzyme-substrate`. Arrow from `enzyme-substrate.substrate` down to `enzyme-substrate.active-site`, labelled "fits like a key". Label `enzyme-substrate.enzyme`: "enzyme, a protein".

## Common mistakes
- Saying enzymes get used up: they are reusable, only the substrate changes.
- Saying heat "kills" enzymes: they are not alive. Heat denatures them (changes shape).
- Thinking one enzyme works on anything: each fits just one substrate.

## Check questions
1. What is the part of the enzyme where the substrate fits?
   Answer: The active site.
2. An enzyme works best at 37 °C. What happens at 80 °C?
   Answer: It denatures, its shape changes, and it stops working.
3. Why can amylase not break down proteins?
   Answer: Proteins do not fit its active site, since enzymes are specific.

## Flashcards
Q: What are enzymes made of?
A: Protein.
Q: What is an enzyme's job?
A: Speed up a reaction without being used up.
Q: What is a substrate?
A: The molecule an enzyme acts on.
Q: What does denatured mean?
A: The enzyme's shape has changed, so it no longer works.
Q: What is the optimum temperature?
A: The temperature where an enzyme works fastest.
