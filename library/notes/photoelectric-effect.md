# Photoelectric effect

## Simple idea
Light comes in tiny packets called photons. Shine light on a metal and each photon can kick out one electron, like a ball hitting a coin. It only works if the photon has enough energy. A faint blue light can do it; a very bright red light cannot.

## Key formulas
- E = h·f = h·c / λ. E: photon energy (joules, J), h: 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, f: frequency (hertz, Hz), c: 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, λ: wavelength (metres, m)
- K(max) = h·f − φ. K(max): top kinetic energy of an electron (J), φ: work function, energy needed to escape (J)
- 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. eV: electronvolt, a small energy unit

## Common mistakes
- Thinking brighter light fixes it: if f is too low, no electrons come out at any brightness.
- Mixing up brightness and energy: brightness gives more photons, frequency gives more energy each.
- Forgetting to subtract the work function: K(max) = hf − φ.

## Check questions
1. What is the energy of a photon with f = 6.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz?
   Answer: About 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, which is 2.5 eV.
2. A photon of 2.5 eV hits a metal with φ = 2.0 eV. What is K(max)?
   Answer: 0.5 eV.
3. Why does brighter red light not free electrons when dim blue light does?
   Answer: Each red photon has too little energy, and one photon frees one electron.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a photon?
A: A packet of light energy.
Q: What is the work function?
A: The least energy needed to free an electron from a metal.
Q: What is threshold frequency?
A: The lowest frequency that frees electrons from the metal.
Q: What does greater brightness change?
A: The number of electrons freed, not their top energy.
Q: What does Einstein's equation say?
A: Photon energy = work function + electron's top kinetic energy.
