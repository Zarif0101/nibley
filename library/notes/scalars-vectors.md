# Scalars and vectors in physics

## Simple idea
Some quantities need only a size. "5 kg of rice" is complete. Others need a direction too. "Walk 5 m" is not enough; "walk 5 m north" is. Size only is a scalar. Size plus direction is a vector. Vectors are drawn as arrows: longer arrow, bigger size.

## Key formulas
- Scalars: mass, time, distance, speed, energy. Size only
- Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force. Size and direction
- R = A + B. R: resultant vector, A and B: vectors being added (same unit). Place head to tail
- Two vectors in the same direction: sizes add. In opposite directions: sizes subtract

## Common mistakes
- Calling velocity a scalar: velocity has a direction, speed does not.
- Adding vector sizes without direction: 3 N left plus 3 N right gives 0.
- Giving a vector with no direction: say "5 m east", not just "5 m".

## Check questions
1. Is mass a scalar or a vector?
   Answer: A scalar, it has no direction.
2. Forces of 5 N right and 2 N left act on a box. What is the resultant?
   Answer: 3 N to the right.
3. Why is velocity a vector but speed is not?
   Answer: Velocity includes direction; speed is only how fast.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a scalar?
A: A quantity with size only.
Q: What is a vector?
A: A quantity with both size and direction.
Q: Name two vector quantities.
A: Force and velocity (also displacement, acceleration).
Q: Name two scalar quantities.
A: Mass and time (also speed, distance).
Q: What does a longer arrow mean?
A: A bigger size of the vector.
