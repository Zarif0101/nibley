# Heart and circulation

## Simple idea
The heart is a muscle pump with two sides. The right side sends blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen. The left side sends it to the rest of the body. Blood goes round twice per trip, like a figure of eight. It carries oxygen and food to every cell.

## Key formulas
- body → vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → lungs
- lungs → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body

## Picture it
Draw `heart`. Arrow into `heart.right-atrium` from `heart.vena-cava`, labelled "blood from body". Arrow out of `heart.left-ventricle` to `heart.aorta`, labelled "oxygen-rich blood to body". Label `heart.septum`: "wall between sides".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up sides: the patient's left is on the right of the picture.
- Thinking all arteries carry oxygen-rich blood: the pulmonary artery does not.
- Thinking veins have no valves: veins have valves that stop blood flowing backwards.

## Check questions
1. Which chamber pumps blood out to the body?
   Answer: The left ventricle.
2. What do arteries do, and what do veins do?
   Answer: Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it back.
3. Why is the left ventricle wall thicker than the right?
   Answer: It pumps blood around the whole body, which needs more force.

## Flashcards
Q: What do capillaries do?
A: Swap oxygen and food with cells; their walls are one cell thick.
Q: What do valves in the heart do?
A: Stop blood flowing backwards.
Q: Which vessel leaves the left ventricle?
A: The aorta.
Q: What carries oxygen in blood?
A: Red blood cells.
Q: What is the septum?
A: The wall between the left and right sides.

## Root check
- gas-exchange: Which gas moves from the alveoli into the blood? Answer: Oxygen.
