# Entropy and Gibbs free energy

## Simple idea
Things drift towards more mess and spread-out energy: a hot drink cools, a dropped tray of marbles scatters. Entropy measures that spread-out-ness. A reaction can happen by itself when it gives out heat, or makes more mess, or both. Gibbs free energy (ΔG) combines the two into one number: negative means it can go by itself.

## Key formulas
- ΔG = ΔH − T·ΔS. ΔG: Gibbs free energy change (kJ/mol), ΔH: enthalpy change (kJ/mol), T: temperature (kelvin, K), ΔS: entropy change (J/(K·mol))
- ΔG < 0: the reaction can happen by itself
- K = °C + 273. Kelvin from Celsius
- ΔS is in J but ΔH is in kJ: divide T·ΔS by 1000 first

## Common mistakes
- Using °C for T: change to kelvin by adding 273.
- Mixing kJ and J: turn ΔS into kJ/(K·mol) (divide by 1000) before using the formula.
- Thinking "spontaneous" means fast: negative ΔG says it can happen, not how quickly.

## Check questions
1. What sign of ΔG means a reaction can happen by itself?
   Answer: Negative.
2. ΔH = +30 kJ/mol, ΔS = +100 J/(K·mol), T = 400 K. Find ΔG.
   Answer: T·ΔS = 40 kJ/mol, so ΔG = 30 − 40 = −10 kJ/mol. It can happen.
3. Why does a reaction that takes in heat sometimes still happen by itself?
   Answer: If ΔS is positive and T is high, T·ΔS is big enough to make ΔG negative.

## Flashcards
Q: What does entropy measure?
A: How spread out the energy and particles are.
Q: Write the Gibbs equation.
A: ΔG = ΔH − T·ΔS.
Q: Which temperature unit must be used in ΔG = ΔH − T·ΔS?
A: Kelvin.
Q: Does a gas have higher or lower entropy than the same liquid?
A: Higher, because gas particles are more spread out.
Q: What does negative ΔG tell you about speed?
A: Nothing. It only says the reaction can happen.

## Root check
- hess-law: A→B has ΔH = +50 kJ and B→C has ΔH = −20 kJ. What is ΔH for A→C? Answer: +30 kJ.
- second-law-thermodynamics: In a natural change, does the total entropy of the universe go up or down? Answer: It goes up.
