# Gravitational potential energy

## Simple idea
Potential energy is energy stored because of where something is. Carry a book up to a shelf and you do work on it. That work is now stored. Drop the book and the stored energy turns into movement. The higher it sits, and the heavier it is, the more is stored.

## Key formulas
- ΔGPE = m·g·Δh. ΔGPE: change in potential energy (J), m: mass (kg), g: 9.8 (m/s² or N/kg), Δh: change in height (m)

## Picture it
Draw `ball` above `ground`. Dashed line from `ball.bottom` down to `ground.mid`, labelled "height h".
Label beside `ball.right`: "stored energy = m·g·h".

## Common mistakes
- Measuring height along a slope: use the straight-up height only.
- Using grams: mass must be in kg.
- Thinking the zero level is fixed: you choose it, and only changes in height matter.

## Check questions
1. What is the unit of potential energy?
   Answer: The joule (J).
2. How much energy is stored by lifting a 3 kg bag up 2 m?
   Answer: 3 × 9.8 × 2 = 58.8 J.
3. Why does the same ball hold more energy on a high shelf than a low one?
   Answer: More height means more work was done lifting it, and ΔGPE = m·g·Δh.

## Flashcards
Q: What is gravitational potential energy?
A: Energy stored in an object because of its height.
Q: What is the formula for the change in GPE?
A: ΔGPE = m·g·Δh.
Q: Height doubles. What happens to GPE?
A: It doubles.
Q: What value of g do we use?
A: 9.8 m/s², or 9.8 N/kg.
Q: What happens to GPE as an object falls?
A: It decreases, turning into kinetic energy.

## Root check
- work-done: A 20 N force moves a box 4 m in its own direction. How much work? Answer: 80 J.
