# Acids and bases

## Simple idea
Lemon juice is sour and soap feels slippery. The sour one is an acid and the slippery one is a base. An acid gives away H⁺ ions (hydrogen ions). A base takes them. Strong acids hand over nearly all their H⁺ in water. Weak ones hand over only a few.

## Key formulas
- HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻. Strong acid: splits up completely in water
- CH₃COOH ⇌ H⁺ + CH₃COO⁻. Weak acid: splits only partly
- acid + base → salt + water. Neutralisation

## Picture it
Draw `beaker` with "acid" written at `beaker.liquid-mid`. Draw `ph-scale` and mark `ph-scale.acid-end` "acidic", `ph-scale.neutral` "neutral" and `ph-scale.base-end` "basic".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up strong with concentrated: strong is about splitting up, concentrated is about amount.
- Thinking a base must be a hydroxide: ammonia, NH₃, is a base too.
- Forgetting that acids give H⁺ and bases take H⁺: remember "acid donates".

## Check questions
1. What ion do all acids give in water?
   Answer: The H⁺ ion.
2. HCl and NaOH are mixed. What two things are made?
   Answer: Salt (NaCl) and water.
3. Why is ethanoic acid a weak acid?
   Answer: Only a small part of it splits into H⁺ ions.

## Flashcards
Q: What does an acid do with H⁺?
A: It gives it away (donates it).
Q: What does a base do with H⁺?
A: It accepts it.
Q: Name one strong acid.
A: Hydrochloric acid, HCl.
Q: What is made when an acid and a base react?
A: A salt and water.
Q: What does a double arrow ⇌ show in a weak acid equation?
A: It only partly splits up.
