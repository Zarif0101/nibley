# Electron configuration

## Simple idea
Electrons sit in shells around the nucleus, like seats in a theatre filling from the front row. Each shell has subshells called s, p and d. Electrons take the lowest-energy seat that is free. The pattern of filled seats is the electron configuration.

## Key formulas
- Subshell capacity: s holds 2 electrons, p holds 6, d holds 10
- Filling order: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d
- Sodium (11 electrons): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹
- Chlorine (17 electrons): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵
- The small raised number is how many electrons are in that subshell

## Picture it
Draw `atom` and label `atom.orbit-1` "shell 1: 2 electrons", `atom.orbit-2` "shell 2: up to 8".
Draw `orbitals`: label `orbitals.s-orbital` "s: round" and `orbitals.p-orbital` "p: two lobes".

## Common mistakes
- Filling 3d before 4s: 4s has lower energy, so fill it first.
- Writing too many electrons in p: p holds at most 6.
- Forgetting to count to the right total: the numbers must add up to the atomic number.

## Check questions
1. Write the configuration of oxygen (8 electrons).
   Answer: 1s² 2s² 2p⁴.
2. How many electrons fit in the 2p subshell?
   Answer: 6.
3. Why does a neutral calcium atom, with 20 electrons, end in 4s² and not 3d²?
   Answer: 4s fills before 3d because it has lower energy.

## Flashcards
Q: How many electrons can an s subshell hold?
A: 2.
Q: How many electrons can a p subshell hold?
A: 6.
Q: What is the configuration of sodium?
A: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹.
Q: Which fills first, 4s or 3d?
A: 4s.
Q: What do the numbers in 2p⁶ mean?
A: Shell 2, p subshell, 6 electrons.
