# Control of gene expression

## Simple idea
Every cell in your body has the same DNA, but a muscle cell and a nerve cell act very differently. That is because each cell switches different genes on and off, like using only the apps you need on a phone. Helper proteins decide which genes get read.

## Key formulas
- Gene on: transcription happens → mRNA → protein made
- Gene off: transcription blocked → no protein made
- Transcription factor: a protein that helps switch a gene on or off

## Picture it
Draw `dna-helix`. Mark a section near `dna-helix.top` with "switch: protein binds here". Draw a stretch lower down, near `dna-helix.bottom`, labelled "gene".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking different cells have different DNA: nearly all cells carry the same DNA.
- Thinking genes are always on: most genes in a cell are switched off.
- Thinking the lac operon is in humans: it is a bacterial example.

## Check questions
1. Do a skin cell and a liver cell of one person have the same genes?
   Answer: Yes, nearly the same DNA.
2. A gene is switched off. Is its protein made?
   Answer: No, because transcription is blocked.
3. Why do cells switch genes off?
   Answer: To make only the proteins they need, saving energy.

## Flashcards
Q: What is gene expression?
A: When a gene is read to make its protein.
Q: What is a transcription factor?
A: A protein that switches a gene on or off.
Q: Why do cells differ if they share DNA?
A: They use different genes.
Q: In bacteria, what does the lac operon control?
A: Genes for digesting lactose sugar.
Q: When does the lac operon switch on?
A: When lactose is present.

## Root check
- protein-synthesis: What are the two steps from gene to protein? Answer: Transcription, then translation.
