# Transformers

## Simple idea
A phone charger turns the wall's 230 V into a small, safe voltage. Inside are two coils of wire on one iron core. Alternating current in the first coil makes a changing magnetic field, and that pushes a voltage in the second coil. More turns means more voltage.

## Key formulas
- Vs / Vp = Ns / Np. Vs, Vp: secondary and primary voltage (volts, V); Ns, Np: number of turns (no unit)
- Vp·Ip = Vs·Is. Ideal transformer, no energy lost. Ip, Is: primary and secondary current (amperes, A)

## Picture it
Draw `transformer`. Label `transformer.primary` "few turns, input" and `transformer.secondary` "many turns, output". Arrow along `transformer.core-top` labelled "changing magnetic field".

## Common mistakes
- Trying it with a battery: it needs alternating current, or the field never changes.
- Thinking a step-up transformer gives free power: voltage rises but current falls.
- Flipping the ratio: the coil with more turns has the bigger voltage.

## Check questions
1. A transformer has 100 turns on the primary and 10 on the secondary. Input is 230 V. What is the output?
   Answer: 23 V.
2. Is that transformer step-up or step-down?
   Answer: Step-down, because the voltage goes down.
3. Why does a transformer not work with steady direct current?
   Answer: The magnetic field stays constant, so no voltage is made in the second coil.

## Flashcards
Q: What two parts make a transformer?
A: Two coils on a shared iron core.
Q: What does a step-up transformer do?
A: It raises the voltage, with more turns on the secondary.
Q: What does the iron core do?
A: It carries the changing magnetic field from one coil to the other.
Q: In an ideal transformer, what stays equal?
A: Power in equals power out.
Q: Why is power sent over long distances at high voltage?
A: Lower current means less energy lost as heat in the wires.

## Root check
- alternating-current: Does alternating current keep one direction or keep reversing? Answer: It keeps reversing direction.
