# Group 1, group 2 and the halogens

## Simple idea
Elements in the same column have the same number of outer electrons, so they react alike. Group 1 metals are keen to lose one electron, and get keener going down. Halogens (group 17) are keen to gain one, and get less keen going down. Like a family with the same habits, but sizes differ.

## Key formulas
- 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ (sodium, Na, makes hydrogen gas, H₂, in water)
- Group 1 forms 1+ ions, group 2 forms 2+ ions, halogens form 1− ions
- Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂ (chlorine pushes bromine out)
- Group 1 and 2: reactivity increases down. Halogens: reactivity decreases down

## Common mistakes
- Saying halogens get more reactive down the group: they get less reactive, and fluorine is the most reactive.
- Mixing up the 1+ and 2+ ions: group 1 loses 1 electron, group 2 loses 2.
- Thinking a less reactive halogen can push out a more reactive one: only the more reactive one wins.

## Check questions
1. Which is more reactive with water, lithium or potassium?
   Answer: Potassium.
2. What ion does magnesium (group 2) form?
   Answer: Mg²⁺.
3. Why does reactivity of group 1 metals increase down the group?
   Answer: The outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is lost more easily.

## Flashcards
Q: How many outer electrons do group 1 metals have?
A: One.
Q: What gas forms when group 1 metals react with water?
A: Hydrogen.
Q: Which halogen is the most reactive?
A: Fluorine.
Q: Which group 1 metal is the most reactive of lithium, sodium and potassium?
A: Potassium.
Q: What charge does a halogen ion have?
A: 1−.
