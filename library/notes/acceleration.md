# Acceleration

## Simple idea
Acceleration is how fast your velocity changes. A car pulling away from the lights speeds up, so it accelerates. Braking is acceleration too, just pointing backwards. Turning a corner at steady speed also counts, because the direction changes.

## Key formulas
- a = (v − u) ÷ t. a: acceleration (m/s²), u: starting velocity (m/s), v: final velocity (m/s), t: time taken (s)
- a = Δv ÷ t. Δv: change in velocity (m/s)

## Picture it
Draw `car` facing right. Arrow from `car.front` pointing right labelled "a = 2 m/s²". Label above `car.roof`: "speeding up". Draw a second arrow from `car.back` pointing right, labelled "velocity".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking big speed means big acceleration: a car at steady 100 km/h has zero acceleration.
- Calling slowing down "negative acceleration" always: it depends on which way is positive.
- Using km/h with seconds: change to m/s first.

## Check questions
1. A bike goes from 0 to 6 m/s in 3 s. What is its acceleration?
   Answer: 2 m/s².
2. A car slows from 20 m/s to 10 m/s in 5 s. What is a?
   Answer: −2 m/s², so 2 m/s² backwards.
3. Why is a car going round a bend at steady speed accelerating?
   Answer: Its direction changes, so its velocity changes.

## Flashcards
Q: What is acceleration?
A: The rate of change of velocity.
Q: Unit of acceleration?
A: Metres per second squared, m/s².
Q: What does a = 3 m/s² mean?
A: Velocity goes up by 3 m/s every second.
Q: Is acceleration a scalar or vector?
A: A vector.
Q: Name three ways to accelerate.
A: Speed up, slow down, or change direction.

## Root check
- displacement-velocity: A runner covers 12 m in 4 s in a straight line. What is the velocity? Answer: 3 m/s.
