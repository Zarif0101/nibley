# Shapes of molecules

## Simple idea
Pairs of electrons around a central atom all have negative charge, so they push each other away, like balloons tied together. They settle as far apart as they can. That spacing gives the molecule its shape. Lone pairs (pairs not in a bond) push a little harder than bonds.

## Key formulas
- 2 bonds, no lone pairs: linear, 180° (CO₂)
- 3 bonds, no lone pairs: trigonal planar, 120°
- 4 bonds, no lone pairs: tetrahedral, 109.5° (CH₄)
- 3 bonds + 1 lone pair: pyramidal, about 107° (NH₃)
- 2 bonds + 2 lone pairs: bent, 104.5° (H₂O)

## Common mistakes
- Drawing water as a straight line: its 2 lone pairs bend it.
- Thinking the bond angle in CH₄ is 90°: the four bonds spread to 109.5° in 3D.
- Ignoring lone pairs when counting: count them too, since they take up space.

## Check questions
1. What shape is methane, CH₄?
   Answer: Tetrahedral, with 109.5° bond angles.
2. What angle do three bonds make in a flat trigonal planar molecule?
   Answer: 120°.
3. Why is water bent and not linear?
   Answer: Its two lone pairs repel the bonds and push them closer together.

## Flashcards
Q: What rule decides molecule shape?
A: Electron pairs repel and spread as far apart as possible.
Q: What is the shape of CO₂?
A: Linear, 180°.
Q: What is the bond angle in water?
A: About 104.5°.
Q: What is the shape of ammonia, NH₃?
A: Pyramidal.
Q: What is a lone pair?
A: A pair of outer electrons not used in a bond.
