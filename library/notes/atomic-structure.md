# Atomic structure and isotopes

## Simple idea
An atom is mostly empty space. In the middle is a tiny, heavy nucleus made of protons and neutrons. Electrons whizz around it. Picture a pea in the middle of a football stadium: the pea is the nucleus. The number of protons names the element. Isotopes are the same element with different numbers of neutrons.

## Key formulas
- Atomic number Z = number of protons
- Mass number A = protons + neutrons
- Neutrons = A − Z
- Neutral atom: electrons = protons
- Charges: proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1

## Picture it
Draw `nucleus` in the middle of `atom`. Label `nucleus.proton` "proton, +" and `nucleus.neutron` "neutron, no charge".
Label `atom.electron` "electron, −" and `atom.orbit-1` "electron shell".

## Common mistakes
- Saying neutrons have a charge: they have none, so their charge is 0.
- Mixing up mass number and atomic number: the atomic number counts only protons.
- Thinking isotopes are different elements: same protons, so same element and same chemistry.

## Check questions
1. How many neutrons are in carbon-14 (Z = 6, A = 14)?
   Answer: 8, because 14 − 6 = 8.
2. A neutral atom has 11 protons. How many electrons does it have?
   Answer: 11, so the charges cancel.
3. Why do carbon-12 and carbon-14 behave alike in reactions?
   Answer: They have the same number of protons and electrons.

## Flashcards
Q: Where are protons and neutrons found?
A: In the nucleus, at the centre of the atom.
Q: What does the atomic number tell you?
A: The number of protons.
Q: What is the mass number?
A: Protons plus neutrons.
Q: What are isotopes?
A: Atoms of one element with different numbers of neutrons.
Q: What is the charge on an electron?
A: Negative, −1.

## Root check
- atoms-elements-compounds: Is a substance made of only one kind of atom an element or a compound? Answer: An element.
