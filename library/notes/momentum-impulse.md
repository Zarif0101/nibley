# Momentum and impulse

## Simple idea
A rolling bowling ball is harder to stop than a tennis ball at the same speed. Momentum measures that "hard to stop" amount: mass times velocity. To change it you need a push over some time. That push-times-time is called impulse. A longer time means a gentler force.

## Key formulas
- p = m·v. p: momentum (kg·m/s), m: mass (kg), v: velocity (m/s)
- J = F·t. J: impulse (N·s), F: force (N), t: time the force acts (s)
- J = Δp = m·v − m·u. Δp: change in momentum (kg·m/s), u: starting velocity, v: final velocity (m/s)

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting direction: momentum is a vector, so left is negative if right is positive.
- Mixing units: impulse is N·s, the same as kg·m/s.
- Using the final velocity only: impulse is the change in momentum.

## Check questions
1. A 2 kg ball moves at 3 m/s. What is its momentum?
   Answer: 6 kg·m/s.
2. A 10 N force acts for 0.5 s. What is the impulse?
   Answer: 5 N·s.
3. Why do airbags make a crash safer?
   Answer: They lengthen the stopping time, so the force is smaller for the same change in momentum.

## Flashcards
Q: Formula for momentum?
A: p = m·v, mass times velocity.
Q: Unit of momentum?
A: Kilogram metres per second, kg·m/s.
Q: Formula for impulse?
A: J = F·t, force times time.
Q: Impulse equals what?
A: The change in momentum.
Q: Is momentum a scalar or vector?
A: A vector, it points the way the object moves.

## Root check
- newtons-second-law: A 4 kg box has a net force of 8 N. What is its acceleration? Answer: 2 m/s².
