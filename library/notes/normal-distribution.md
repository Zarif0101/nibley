# Normal distribution

## Simple idea
Measure the heights of lots of people and most are near the average, a few are very short or very tall. Draw that as a bar graph and you get a smooth bell shape, the same on both sides of the middle. The spread tells you how wide the bell is.

## Key formulas
- μ: the mean, the centre of the bell. σ: the standard deviation, the typical spread (both in the data's unit)
- z = (x − μ)/σ. x: a value, z: how many σ it is from the mean (no unit)
- About 68% of values lie within 1σ of μ, 95% within 2σ, and 99.7% within 3σ
- Total area under the curve is 1

## Picture it
Draw `graph-bell`. Label `graph-bell.mean` "mean μ" and `graph-bell.peak` "most values here".
Label `graph-bell.sd-left` "μ − σ" and `graph-bell.sd-right` "μ + σ"; write "68% in between" in the middle.

## Common mistakes
- Using the variance where σ is needed: take the square root of variance.
- Thinking the curve touches zero: the tails get close but never reach it.
- Mixing up μ and σ: μ is the centre, σ is the width.

## Check questions
1. Mean 100, σ = 15. What is z for x = 130?
   Answer: 2.
2. About what share of values lie between 85 and 115 when μ = 100 and σ = 15?
   Answer: About 68%.
3. Why is half of the data above the mean?
   Answer: The bell is symmetric around the mean.

## Flashcards
Q: What does μ stand for?
A: The mean, the centre of the distribution.
Q: What does σ stand for?
A: The standard deviation, the spread.
Q: Write the z-score formula.
A: z = (x − μ)/σ.
Q: What share of values is within 2σ of the mean?
A: About 95%.
Q: What is the total area under a normal curve?
A: 1.
