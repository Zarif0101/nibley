# Total internal reflection

## Simple idea
Look up from under water in a swimming pool and the surface can act like a mirror. When light inside glass hits the edge at a steep slant, it cannot escape into air. All of it bounces back inside. That is how light stays trapped in optical fibres.

## Key formulas
- sin C = n₂ / n₁. C: critical angle (degrees), n₁: index of the denser material, n₂: index of the less dense one (no units)
- sin C = 1 / n. Glass or water into air, where n is the index of the material

Draw `tir`. Arrow from `tir.ray-in-start` to `tir.hit-point` labelled "angle bigger than C". Arrow from `tir.hit-point` to `tir.ray-out-end` labelled "all light reflected". Label `tir.block-center` "glass".

## Common mistakes
- Expecting it going air to glass: it only happens from denser to less dense.
- Using any angle: the angle must be bigger than the critical angle C.
- Measuring from the surface: measure angles from the normal.

## Check questions
1. Glass has n = 2.0. What is its critical angle against air?
   Answer: 30°, since sin C = 0.5.
2. Which two conditions are needed for total internal reflection?
   Answer: Light goes from denser to less dense material, and the angle is bigger than C.
3. Why does light stay inside an optical fibre?
   Answer: It hits the wall at angles bigger than C, so it reflects fully again and again.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the critical angle?
A: The angle in the denser material where the refracted ray runs along the surface.
Q: At the critical angle, what is the refracted angle?
A: 90°.
Q: Name one use of total internal reflection.
A: Optical fibres, or prisms in binoculars.
Q: Does total internal reflection lose light?
A: Almost none; all of it reflects.
Q: Is C bigger or smaller for higher n?
A: Smaller.

## Root check
- refraction: Light goes from air into glass. Does it bend toward or away from the normal? Answer: Toward the normal.
