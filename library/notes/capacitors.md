# Capacitors

## Simple idea
A capacitor is like a small rechargeable tank for charge. It has two metal plates with a gap between them. A battery pushes charge on, one plate gets positive and the other negative, and energy is stored in the gap. A bigger capacitance holds more charge for each volt.

## Key formulas
- C = Q / V. C: capacitance (farads, F), Q: charge on one plate (coulombs, C), V: voltage across it (volts, V)
- E = ½·C·V². E: stored energy (joules, J)
- C = ε₀·A / d. A: plate area (m²), d: gap (m), ε₀ ≈ 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m

## Common mistakes
- Saying the capacitor holds net charge: the plates hold +Q and −Q, so net is zero.
- Using microfarads in formulas: change 1 μF to 10⁻⁶ F first.
- Forgetting the ½ in the energy formula: E = ½·C·V².

## Check questions
1. A 10 μF capacitor is at 6 V. How much charge is stored?
   Answer: Q = 10 × 10⁻⁶ × 6 = 60 μC.
2. A 10 μF capacitor at 6 V. How much energy is stored?
   Answer: E = ½ × 10 × 10⁻⁶ × 36 = 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ J.
3. Why does a smaller gap give a larger capacitance?
   Answer: C = ε₀A/d, so a smaller d makes C larger.

## Flashcards
Q: What is capacitance?
A: Charge stored per volt, C = Q / V.
Q: What is the unit of capacitance?
A: The farad (F).
Q: What is the energy stored in a capacitor?
A: E = ½·C·V².
Q: What happens to C if the plate area doubles?
A: It doubles.
Q: What two charges sit on the plates?
A: +Q on one plate and −Q on the other.
