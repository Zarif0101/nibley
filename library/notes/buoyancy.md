# Buoyancy

## Simple idea
Push a ball down into a bath and you feel water pushing back up. That upward push is buoyancy (upthrust). It equals the weight of the water the ball pushes out of the way. If that push beats the ball's weight, the ball floats up.

## Key formulas
- U = ρ·g·V. U: upthrust (N), ρ: fluid density (kg/m³), g: 9.8 (m/s²), V: volume of fluid pushed aside (m³)
- floating: U = W. W: weight of the object (N)

## Picture it
Draw `block` sitting in `beaker`, with its bottom below `beaker.liquid-surface`. Arrow up from `block.bottom`, labelled "upthrust". Arrow down from `block.center`, labelled "weight".
Label at `beaker.liquid-mid`: "water pushed aside".

## Common mistakes
- Using the object's volume when it is only part-sunk: use only the part under the surface.
- Using the object's density in the formula: use the fluid's density.
- Thinking heavy objects always sink: only if their average density beats the fluid's.

## Check questions
1. What is upthrust equal to?
   Answer: The weight of the fluid pushed aside.
2. A block of volume 0.002 m³ is fully under water (1000 kg/m³). What is the upthrust?
   Answer: 1000 × 9.8 × 0.002 = 19.6 N.
3. Why does a huge steel ship float?
   Answer: Its shape pushes aside lots of water, so upthrust equals its weight.

## Flashcards
Q: State Archimedes' principle.
A: Upthrust equals the weight of fluid displaced.
Q: What is the formula for upthrust?
A: U = ρ·g·V.
Q: When does an object float steadily?
A: When upthrust equals its weight.
Q: Which density goes in the upthrust formula?
A: The density of the fluid.
Q: Object's average density is less than the fluid's. What happens?
A: It floats.

## Root check
- fluid-pressure: What is the pressure at 3 m deep in water (1000 kg/m³)? Answer: 1000 × 9.8 × 3 = 29 400 Pa.
