# Density

## Simple idea
Density tells you how much stuff is squashed into a space. A rock and a sponge can be the same size, but the rock is heavier because its particles are packed closer. Things less dense than water float, and things more dense sink.

## Key formulas
- ρ = m / V. ρ: density (kg/m³), m: mass (kg), V: volume (m³)
- m = ρ·V. Mass from density and volume
- V = m / ρ. Volume from mass and density
- Water has density 1000 kg/m³ (the same as 1 g/cm³)

## Common mistakes
- Dividing the wrong way: density is mass divided by volume.
- Mixing units: keep kg with m³, or g with cm³.
- Thinking heavy things always sink: it depends on density, not total mass.

## Check questions
1. A block has mass 500 kg and volume 2 m³. What is its density?
   Answer: 250 kg/m³.
2. What is the mass of 3 m³ of water?
   Answer: 3000 kg (1000 × 3).
3. Why does a wooden log float on water?
   Answer: Wood is less dense than water.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the formula for density?
A: Density = mass ÷ volume.
Q: What is the SI unit of density?
A: Kilograms per cubic metre (kg/m³).
Q: What is the density of water?
A: 1000 kg/m³.
Q: A material is denser than water. Does it float or sink?
A: It sinks.
Q: How do you find mass from density?
A: Mass = density × volume.
