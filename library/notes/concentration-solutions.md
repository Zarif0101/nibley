# Concentration and titration

## Simple idea
Concentration says how much solute is packed into a solution, like squash: lots of squash in a cup is strong, a little is weak. We measure it as moles of dissolved substance in each dm³ (one litre). A titration adds a known solution drop by drop until it exactly reacts with an unknown one.

## Key formulas
- c = n / V. c: concentration (mol/dm³), n: amount (mol), V: volume (dm³)
- n = c × V
- V in dm³ = V in cm³ / 1000
- At the end point, use the equation's ratio. HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O is 1 : 1

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to change cm³ to dm³: divide by 1000 first.
- Using the volume of acid as the answer: find moles first, then divide by the flask volume.
- Skipping the ratio: if the equation is not 1 : 1, scale the moles.

## Check questions
1. What is the concentration of 0.5 mol in 2 dm³?
   Answer: 0.25 mol/dm³.
2. How many moles are in 250 cm³ of 0.20 mol/dm³ solution?
   Answer: 0.050 mol.
3. Why do we divide cm³ by 1000?
   Answer: Concentration uses dm³, and 1 dm³ is 1000 cm³.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the formula for concentration?
A: c = n / V.
Q: What is the unit of concentration?
A: mol/dm³ (the same as mol/L).
Q: How many cm³ are in 1 dm³?
A: 1000.
Q: What is a titration?
A: Adding a known solution until it exactly reacts with an unknown one.
Q: What shows the end point of a titration?
A: A colour change in the indicator.
