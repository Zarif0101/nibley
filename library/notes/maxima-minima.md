# Maxima and minima

## Simple idea
Walk over a hill. Going up, the slope is positive. At the very top, you are flat for a moment: slope zero. Then you go down, slope negative. So peaks and valleys sit where the slope is zero. Find them by solving f′(x) = 0, then check whether each is a top or a bottom.

## Key formulas
- Stationary point: f′(x) = 0. f′: the slope formula (derivative) of f
- f″(x) > 0 means a minimum (valley). f″(x) < 0 means a maximum (peak). f″: derivative of f′
- Plug x back into f(x) to find the height

## Picture it
Draw `graph-cubic`. Mark `graph-cubic.local-max` "maximum, slope 0" and `graph-cubic.local-min` "minimum, slope 0".
Draw short flat tangent lines at both points.

## Common mistakes
- Stopping at f′(x) = 0: also find the y value and decide max or min.
- Thinking every flat spot is a peak or valley: x³ is flat at 0 but just keeps rising.
- Mixing up the sign test: f″ > 0 is a valley, f″ < 0 is a peak.

## Check questions
1. What is the slope at the top of a smooth peak?
   Answer: Zero.
2. Find the minimum of y = x² − 6x + 5.
   Answer: y′ = 2x − 6 = 0, so x = 3. Then y = 9 − 18 + 5 = −4.
3. Why does f″ > 0 mean a minimum?
   Answer: The slope is increasing, so the curve bends up like a bowl.

## Flashcards
Q: Where do maxima and minima of a smooth curve occur?
A: Where the slope f′(x) is zero.
Q: What does f″(x) > 0 tell you?
A: The point is a minimum.
Q: What does f″(x) < 0 tell you?
A: The point is a maximum.
Q: For y = x³ − 3x, where is the slope zero?
A: At x = 1 and x = −1.
Q: What is a stationary point?
A: A point where the slope is zero.

## Root check
- differentiation-rules: Differentiate x³ − 3x. Answer: 3x² − 3.
- quadratics: Solve x² − 4 = 0. Answer: x = 2 or x = −2.
