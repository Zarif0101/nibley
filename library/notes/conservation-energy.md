# Conservation of energy

## Simple idea
Energy never disappears; it only changes form. On a swing, at the top you are high and slow. At the bottom you are low and fast. Height energy turned into movement energy, and then back. Add them up at any moment and you get the same total, if friction is ignored.

## Key formulas
- KE + GPE = constant (no friction). KE: kinetic energy (J), GPE: potential energy (J)
- ½·m·v² = m·g·h. v: speed at the bottom (m/s), m: mass (kg), g: 9.8 (m/s²), h: height dropped (m)
- v = √(2·g·h). The same law, solved for speed (m/s)

## Picture it
Draw `pendulum` with the bob lifted at `pendulum.angle`, labelled "high: most GPE". Label at `pendulum.rest-position`: "low: most KE".
Or draw `ball` at `ramp.slope-top` labelled "all GPE" and at `ramp.slope-bottom` labelled "all KE".

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting friction turns some energy into heat: then KE + GPE shrinks.
- Thinking mass changes the speed of a fall: it cancels out of v = √(2gh).
- Using the slope length as h: use the straight-up height.

## Check questions
1. What happens to a falling ball's GPE and KE?
   Answer: GPE goes down, KE goes up, and the total stays the same.
2. A ball falls 5 m from rest. About how fast is it going at the bottom?
   Answer: v = √(2 × 9.8 × 5) = √98 ≈ 9.9 m/s.
3. Why does a swing stop after a while?
   Answer: Friction and air resistance turn energy into heat.

## Flashcards
Q: State the law of conservation of energy.
A: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed in form.
Q: At the top of a pendulum swing, what is the speed?
A: Zero for a moment, so all energy is GPE.
Q: Where is a pendulum's KE largest?
A: At the bottom of the swing.
Q: Where does energy go when friction acts?
A: Into heat energy.
Q: What formula gives speed after falling height h?
A: v = √(2·g·h).

## Root check
- kinetic-energy: What is the kinetic energy of a 2 kg ball at 4 m/s? Answer: 16 J.
- potential-energy: How much GPE does a 2 kg ball gain when lifted 5 m? Answer: 98 J.
