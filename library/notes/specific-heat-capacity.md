# Specific heat capacity

## Simple idea
Some stuff is easy to warm up and some is stubborn. Water is stubborn: a pan of water takes ages to heat, while a metal spoon in it gets hot fast. Specific heat capacity says how much energy 1 kg of a material needs to warm up by 1 degree.

## Key formulas
- Q = m·c·ΔT. Q: heat energy added (joules, J), m: mass (kilograms, kg), c: specific heat capacity (J/(kg·K)), ΔT: temperature change (K or °C)
- Water has c = 4200 J/(kg·K)

## Picture it
Draw `beaker` over `burner`. Arrow from `burner.flame-tip` up to `beaker.bottom`, labelled "heat Q in".
Draw `thermometer` beside `beaker.liquid-mid`, labelled "ΔT".

## Common mistakes
- Using grams: change mass to kilograms first.
- Using the final temperature as ΔT: ΔT is final minus start.
- Thinking a big c means it heats fast: big c means it heats slowly.

## Check questions
1. What is Q for 2 kg of water warmed by 5 K? (c = 4200)
   Answer: 2 × 4200 × 5 = 42 000 J.
2. A 0.5 kg metal has c = 400 J/(kg·K). How much heat warms it by 10 K?
   Answer: 0.5 × 400 × 10 = 2000 J.
3. Why does a beach sand get hot faster than the sea in the sun?
   Answer: Sand has a lower c, so the same energy warms it more.

## Flashcards
Q: What does specific heat capacity mean?
A: Energy to warm 1 kg of a material by 1 degree.
Q: Unit of c?
A: J/(kg·K).
Q: Formula for heat needed to change temperature?
A: Q = m·c·ΔT.
Q: Value of c for water?
A: 4200 J/(kg·K).
Q: Material with high c: heats fast or slowly?
A: Slowly.
