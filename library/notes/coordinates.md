# Coordinates and plotting

## Simple idea
A treasure map says "3 steps across, 2 steps up". That is the point (3, 2). The first number is always across, the second is up. The two lines meet at the origin, (0, 0). Going left or down makes the number negative.

## Key formulas
- (x, y). x: steps across (right is +, left is −), y: steps up (up is +, down is −)
- Midpoint of (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is ((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2)

## Picture it
Draw `axes`. Label `axes.origin` "(0, 0)". Draw a dot 3 across and 2 up, with dashed lines to `axes.x-mid` and `axes.y-mid`, labelled "(3, 2)".
Label `axes.x-end` "x" and `axes.y-end` "y".

## Common mistakes
- Swapping x and y: say "across, then up".
- Going the wrong way for negatives: −2 across means 2 to the left.
- Counting from the point you last plotted: always count from the origin.

## Check questions
1. What are the coordinates of the origin?
   Answer: (0, 0).
2. What is the midpoint of (0, 0) and (4, 6)?
   Answer: (2, 3).
3. Why does everyone write x first?
   Answer: It is an agreed rule, so any reader plots the same point.

## Flashcards
Q: Which axis is horizontal?
A: The x-axis.
Q: Which axis is vertical?
A: The y-axis.
Q: Where is the point (−2, 3)?
A: 2 left of the origin and 3 up.
Q: What is y for any point on the x-axis?
A: 0.
Q: What does the first number in (x, y) mean?
A: How far across, left or right.
