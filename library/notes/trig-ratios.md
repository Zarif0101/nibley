# Trigonometric ratios

## Simple idea
In a right triangle, once the angle is fixed, the sides always keep the same ratio, big or small. Pick one angle, θ. The side across from it is "opposite", the side touching it is "adjacent", and the longest side is the hypotenuse. Sine, cosine and tangent are three of these ratios.

## Key formulas
- sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse
- cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse
- tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent
- θ: the angle (degrees, °). Sides are in the same unit, so ratios have no unit
- Memory trick: SOH CAH TOA

## Picture it
Draw `right-triangle`. Mark `right-triangle.angle-a` as "θ".
Label `right-triangle.side-mid` "opposite", `right-triangle.base-mid` "adjacent" and `right-triangle.hypotenuse-mid` "hypotenuse".

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up opposite and adjacent: opposite is across from the angle.
- Calculator in the wrong mode: set it to degrees.
- Using sin, cos or tan on a triangle with no right angle.

## Check questions
1. What does SOH stand for?
   Answer: sin = opposite ÷ hypotenuse.
2. Opposite is 3 and hypotenuse is 6. What is sin θ?
   Answer: 0.5 (so θ = 30°).
3. Why does the ratio not depend on triangle size?
   Answer: A bigger triangle with the same angles is a scaled copy.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the formula for tan θ?
A: Opposite ÷ adjacent.
Q: What is the formula for cos θ?
A: Adjacent ÷ hypotenuse.
Q: What is sin 30°?
A: 0.5.
Q: What is tan 45°?
A: 1.
Q: Which side is the hypotenuse?
A: The longest side, opposite the right angle.

## Root check
- pythagoras: A right triangle has short sides 3 and 4. How long is the hypotenuse? Answer: 5.
- ratios-percentages: Write the ratio 4:8 in simplest form. Answer: 1:2.
