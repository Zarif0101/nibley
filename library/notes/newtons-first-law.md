# Newton's first law

## Simple idea
A book on a desk stays put until you push it. A puck on smooth ice keeps gliding until something slows it. Things keep doing what they are already doing, resting or moving in a straight line at steady speed, unless an unbalanced (net) force acts. This habit is called inertia.

## Key formulas
- none

## Picture it
Draw `block` on `ground`. Arrow from `block.top` pointing up labelled "normal force" and an arrow from `block.bottom` pointing down labelled "weight". Label `block.center`: "forces balance, no change".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a moving object needs a force to keep moving: it keeps going by itself.
- Thinking "no motion" means no forces: forces can be there but balanced.
- Thinking inertia is a force: it is the habit of keeping the same motion.

## Check questions
1. A hockey puck slides at steady speed on frictionless ice. What is the net force?
   Answer: Zero.
2. Why do you lurch forward when a bus brakes suddenly?
   Answer: Your body keeps moving forward while the bus slows.
3. A box at rest has 5 N pushing right and 5 N pushing left. Does it move?
   Answer: No, the forces balance so it stays at rest.

## Flashcards
Q: State Newton's first law.
A: With zero net force, an object keeps its rest or steady motion.
Q: What is inertia?
A: An object's resistance to changes in its motion.
Q: What does balanced forces mean?
A: The net force is zero.
Q: Which objects have more inertia?
A: Ones with more mass.
Q: Can an object move with zero net force?
A: Yes, at constant velocity in a straight line.
