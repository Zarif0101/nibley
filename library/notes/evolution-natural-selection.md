# Evolution by natural selection

## Simple idea
In any group, living things differ a little. Some differences help them survive, like a moth that blends in with tree bark. Those moths get eaten less, so they breed more and pass on the helpful trait. Over many generations, the whole group changes. This slow change is evolution.

## Key formulas
- Variation + competition + survival of the best fitted + inheritance → change in the group
- Mutation and sexual reproduction make the variation
- Fitness = how many offspring survive to breed

## Common mistakes
- Saying individuals evolve: groups change over generations, not one animal.
- Thinking animals change on purpose: helpful traits come from random variation.
- Thinking survival of the fittest means strongest: it means best suited to the place.

## Check questions
1. What is the source of new variation?
   Answer: Mutations (plus mixing genes in sexual reproduction).
2. Dark moths survive better on dark trees. What happens to the group over time?
   Answer: More moths become dark.
3. Why must a trait be inherited to be selected?
   Answer: Only inherited traits pass to offspring and build up over generations.

## Flashcards
Q: Who proposed natural selection?
A: Charles Darwin.
Q: What is a species?
A: A group that can breed and have fertile offspring.
Q: What is adaptation?
A: A trait that helps an organism survive and breed.
Q: What does a phylogenetic tree show?
A: How living things are related through shared ancestors.
Q: Name one thing that can drive selection.
A: Predators, disease, food shortage or climate.

## Root check
- mutations: Where does a new allele first come from? Answer: A mutation in DNA.
- mendelian-genetics: Which type of allele shows in a heterozygote? Answer: The dominant allele.
