# Simple harmonic motion

## Simple idea
Pull a bouncing spring down and let go. It moves up and down about the middle, faster in the middle and stopping at each end. The further you pull, the harder it pulls back. That back-and-forth motion, with the pull back growing with distance from the middle, is simple harmonic motion.

## Key formulas
- F = −k·x. F: restoring force (newtons, N), k: spring stiffness (N/m), x: distance from the middle (m); minus means it pulls back
- T = 1 / f. T: period, time for one full swing (seconds, s), f: frequency (hertz, Hz)
- T = 2π√(m/k) for a mass on a spring. m: mass (kg)

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the minus sign: the force always points back to the middle.
- Thinking it is fastest at the ends: it is fastest in the middle, stopped at the ends.
- Mixing T and f: T = 1 / f.

## Check questions
1. A spring swings 2 times each second. What is its period?
   Answer: 0.5 s.
2. A 1 kg mass sits on a spring with k = 4 N/m. What is T?
   Answer: 2π√(1/4) = π ≈ 3.1 s.
3. Why does the mass slow down towards the ends?
   Answer: The pull back is biggest there, so it slows, stops and turns round.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the period?
A: The time for one full swing.
Q: Formula linking period and frequency?
A: T = 1 / f.
Q: Where is the speed greatest?
A: At the middle position.
Q: Where does the restoring force point?
A: Towards the middle.
Q: Unit of frequency?
A: The hertz (Hz), swings per second.

## Root check
- elasticity: A spring has k = 50 N/m and is stretched 0.2 m. What force does it pull back with? Answer: F = k·x = 10 N.
- newtons-second-law: A 3 kg object has a net force of 12 N. What is its acceleration? Answer: 4 m/s².
- trig-functions: What is sin 30°? Answer: 0.5.
