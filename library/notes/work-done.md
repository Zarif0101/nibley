# Work done

## Simple idea
In physics, work means a force moves something. Push a heavy box across the floor and you do work. Hold it still and your arms get tired, but no work is done, because it did not move. Only the part of the force pointing along the movement counts.

## Key formulas
- W = F·d. W: work (joules, J), F: force along the movement (newtons, N), d: distance moved (metres, m)
- W = F·d·cos θ. θ: angle between force and movement (degrees)

## Picture it
Draw `block` on `ground`. Arrow from `block.right` pointing right, labelled "force F". Arrow along `ground.mid` pointing right, labelled "distance d".
Tilted force: draw `vector-arrow` with its `vector-arrow.tail` at `block.top-right`, labelled "force at angle θ".

## Common mistakes
- Using distance when the force is sideways: only the part along the movement counts (cos θ).
- Saying holding something still is work: no movement, no work.
- Forgetting units: distance in metres, force in newtons, answer in joules.

## Check questions
1. What is the unit of work?
   Answer: The joule (J), which is 1 N·m.
2. You push a box with 10 N for 3 m. How much work?
   Answer: 10 × 3 = 30 J.
3. Why does a waiter carrying a tray level do no work on the tray?
   Answer: The lift force is up, the movement is sideways, so cos 90° = 0.

## Flashcards
Q: What is work in physics?
A: Force times distance moved in the force's direction.
Q: What is 1 joule?
A: The work done by 1 N moving something 1 m.
Q: What is the work when force is at 90° to movement?
A: Zero.
Q: Is work a vector or a scalar?
A: A scalar. It has size but no direction.
Q: What happens to the energy when you do work on an object?
A: Energy is transferred to it.
