# Tangents and normals

## Simple idea
A tangent is a straight line that just touches a curve at one point and has the same steepness there. Like a skateboard wheel touching a ramp. The normal goes through the same point at a right angle to the tangent, like a pin pushed straight into the ramp.

## Key formulas
- Tangent slope m = f′(x₁). x₁: the x of the touch point, m: slope (no unit)
- Line through a point: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). y₁: the y of the touch point
- Normal slope = −1/m. Slopes of perpendicular lines multiply to −1

## Common mistakes
- Using y instead of the derivative for the slope: the slope is f′(x₁).
- Using the tangent slope for the normal: flip it and change the sign.
- Skipping the y value of the point: put x₁ into the curve to get y₁.

## Check questions
1. What is the slope of the tangent to y = x² at x = 3?
   Answer: dy/dx = 2x = 6.
2. A tangent has slope 4. What is the normal's slope?
   Answer: −1/4.
3. Why is the normal slope −1/m?
   Answer: Perpendicular lines have slopes that multiply to −1.

## Flashcards
Q: What is a tangent?
A: A line touching the curve at one point with the same slope.
Q: What is a normal?
A: A line through the touch point at right angles to the tangent.
Q: How do you find the tangent slope?
A: Put the x value into the derivative.
Q: What is the tangent to y = x² at x = 2?
A: y = 4x − 4.
Q: A tangent is horizontal. What is the normal?
A: A vertical line.

## Root check
- differentiation-rules: Differentiate 3x². Answer: 6x.
- straight-lines: Give the line through (0, 1) with slope 3. Answer: y = 3x + 1.
