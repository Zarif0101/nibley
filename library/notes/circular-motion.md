# Circular motion

## Simple idea
Something moving in a circle keeps changing direction, so it keeps accelerating, even at steady speed. Swing a bucket on a rope: the rope pulls it toward your hand all the time. Let go and the bucket flies off in a straight line. That inward pull is the centripetal force.

## Key formulas
- a = v² / r. a: centripetal acceleration (m/s²), v: speed (m/s), r: radius (m)
- F = m·v² / r. F: force toward the centre (N), m: mass (kg)
- v = 2π·r / T. T: time for one circle (s)
- ω = v / r. ω: angular speed (rad/s)

## Picture it
Draw `ball` on the edge of `circle`. Arrow from `circle.edge-right` pointing up, labelled "velocity (tangent)". Arrow from `circle.edge-right` pointing left to `circle.center`, labelled "centripetal force".

## Common mistakes
- Drawing an outward force: the real force points to the centre.
- Saying steady speed means no acceleration: direction changes, so velocity changes.
- Forgetting to square v in a = v² / r.

## Check questions
1. Which way does the net force point in circular motion?
   Answer: Toward the centre of the circle.
2. A 2 kg ball moves at 3 m/s in a circle of radius 1.5 m. What is the force?
   Answer: F = 2 × 9 ÷ 1.5 = 12 N.
3. Why does a ball on a string fly off straight if the string snaps?
   Answer: No force pulls it inward, so it keeps its velocity along the tangent.

## Flashcards
Q: What is centripetal force?
A: The force toward the centre that keeps something in a circle.
Q: What is the formula for centripetal acceleration?
A: a = v² / r.
Q: Speed doubles, same radius. What happens to the force?
A: It becomes four times bigger.
Q: Which way does velocity point in a circle?
A: Along the tangent, at right angles to the radius.
Q: What is ω?
A: Angular speed: the angle turned per second, in rad/s.

## Root check
- newtons-second-law: A 3 kg object has a 12 N net force. What is its acceleration? Answer: 4 m/s².
- radians: How many radians are in one full circle? Answer: 2π, about 6.28.
