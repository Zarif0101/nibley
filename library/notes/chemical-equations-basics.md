# Writing and balancing equations

## Simple idea
A chemical equation is a recipe. Reactants on the left turn into products on the right. Atoms never vanish or appear, so each kind of atom must be counted the same on both sides. Like a balance scale: if one side has more, add big numbers in front until it levels.

## Key formulas
- Reactants → products. The arrow means "turns into"
- 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. The big number in front counts whole molecules
- 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. Magnesium (Mg) burning in oxygen (O₂) to make magnesium oxide (MgO)
- Never change the small numbers inside a formula, only the big ones in front

## Common mistakes
- Changing a small number (H₂O to H₂O₂): that makes a different substance, so only change the big numbers in front.
- Balancing one element and stopping: recount every element at the end.
- Forgetting a big number multiplies the whole formula: 2H₂O has 4 H and 2 O.

## Check questions
1. Balance: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O.
   Answer: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
2. In 3H₂O, how many hydrogen atoms and oxygen atoms are there?
   Answer: 6 hydrogen atoms and 3 oxygen atoms.
3. Why can't we just change a small number to balance?
   Answer: It changes the substance itself, but balancing only changes how many molecules.

## Flashcards
Q: What goes on the left of the arrow?
A: The reactants, the substances you start with.
Q: What goes on the right of the arrow?
A: The products, the new substances made.
Q: What must be equal on both sides?
A: The number of each kind of atom.
Q: Which numbers may you change when balancing?
A: The big numbers in front of formulas.
Q: What is the balanced equation for making water from hydrogen and oxygen?
A: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
