# Kidneys and excretion

## Simple idea
Your kidneys are like a coffee filter and a recycler in one. Blood is squeezed through tiny filters. Waste, like urea, goes out in urine. Useful things, like glucose, are taken back. The kidney also decides how much water to keep, so your blood stays balanced.

## Key formulas
- blood → filtered at glomerulus → useful things reabsorbed → urine (urea, extra water, salts)
- excess amino acids → urea, made in the liver

## Picture it
Draw `nephron`. Arrow into `nephron.glomerulus`, labelled "blood filtered here". Label `nephron.loop`: "water taken back". Arrow from `nephron.collecting-duct`, labelled "urine to bladder".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking kidneys make urea: the liver makes it, kidneys remove it.
- Thinking all filtered stuff leaves: glucose is reabsorbed, so none should be in urine.
- Mixing up excretion and egestion: excretion removes body waste, egestion removes undigested food.

## Check questions
1. What waste does the kidney remove from blood?
   Answer: Urea.
2. Where does filtering of blood happen in the nephron?
   Answer: At the glomerulus.
3. Why does your urine get darker when you drink less water?
   Answer: Your body keeps more water, so the urine is more concentrated.

## Flashcards
Q: What is excretion?
A: Removing waste made by the body's own cells.
Q: Where is urea made?
A: In the liver.
Q: What is reabsorbed back into the blood?
A: Glucose, useful salts and most of the water.
Q: What hormone controls water in urine?
A: ADH.
Q: Where is urine stored?
A: In the bladder.
