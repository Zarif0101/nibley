# Latent heat

## Simple idea
When you heat ice at 0 °C, it does not get warmer at first. It melts. All the energy goes into pulling particles apart, not into making them move faster. Boiling water works the same way. Latent heat is the energy needed to change state while the temperature stays the same.

## Key formulas
- Q = m·L. Q: heat energy (joules, J), m: mass that changes state (kilograms, kg), L: specific latent heat (J/kg)
- L is for melting (fusion) or boiling (vaporisation); boiling needs much more than melting

## Picture it
Draw `beaker` over `burner`, arrow from `burner.flame-tip` to `beaker.bottom` labelled "heat Q in".
Draw `thermometer` by `beaker.liquid-surface`, with `thermometer.level` labelled "stays at 0 °C while melting".

## Common mistakes
- Using Q = m·c·ΔT during melting: the temperature does not change, so use Q = m·L.
- Forgetting the energy: melting still takes a lot of heat.
- Mixing up L with c: c is per degree, L is for the whole change of state.

## Check questions
1. How much heat melts 2 kg of ice at 0 °C? (L = 3.3 × 10⁵ J/kg)
   Answer: 2 × 3.3 × 10⁵ = 6.6 × 10⁵ J.
2. Heat of 1.0 × 10⁵ J melts a block with L = 2.0 × 10⁵ J/kg. What is its mass?
   Answer: m = Q / L = 0.5 kg.
3. Why does ice water stay at 0 °C while the ice melts?
   Answer: The heat goes into breaking particle bonds, not into speeding particles up.

## Flashcards
Q: What is latent heat?
A: Energy to change state without changing temperature.
Q: Formula for latent heat?
A: Q = m·L.
Q: Unit of specific latent heat?
A: J/kg.
Q: What happens to the temperature during melting?
A: It stays the same.
Q: Which needs more energy: melting or boiling the same mass?
A: Boiling.
