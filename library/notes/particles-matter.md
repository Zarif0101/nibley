# Particles and states of matter

## Simple idea
Everything is made of tiny particles that are always moving. In a solid they are packed tight and just jiggle in place. In a liquid they are close but slide past each other. In a gas they are far apart and zoom around. Think of a crowded lift, a busy hallway, and an empty field.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking particles in a solid are still: they vibrate in place.
- Thinking particles themselves grow when heated: they move faster and spread out.
- Thinking there is air or something between gas particles: it is empty space.

## Check questions
1. Name the three states of matter.
   Answer: Solid, liquid and gas.
2. Which state has particles that are far apart and move freely?
   Answer: Gas.
3. Why can you pour a liquid but not a solid?
   Answer: Liquid particles can slide past each other; solid particles are locked in place.

## Flashcards
Q: How do particles in a solid move?
A: They vibrate in fixed places.
Q: How do particles in a liquid move?
A: They are close together and slide past each other.
Q: How are gas particles arranged?
A: Far apart, moving fast in all directions.
Q: What happens to particles when a substance is heated?
A: They move faster.
Q: What are all substances made of?
A: Tiny particles.
