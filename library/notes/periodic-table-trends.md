# Periodic table trends

## Simple idea
Atom size, how hard it is to pull an electron off, and how strongly it grabs electrons all change in steady patterns. Across a row, more protons pull the same shell tighter, so atoms shrink. Down a column, new shells are added, so atoms grow and hold their outer electrons less tightly.

## Key formulas
- Across a period (left to right): atomic radius decreases
- Across a period: ionisation energy (energy to remove an electron, kJ/mol) and electronegativity (pull on shared electrons) increase
- Down a group: atomic radius increases
- Down a group: ionisation energy and electronegativity decrease

## Common mistakes
- Thinking atoms get bigger across a period: more protons pull harder, so they get smaller.
- Forgetting extra shells down a group: more shells means a bigger atom.
- Calling sodium more electronegative than chlorine: electronegativity rises to the right, so chlorine is higher.

## Check questions
1. Which is bigger, a sodium atom or a chlorine atom?
   Answer: Sodium.
2. Which needs more energy to lose an electron, lithium or potassium?
   Answer: Lithium.
3. Why does ionisation energy fall going down a group?
   Answer: The outer electron is further away and more shielded, so it is easier to remove.

## Flashcards
Q: What happens to atomic radius across a period?
A: It decreases.
Q: What happens to atomic radius down a group?
A: It increases.
Q: What is ionisation energy?
A: The energy needed to remove an electron from an atom.
Q: What is electronegativity?
A: How strongly an atom pulls on shared electrons.
Q: Which element is the most electronegative?
A: Fluorine.
