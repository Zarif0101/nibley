# Electric charge and Coulomb's law

## Simple idea
Rub a balloon on your hair and it sticks to the wall. That is electric charge. Charges are positive or negative. Same signs push apart, different signs pull together. The closer they are, the stronger the push or pull. Double the distance and the force drops to a quarter.

## Key formulas
- F = k·q₁·q₂ / r². F: force (newtons, N), q₁ and q₂: charges (coulombs, C), r: distance between them (metres, m), k ≈ 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
- e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. e: charge of one proton (an electron has −e)

## Picture it
Draw `charge-positive` on the left and `charge-negative` on the right. Arrow from `charge-positive.right` pointing right and arrow from `charge-negative.top` pointing left, both labelled "attract".
Label the gap between them "distance r".

## Common mistakes
- Using r instead of r²: square the distance before dividing.
- Forgetting units: charges in microcoulombs must become coulombs (1 μC = 10⁻⁶ C).
- Thinking the bigger charge pushes harder: both charges feel the same size force.

## Check questions
1. Two 1 μC charges sit 0.1 m apart. What is the force between them?
   Answer: F = 9.0 × 10⁹ × 10⁻⁶ × 10⁻⁶ / 0.01 = 0.9 N.
2. The distance between two charges doubles. What happens to the force?
   Answer: It becomes one quarter.
3. Why do a positive and a negative charge move toward each other?
   Answer: Unlike charges attract.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the unit of charge?
A: The coulomb (C).
Q: What do like charges do?
A: They repel each other.
Q: What is the charge of one proton?
A: +1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.
Q: How does Coulomb force change if r is tripled?
A: It becomes one ninth.
Q: What is the approximate value of k?
A: 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C².
