# Doppler effect

## Simple idea
An ambulance siren sounds high as it comes towards you and drops lower after it passes. Moving towards you, the source squeezes the sound waves closer together, so more reach your ear each second. Moving away stretches them out, so fewer reach you.

## Key formulas
- Towards you: f' = f·v / (v − vₛ)
- Away from you: f' = f·v / (v + vₛ)
- f: frequency the source makes (Hz), f': frequency you hear (Hz), v: speed of sound (about 340 m/s), vₛ: speed of the source (m/s). You stand still.

## Picture it
Draw `wavefronts`. Arrow along `wavefronts.direction` labelled "source moves this way".
Label `wavefronts.bunched-side` "waves bunched: higher pitch" and `wavefronts.spread-side` "waves spread: lower pitch".

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the source's real pitch changes: only what you hear changes.
- Mixing which side is higher: ahead of the source is higher, behind is lower.
- Thinking it needs the source close: it depends on speed, not distance.

## Check questions
1. A siren moves away from you. Is the pitch you hear higher or lower?
   Answer: Lower.
2. A 400 Hz source moves towards you at 34 m/s. What do you hear? (v = 340 m/s)
   Answer: 400 × 340 / 306 ≈ 444 Hz.
3. Why do the waves bunch up in front of a moving source?
   Answer: The source follows its own waves, so each new wave starts closer to the last.

## Flashcards
Q: What is the Doppler effect?
A: The pitch you hear changes when the source moves.
Q: Source coming towards you: pitch heard?
A: Higher than the real pitch.
Q: Source moving away: pitch heard?
A: Lower than the real pitch.
Q: Does the pitch the source makes change?
A: No, only the pitch you hear.
Q: Why is the pitch higher when it comes towards you?
A: More wave crests reach your ear each second.

## Root check
- sound-waves: Does a higher pitch mean a higher or lower frequency? Answer: Higher.
