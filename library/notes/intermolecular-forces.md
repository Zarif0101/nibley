# Intermolecular forces

## Simple idea
Molecules attract each other with weak pulls, much weaker than the bonds inside them. Think of Velcro between people holding hands: the hands are strong, the Velcro is weak. Stronger pulls mean a higher boiling point, because more energy is needed to pull molecules apart.

## Key formulas
- London forces: in all molecules, stronger in bigger molecules
- Dipole-dipole: between molecules with a slight + end and a slight − end
- Hydrogen bond: H bonded to N, O or F, attracted to a lone pair on another N, O or F
- Strength order: London < dipole-dipole < hydrogen bond

## Common mistakes
- Thinking boiling water breaks O–H bonds: it only breaks the pulls between molecules.
- Calling a hydrogen bond a normal covalent bond: it is a weak pull between molecules.
- Saying any molecule with H has hydrogen bonds: H must be on N, O or F.

## Check questions
1. Which three elements can make hydrogen bonds when bonded to H?
   Answer: Nitrogen, oxygen and fluorine.
2. Which has the higher boiling point, H₂O or H₂S?
   Answer: H₂O, because it has hydrogen bonds.
3. Why does water boil at 100 °C when many similar small molecules boil far lower?
   Answer: Its hydrogen bonds are strong, so much energy is needed to separate molecules.

## Flashcards
Q: What are intermolecular forces?
A: Weak attractions between molecules.
Q: What is the weakest type of intermolecular force?
A: London forces.
Q: What does a hydrogen bond need?
A: H bonded to N, O or F.
Q: What happens to boiling point when forces get stronger?
A: It goes up.
Q: What rule predicts what dissolves in what?
A: Like dissolves like.
