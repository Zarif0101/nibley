# Equilibrium

## Simple idea
Something is in equilibrium when it is perfectly balanced: it does not speed up, and it does not start to spin. Think of a seesaw level in the air. The pushes up and down cancel, and the turning effects on each side cancel too. Both checks must pass.

## Key formulas
- ΣF = 0. ΣF: sum of all forces (N), up cancels down and left cancels right
- Στ = 0. Στ: sum of torques about any point (N·m)
- F₁·d₁ = F₂·d₂. F: forces (N), d: distances from the pivot (m)

## Picture it
Draw `seesaw` level. Arrow down at `seesaw.left-end`, labelled "weight 1". Arrow down at `seesaw.right-end`, labelled "weight 2". Arrow up at `seesaw.pivot`, labelled "support force".
Or draw `scale-balance` with equal arrows down on `scale-balance.left-pan` and `scale-balance.right-pan`.

## Common mistakes
- Checking only forces: also check that torques cancel.
- Forgetting the support force at the pivot: it carries the total weight.
- Measuring distances from different points: pick one pivot for all.

## Check questions
1. What two conditions give equilibrium?
   Answer: Forces cancel, and torques cancel.
2. A 300 N child sits 2 m left of the pivot. Where does a 200 N child sit to balance?
   Answer: 300 × 2 = 200 × d, so d = 3 m on the right.
3. Why can a beam have zero net force but still spin?
   Answer: The forces can cancel while their torques do not.

## Flashcards
Q: What is equilibrium?
A: No change in motion and no change in spin.
Q: Write the force condition for equilibrium.
A: ΣF = 0, the net force is zero.
Q: Write the torque condition for equilibrium.
A: Στ = 0, clockwise torque equals anticlockwise torque.
Q: A seesaw balances. What does the pivot push with?
A: An upward force equal to the total weight.
Q: Does equilibrium mean the object must be at rest?
A: No. It can move at steady speed in a line.

## Root check
- torque: A 10 N force acts 0.3 m from a pivot at right angles. What is the torque? Answer: 3 N·m.
- free-body-diagrams: A book rests on a table. Name the two forces on it. Answer: Its weight down and the table's push (normal force) up.
