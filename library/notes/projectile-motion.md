# Projectile motion

## Simple idea
Throw a ball across a field. It moves forward at a steady pace while gravity pulls it down at the same time. The two motions do not affect each other. A ball rolled off a table and one dropped from the table edge hit the floor together.

## Key formulas
- vₓ = v·cos θ. vₓ: horizontal speed (m/s), v: launch speed (m/s), θ: launch angle above horizontal
- v_y = v·sin θ. v_y: starting upward speed (m/s)
- x = vₓ·t. x: horizontal distance (m), t: time in the air (s). No acceleration sideways
- Vertical: use free fall with a = 9.8 m/s² down

## Common mistakes
- Thinking sideways speed drops in flight: without air it stays constant.
- Mixing sin and cos: horizontal is cos θ, vertical is sin θ (angle from the ground).
- Mixing sideways and up-down numbers in one equation: keep them separate.

## Check questions
1. A ball flies sideways at 12 m/s for 3 s. How far does it go sideways?
   Answer: 36 m.
2. What is the starting upward speed of a 20 m/s throw at 30°?
   Answer: 20 × sin 30° = 10 m/s.
3. Why does the ball keep its sideways speed while falling?
   Answer: Gravity pulls only downward, so nothing changes the sideways motion.

## Flashcards
Q: What acceleration does a projectile have sideways?
A: Zero (ignoring air).
Q: What acceleration does a projectile have vertically?
A: 9.8 m/s² downward.
Q: What shape is the path?
A: A parabola.
Q: Horizontal part of launch speed v at angle θ?
A: v·cos θ.
Q: What is the upward speed at the very top?
A: Zero.

## Root check
- free-fall: A ball is dropped from rest. What is its speed after 1 s? Answer: 9.8 m/s.
- vectors-basics: What two things describe a vector? Answer: Its size and its direction.
- trig-ratios: In a right triangle, what is sin θ? Answer: Opposite side divided by hypotenuse.
